# Changelog

This file says what each publication of the source brings. The public repository holds one commit per publication: the development history is not published, and each publication replaces the whole tree with the files named in `public-manifest.json`. A section headed by a date lists what the commit of that date brings, newest first. How a publication is made: `docs/publishing.md`.

Two marks are used:

- **Stored format**: something written to disk or to a browser's storage changes (a vault record, a file a person keeps, a service's store). Read the entry before you update a wallet page or a service that holds data.
- **Rule change**: a rule that people or programs rely on changes (what a setting accepts, what a person must keep, how a program must be run).

"Not yet run on real devices" means that it ran in tests and in Chrome with its virtual authenticator only: no phone, no Windows Hello or Touch ID, no Safari or Firefox.

## 25 November 2026: recovery words on paper, alerts, the fixes of the project's own web audit, no key under a chosen password, and moving an account to another device

### Account security

- **Recovery is five words written on paper.** At a protected sign-up and in "Protect my account" the wallet shows five recovery words to write down by hand. Nothing is downloaded, printed or asked back, and both recovery keys of the account come from the words ("words only"; `web-v4/wallet/papers.mjs`, `security-flows.mjs`). The chain's rule is unchanged: two recovery keys, both needed for a big change. **Rule change:** new recovery words are five words (90 bits) where they were four (72 bits). The four words of accounts made before are still read wherever a person types words.
- **The Recovery Kit, an option.** Under Keys, "New recovery papers" is now "New recovery words", with "Words only" or "Words and a Recovery Kit". With a kit, the second recovery key is a random recovery sheet, kept in a file locked with the words (AES-256-GCM under Argon2id of the words, with a salt of its own) or printed, and the paper alone is not enough to recover. **Stored format:** the Recovery Kit is a new file; `web-v4/wallet/RECOVERY_FILE_FORMAT.md` gives its format and a test vector.
- **"Recover my account"** asks for the name, the recovery words and new passwords, and for the kit or the printed sheet only where the account has one. Every other form that needs one recovery key has one field, "Recovery words", which also takes a printed sheet. The page says "recovery words" where it said "recovery passphrase".
- **What a taken paper means** (web audit, K-1; the findings are named as in `AUDIT-2026-11.md`). With words only, whoever takes the only copy of the paper can ask to take over the account. That waits the account's waiting period, and only the words stop it. The step now says so and asks for two copies kept in two places; the alert of a waiting replacement leads first to "New recovery words"; an account whose recovery is one paper and that holds more than an hour of seconds gets one line under Keys that suggests a Recovery Kit. The text before ("you can cancel it") was wrong for an owner whose only copy was taken.
- **No key under a password a person chose, alone** (`web-v4/wallet/vault.mjs`, `RULE` and `sealVault`; `web-v4/wallet/README.md`, "No key under a password you chose alone"). Where the device can lock keys, the person who makes new keys (at sign-up, in "Protect my account", in "Recover my account" and in "Move my account here") chooses between two ways, neither chosen beforehand: "Lock them to this device", with passwords of their own, or "Let the wallet make the passwords". The form is not sent before a way is chosen, nor before the browser has said what the device can do (`web-v4/wallet/device-lock-ui.mjs`, `wayChoice`). Where the device cannot, or fails after the lock was chosen, the wallet makes both passwords (four words joined by dashes, 72 bits) and shows them once, in a dialog that goes on only with "I have them", before anything they seal is kept or sent. A new login or payment password and a login asked for a second browser are made by the wallet too, unless the keys are locked to the device. Keys sealed before under chosen passwords keep opening; Keys names them and offers "Lock my keys to this device" and "Let the wallet make my passwords". `web-v4/wallet/sealing-rule.test.mjs` fails a module that seals a key any other way. Not yet run on real devices.
- **Stored format: vault records of versions 3 and 4, and the mark of a made password** (`web-v4/wallet/BACKUP_FORMAT.md`). Version 3 is a key locked to the device. A record sealed under a password the wallet made carries `"password": "made"` in its authenticated header (versions 1, 2 and 3). Version 4 keeps the registration key of a kept sign-up under the new login key, no longer under the login password. **A wallet page from before this publication refuses records marked as sealed under a made password and every record of version 4, and cannot restore a backup file made since.** Do not serve an older wallet page to browsers that used this one: their keys would not open there (the records stay, and a page of this version opens them again).
- **Backup files** are made only of a key whose password the wallet made, never of a key locked to the device. A file made before, under a chosen password, still restores: its key is then kept under a password the wallet makes, shown first, and Home suggests a new file.
- **"Lock my keys to this device"**, under Keys (`web-v4/wallet/device-lock.mjs`). The login key and the payment key open only with the device's secret (WebAuthn PRF, after the device checked the person) and the password together, so a copy of the browser's data opens nothing elsewhere. Turning it off seals the keys again under passwords the wallet makes. An account with one key (a network without protected registration) cannot be locked. Not yet run on real devices.
- **"Move my account to another device"** (`web-v4/wallet/device-move.mjs`; README, "Move my account to another device"). The new device ("Move my account here" on the start screen) makes its keys by the rule above and shows a request and a code of 16 characters. The old device, under Keys, checks the request, takes the typed code, the payment password and the recovery words, and sends one replacement of the account's keys, in force at once; it forgets its keys once the network shows the move. No key, password or device secret leaves a device. The code proves which request it is, not who made it: a person talked into typing a code someone sent gives that person the account. The engine would take a move over a freeze or a waiting replacement; only the wallet refuses it. Not yet run on real devices.
- **A locked wallet's storage no longer shows what the account did** (`web-v4/wallet/locked-store.mjs`). The activity, the names paid and the marks of the orders paid are encrypted under a data key that exists only while the wallet is unlocked. **Stored format:** they move to new places (`timecurrency.activity.v2:`, `timecurrency.names.v2:`, `timecurrency.orders.v2:`, with `timecurrency.store-key.v1:`); the plain records of the earlier version are encrypted at the first unlock and removed once the encrypted copy is written. That move ran in unit tests only. The account's name and public keys stay readable in the storage.
- **Secret fields.** The fields of the payment password, the recovery words and a printed sheet are marked for password managers to leave alone, and each field of recovery words says not to let the browser save them. In Chrome 158 the browser offered to save the login password only. Firefox, Safari and phones have not been tried.

### Wallet

- **Alerts** at the top of Home (`web-v4/wallet/alerts.mjs`, `alerts-ui.mjs`): what needs attention, worked out from the state the wallet verified (keys being replaced, guardians recovering, a freeze, a payment or a raise of the limit waiting, actions signed with the account's keys that this browser did not send), a banner for what is urgent, and "I have seen these". While the page is open it reads at once when the API's hint names the account. A switch turns on alerts for a closed wallet (a browser push from the alert service, below), and asks the service once a minute whether it still has this browser; the service worker (`sw.js`) shows one fixed sentence, "Open your wallet to check your account." No push has reached a real browser yet.
- **On a phone's Home Screen**: a web app manifest and icons (`manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/`). Not tried on a phone.
- **A busy network** (`web-v4/wallet/busy.mjs`; README, "A busy network"). A refusal for load (429 or 503) no longer stops the wallet: reads wait and ask again, the header says that the network is busy, signing stays on, a signed action is sent again as the same bytes, and an action that protects the account (a freeze, a cancellation, new recovery words) is sent again for two minutes and then shown in an urgent notice with "Try again". Before, one such refusal turned signing off, freeze included. Run on the preview server and in tests only.
- **Fixes of the web audit, on the page** (`web-v4/wallet/web-audit.test.mjs` holds a test for each): a website's request is checked in full before the approval window shows anything, and a request in no known form gets one fixed sentence (W-1); text from a node, or from errors that are not the wallet's, is shown in fixed words (W-2); two paid names that look alike are named in the review (W-4); a waiting payment is named from a proof and reviewed like any payment (W-5); "Register my validator" names the bond (W-7); the payment password field is emptied on Decline and on each request (W-8); `cl` is read as `d` and `nn` as `m` when names are compared (F-3; the chain's own rule can change only at a new genesis); passwords that guessing programs try first are refused where a password is chosen (K-6). No step of the browser check tries what the findings describe.
- **A page that could not load** now says "The wallet could not load. Check your connection and reload this page. Nothing was changed." with "Reload" (`boot-check.js`).
- **"Check this device"** (`device-check.html`, `device-key.mjs`): a trial page beside the wallet that tells whether the device's own fingerprint, face or screen lock can lock a value (WebAuthn with PRF), and ends with one of eight plain outcomes. It holds nothing, and it works only under a name, not under a numeric address. Run only with Chrome's virtual authenticators.

### Services (API, faucet, gateway, alert service)

- **New: the alert service** (`client-v4/alert-push.mjs`, `deploy/alerts/README.md`). When a verified block holds an action that changes an account's keys or security rules, or starts a wait on it, every browser subscribed to that account gets a push whose content is the constant `{"v":1}`. A payment wakes nobody (`client-v4/touches.mjs` lists which actions wake whom). A browser subscribes with a proof by the account's login key; an account has at most eight, and a ninth is refused rather than pushing one out. No push is dropped for want of a place, accounts are served in turn, and every browser of an account is woken before any of them is removed. The service keeps the list of subscribed browsers, its own key for the push services and a checkpoint, nothing else. The push services see when a browser is woken, which over a few events can tie a person to an account number.
- **API: hints for wallets** (`deploy/api/README.md`, "Hints for wallets"). `GET /v1/touches?account=N` sends one event for each new block that names the account, with its height and nothing else; nothing is kept or sent again. New settings `max_touch_streams` and `max_touch_streams_per_client`.
- **API: places shared fairly** (web audit, A-1 and A-4; "Proof reads"). A client has at most two proof reads under way (`max_proof_reads_per_client`); a third gets 429 `too_many_reads`. Reads wait in a fair line, and get 503 `busy` when it is full. Identical reads within a second share one proof, now also `/v1/accounts/{number or name}`. A share of the stream places (`unfiltered_stream_percent`, a fifth by default) is kept for readers that follow no account, such as the explorer.
- **API: no loads from other sites.** An image, a script, a frame or a similar load of an address under `/v1/` from a page of another site gets 403 `cross_site_refused`. A request with a body ends its connection with its answer, and one still arriving after 15 seconds is answered 408. Two reads that cross a block boundary no longer give a 503 when nothing is wrong.
- **Rule change: webhook secrets** (the API and the alert watch: `deploy/api/README.md`, "Webhooks"; `deploy/watch/README.md`). A secret must be 32 random bytes or more, written as 64 to 256 hexadecimal characters made by a generator (`openssl rand -hex 32`); any other secret is refused at start. Before this publication any 16 characters were taken: replace such a secret before you update. Receivers need no change.
- **Faucet: who asked is not kept** (`client-v4/faucet.mjs`; `deploy/faucet/README.md`, "Who asked, and for how long it is kept"). A caller is kept as a keyed hash for 24 hours; the key of those hashes is no longer in the store (it is held in memory, and in the optional `caller_key_file` on a file system in memory), and no caller address is logged. **Stored format:** the faucet's store is version 5. Stores of versions 3 and 4 are moved in place at the first start; older ones are refused. A backup taken before the move still holds what the store held (for version 4, the key beside the hashes): delete such copies.
- **Faucet: limits that count networks** (web audit, F-2 and F-4; "How the limits count"). An IPv6 /48 counts as one network (`limits.per_network_per_day`, `limits.registrations_per_network_per_day`); sponsored sign-ups have a total of their own (`limits.registrations_per_day`); an account whose registration the faucet sponsored gets one grant in its first 24 hours. A sponsorship that the node refuses for good is withdrawn at once, instead of holding up every grant. A body must arrive within 5 seconds (408 `body_too_slow`). `GET /v1/faucet` reports the new limits, and a page of another origin may read `Retry-After`. Someone with many addresses can still take every grant and sign-up of a day.
- **Gateway: limits per caller** (web audit, G-1 and D-4; `deploy/gateway/README.md`, "Limits, and who the caller is"). The page's own files no longer count against the limit of requests that ask the node. With the new setting `trusted_proxies` the gateway counts each caller apart (an IPv4 address or an IPv6 /64, and its /48), and its checks wait in a fair line. A refusal that may be asked again now carries a `code` (`RATE_LIMITED` with 429, or `GATEWAY_BUSY` with 503, which is new) and a `Retry-After` of 1 to 30 seconds. Set `trusted_proxies` only if your reverse proxy writes `X-Forwarded-For` itself.
- **Gateway: other fixes of the web audit.** Sign-in challenges travel in their cookie under a MAC instead of filling a table (G-2); an account has at most three open orders, and an unpaid order is deleted a day after it expired (G-3); the demo blog takes 60 posts an hour from everyone together and keeps a post a week (G-4); no answer carries a file path, an address of the machine or of a node, or a node's words (G-5, with `client-v4/own-node.mjs`); only the files of a list made at start are served, by their exact names (G-6); signing out does not read the chain (G-7); the wallet's pages send `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy`, except `/approve`, and a `Permissions-Policy` (W-3). `client-v4/nginx.example.conf` limits each client, sets `X-Forwarded-For` and passes `/alerts/` on (D-11).
- **SDK and verifier.** The SDK tells a 429 or a 503 by its status and waits (`BUSY` in `client-v4/time-sdk.js`), where it failed to read the answer; the proof verifier's errors show only its own sentences, other errors fixed words (`client-v4/proof-verifier.mjs`). `client-v4/api/openapi.json` describes the new route and errors.

### Chain and node

- No change to the engine's code (`v5/src/`), to its rules or to the genesis format.
- `v5/tests/security.rs`: the engine takes the replacement of keys that a move to another device builds; this test and the wallet's (`client-v4/device-move.fixture.json`) pin the same bytes. It also shows that the engine would take such a move over a freeze or a waiting replacement.
- **Rule change for operators:** the deployment kit's `deploy/compose.v5.yaml` runs the remote signer as user `10001:10001`, whatever its image says. A signer image of your own must give that user its state directory.

### Websites

- Main page (`web-v4/site/index.html`): "Your keys in one minute" speaks of five recovery words on paper, of the Recovery Kit as an option, and says that only the words stop someone who took the paper.
- Explorer (`web-v4/explorer/`): every verdict of a check names, in one line, the network id it was checked against and where that id came from (web audit, A-5).
- Faucet page (`web-v4/faucet/`): says what a new account gets in its first 24 hours; `/?account=<number or name>` fills in the field and asks for nothing by itself.
- Demo blog (`web-v4/merchant/`): a post is drawn inside its own box, and a post with a letter of more than 4 combining marks, or with more than 16 characters that take no room, is refused (G-8).

- `tools/release-v4.mjs`: the gateway's distribution lists the new files (the wallet's new modules, the page "Check this device", the alert service, `client-v4/callers.mjs`, `client-v4/touches.mjs`), so the identity of that distribution changes; the native release identity does not.
- `tools/wallet-icons.mjs` (new) draws the wallet's Home Screen icons from its clock mark, with Node alone.
- Tests made from the web audit's proof scripts: `web-v4/wallet/web-audit.test.mjs`, `client-v4/gateway-audit.test.mjs`, `client-v4/alert-push-audit.test.mjs`. `web-v4/wallet/password-fields.test.mjs` refuses `XMLHttpRequest` in the wallet's scripts (Chrome took a form as sent after one).
- The wallet's preview server (`web-v4/wallet/tests/preview-server.mjs`) has switches for protected sign-ups, recovery papers, alerts and busy answers (`/__protect`, `/__papers`, `/__alerts`, `/__busy`). The manual tools `tests/preview-drive.mjs` and `tests/security-ui-smoke.mjs` were not brought to the dialog of made passwords, and are not among the repository's checks.
