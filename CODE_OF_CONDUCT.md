# Code of conduct

This applies to everyone who takes part in the project: in issues, pull requests, reviews and any other place the project runs.

## What we expect

- Talk about the work, not about the person. "This check can be bypassed like this" helps. "You clearly don't understand consensus" does not.
- Assume the other person means well, and ask before you conclude that they do not.
- Say what you know, what you measured and what you only suppose. This project handles money; a confident guess can cost someone else.
- Accept review. Finding a flaw in someone's change is a service to them. Give it and take it that way.
- Be patient with newcomers and with people writing in a second language.
- Keep private things private: other people's personal details, keys, and security reports that have not been published.

## What we do not accept

- Insults, harassment, threats, or attacks on anyone for who they are.
- Sexual remarks or unwelcome attention.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Pushing a token, an investment or a price in project spaces. The project gives no financial advice and promises no value.
- Posing as the project, as a maintainer or as another contributor.
- Disrupting a discussion again and again after being asked to stop.

## If something goes wrong

Tell the maintainer privately, through the repository's private vulnerability reporting form (on GitHub: the Security tab, then Report a vulnerability), with "Conduct" at the start of the title. Say what happened, where, and who was involved; add links if you have them. Your report is kept confidential.

The maintainer may edit or remove a contribution, ask for a correction, warn someone, or exclude them from the project for a time or for good. The response depends on how serious the matter is and on whether it has happened before.

Security problems in the software do not belong here. Report them as `SECURITY.md` says.
