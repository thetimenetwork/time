# V4 browser wallet

See [the V4 rehearsal guide](../README.md) and [the SDK trust and integration contract](../../client-v4/README.md).

This surface uses real Bitcoin regtest receipts, dynamic PoS, fresh selected JMT proofs, fourteen-day stake exposure and explicit seven-day checkpoint refresh. It remains a private test network.

## The screens

The page is made for people who are not technical, on a phone or a laptop. A strip at the top of every screen says that this is the Time test network, that its money is test money with no value, and that the network may be reset. The unit is the second.

- **Start.** At a wallet address paired with Time Vault, **Create account** proposes an editable public name and opens the password manager. You choose one master password, confirm your device, and keep a paper copy of your recovery words. **Set up manually** opens the original wallet setup. Under **Other ways in**, you can add this browser to an account, restore a backup file, or recover with words or guardians.
- **Five places.** Home, Payments, Activity, Keys and More, always labelled: at the bottom of the screen on a phone or a tablet, in a column on the left of a screen 1024 px wide or more.
- **Home.** At the top, before anything else, the panel "Alerts": what needs the owner's attention, with a banner for what is urgent, live while the wallet is open, and the switch for alerts while it is closed (see "Alerts: what needs your attention"). Then the `@name` with its initial and "Copy", the balance labelled "Balance", in seconds, and three round actions: Send, Receive, Get test time. Under them one line "Staked" (what the account has lent to validators, which is not in the balance) that opens Staking. The line "Checked by this browser against the network at block N" is the wallet's own verification of the account proof (before: "Account proof verified"). The card "Protection": for a protected account its freeze, its daily limit and its pause, and under it what is waiting on the account; for any other account "Not protected yet" with one button (see "Protection" below). The newest three entries of this browser's activity. Until a backup file was downloaded (or this key came from one), an account with one key sees a line that suggests it.
- **Payments: Send.** Send and Receive are its two sides. Name, amount (large), the fee; under the name, the names this account has paid from this browser, which a tap puts in the field (the same names the review compares a new name with, `names.mjs`). The review says From, To, Amount, Fee, Total (in bold), Network and When ("In the next block, within seconds. Once it is in a block, it cannot be undone."), and its button says what it does ("Send 1.5 seconds"); then "Sent. @bob has the 1.5 seconds now and needs to do nothing. This browser checked the network's receipt." in the notice and under the form. A protected account is asked for its payment password, as before.
- **Payments: Receive.** The name to give, and the account number.
- **Activity.** What this browser signed or checked, newest first, with the pending action and its buttons, and a search that hides the lines without the words typed (it reads the page only).
- **Keys** (`actions.mjs`, `keysView`, drawn by `app.js`, `renderKeys`). Each thing that opens the account, as a card: what it does, where it is, when it is needed, what to do if it is lost, marked in words "Every day", "When you pay" or "Keep safe".
  - An account with one key: the name and the password every day, the backup file to keep safe, and the form that downloads it (only of a key whose password the wallet made). Keys under passwords people chose before the rule get the card "Passwords you chose", with "Let the wallet make my passwords".
  - "Protect my account": first what the person gets (login password, payment password, five recovery words on paper), then the existing steps of `security-ui.mjs` in plainer words.
  - A protected account: its state and its limits, its cards (login password, payment password, recovery words, the Recovery Kit of an account that has one, this browser), "Print this page" (the cards only, black on white; no secret is on them), the backup file (which holds the login key only), and under "Change something" a new login password, new recovery words (words only, or words and a Recovery Kit) and a new payment password. The freeze, the end of a freeze, the daily limit and what is waiting were here; they are on Home now, and Keys says so.
- **More.** The account's initial and name, then Staking (with the Bitcoin regtest auction card only on a network that has that auction), Devices, Guardian recovery, Validator signal (only for the owner of a validator), Use in your app, Validator operator. "Offline trust and checkpoint refresh" stays at the foot of every screen, closed, because it must be reachable while the wallet is locked.
- **Market** (under More, and a line on Home; only on a network whose proven state has the `market` setting). Buy seconds with bitcoin, sell seconds for bitcoin, "My purchases and orders", "My Bitcoin addresses", and "For traders" with the order book, recorded trades, a chart, every order form and stop orders kept by the wallet. See "The market: seconds against bitcoin".
- **A website's window** (`/approve`). The website's address, large; what it asks ("Sign in only", or "Pay exactly 0.1 seconds to account 7, once"); Approve and Decline. The rest of the wallet is hidden there. A request is taken only in its forms (`guards.mjs`, `acceptApprovalEvent`): a payment with an account number, an amount, a network id of 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters and an order reference of the same form, as the merchant SDK makes them, and the demo blog's fee, description and expiry, which are never shown; a sign-in exactly as a website's server issues its challenge, for that website. Anything else, a standing permission included, leaves nothing to approve and one sentence, in the window and in the notice: "This website sent a request the wallet does not understand. Nothing was shown or signed." Nothing the website sent is ever put into the wallet's own words. The payment password field is emptied on Decline and whenever a request arrives. The review and every check of the request are as before.
- **A page that never starts.** When a script or the stylesheet of the wallet does not load, or the page has not started 20 seconds after its document was read, the page shows only "The wallet could not load. Check your connection and reload this page. Nothing was changed." and a button "Reload" (`boot-check.js`, the first script of the page; the page's policy allows no inline script, so it is a file of its own, and app.js tells it when the page has started). Before, such a page showed its form and never answered.

**How it looks** (the same for every page of `web-v4`; the tokens are the first `:root` block of `styles.css`, copied as they are into each page's stylesheet). A white canvas, near-black ink (#222), pale gray surfaces (#F1F1F1), secondary text #666, #DDD only for separators and #767676 where a line marks a control; the dark palette has the same roles and was checked on its own (text at least 4.5:1, the edges of controls at least 3:1). No colour says anything alone: green, red and amber come with a word or a sign. Sections are flat and grouped by space and headings; only the Protection card, what waits on the account, the Alerts panel (a frame, with a red line before each urgent alert and a pale red banner above it) and a website's request sit on a surface or in a frame. Numbers are tabular and bold where they matter, with their unit; names, hashes and keys are in a monospace font. Buttons are pills 52 px tall, fields 56 px, rows 64 px, every target at least 44 px. Signs are line drawings used as masks over the text colour (`data:` images, allowed by the page's `img-src`); the drawings of people are inline SVG in the same colours, only on Receive and an empty Activity, never over a number, a name or a review. Movement is brief (180 ms, the review's sheet 260 ms) and stops when the system asks for less motion. Nothing is loaded from elsewhere: system fonts, no library, and the page's Content-Security-Policy (`style-src 'self'`, no inline script or style) is unchanged but for `manifest-src 'self'`, for its web app manifest (see "On a phone's Home Screen").

**Looking at the screens without a network.** `tests/preview-server.mjs` serves these files on 127.0.0.1 with the gateway's Content-Security-Policy, in front of a mock chain and a mock faucet with a sample website; `tests/preview-drive.mjs` drives a headless Chrome with a new profile through every screen at 390 and 1280 px, or the widths given with `--widths` (on 2026-11-22 also 320 and 768 px, and 390 px with `--theme dark`) (screenshots and a log) and walks the paths of the browser check that the mock can carry; `--only alerts` takes the Alerts panel and its switch through their states (the preview also serves the hint `/v1/touches` and, with `/__alerts?mode=offered`, a stand-in alert service). The mock's state is taken as proven by a stand-in verifier and it applies transfers only: it shows the pages and their wiring, never that a check of the wallet works.

## Get test time

When the wallet's service names a registration service (`registrationService` in `/api/network`: the faucet of a test network), Home offers "Get test time". Without one the button is not there. One press (`app.js`, `getTestTime`; `actions.mjs`, `askFaucet`):

1. reads the faucet's facts (`GET <origin>/v1/faucet`). A faucet that answers for another network, says it is not giving, or does not answer is asked nothing;
2. asks for one grant for this account: `POST <origin>/v1/faucet/grants` with `{"account": "<number>"}`, by its number (a name of digits only would be read as a number by the faucet). Nothing else is sent: no cookie, no referrer, no name;
3. waits for the receipt of the hash the faucet answered, verifies it like any receipt, and checks that it is a transfer to this account from the faucet's account (`faucetReceiptPays`). Only then does it say "You received 0.1 seconds of test time. This browser checked that it arrived." and record it in Activity. Otherwise it says that the faucet says it sent test time and that this browser has not seen it arrive yet.

The wallet waits up to fifteen seconds and reads up to 64 KiB for each faucet reply. Known refusal codes have wallet-written explanations. For an unrecognized refusal, the wallet directs the user to check their balance before asking again. The faucet must list the wallet's origin in its `cors_origins` for both grants and registration.

What a faucet can make the wallet show: one of its fixed sentences, or its own words marked as such. It cannot make the wallet say that time arrived: that is said only after the wallet's own check of the receipt.

## Creating an account: who sponsors the registration

A new account needs a sponsor: an existing account that pays the registration charge and signs for the new key. The wallet finds one in the answer of its own service (`/api/network`):

- `sponsorAccount`: the service sponsors by itself. Only the laboratory's launcher does that (`tools/start-v4-lab.mjs`), with a key of the laboratory.
- `registrationService`: an origin the wallet may ask, such as the faucet of a test network. An operator sets it in the gateway (`deploy/gateway/README.md`, "Where a new account is registered"). The wallet asks it which account sponsors and for which network, signs its consent for that account, and sends the new public key, the commitment of the name and the consent. The name itself is revealed later, by the wallet, on the chain.
- Neither: the wallet says that its service names no registration sponsor, before it makes a key or stores a vault.

In every case the key is made in this browser and the password never leaves it. The sponsor gets no power over the account.

- **The wallet takes a sponsor's word for nothing about the chain.** It waits for the receipt of the sponsor's action, checks that the receipt names its key and its commitment, and reads the commitment in proven state before it reveals the name. When the service answers that this very registration waits on the chain already, there is no receipt to wait for, and the wallet reads proven state for up to half a minute.
- **What a registration service is told.** The address the person comes from, the new public key and the commitment. Not the name. Once the wallet has revealed the name on the chain under that key, the service can put that address and that name together.
- **What the wallet shows.** The wallet explains signup limits and earlier registration attempts using messages it supplies. When the service gives an unfamiliar answer, the wallet directs the user to reopen their saved signup and check its status.
- **What it can hold up.** The wallet waits fifteen seconds for each answer and reads 64 KiB of it at most. A service that is down, slow or refusing stops the sign-up there: the key and its encrypted vault stay in this browser, and unlocking again goes on from what the chain shows.
- **A day's wait in one case.** The faucet sponsors one registration for a key in 24 hours. If it took the request and its action never reached a block, this browser's key for that name gets no second one from it that day.
- The consent, and the registration that waits for its name, are good for five minutes, or twenty seconds less than the network's longest if that is shorter.

A wallet that was installed for one network refuses a registration service that answers for another.

## Signing up protected

On a network whose genesis has `protected_registration` (read from every verified state, never from a gateway: `TimeSDK.protectedRegistration`), "Create an account" makes an account that is protected from its first block (`signup.mjs`, `signup-ui.mjs`, and `client.registerProtected` of the SDK, through the sponsor of "Creating an account" above). On any other network "Create an account" is as it was, and the script draws nothing of this.

**Signup with Time Vault.** Press **Create account** to keep the suggested name, or edit it first. Time Vault prompts for a master password, device verification and an offline copy of the five recovery words. The wallet generates distinct sign-in and payment passwords, uses the default daily spending limit of 600 seconds, and starts the network registration only after Time Vault confirms the encrypted save. Change the daily spending limit from the Protection card on Home.

The wallet stages encrypted private keys and a signed registration note before sending credentials to its paired vault. If the connection closes after the vault saves, opening the account through Time Vault resumes the same registration. An explicit cancellation before the save rolls back the staged records. If the browser closes during setup and the vault has no saved entry, start again with a fresh name. Keep the paper recovery copy somewhere you can reach if you lose this device or forget the master password.

**Manual signup.** Choose **Set up manually** to keep the passwords yourself. Other wallet addresses use this flow directly.

1. On the start screen: the name, and how the keys will be locked (the rule: see "No key under a password you chose alone" below). What the browser says it can do decides whether there is a choice (`device-lock.mjs`, `birthLockable`; the browser asks the person nothing for it):
   - *Where the device can lock keys:* the person chooses one of two ways, and neither is chosen beforehand (see "The person chooses" below; `device-lock-ui.mjs`, `wayChoice`). The form says "Pick a name and how to lock your keys. …" and shows no password field until a way is chosen. Sent with no way chosen, it goes no further, and says "First choose how to lock your new keys: to this device, or with passwords the wallet makes."; nothing is read, made, kept or sent, and the device is asked nothing. With "Lock them to this device" chosen: "Login password" and again, marked "Every day": "Opens this wallet and signs you in to websites. It cannot pay. Nobody can reset it."; "Payment password" and again, marked "When you pay": "Pays up to your daily limit. It must differ from your login password. Do not let the browser save it." (held to `checkPasswords`). With "Let the wallet make the passwords" chosen: no password field, as below. A way can be changed until the form is sent; the fields of the passwords go when the other way is chosen, and what was typed in them goes with them. Going to "Open my account" and back draws the form again, with neither way chosen.
   - *Where it cannot:* no choice: "The wallet makes your passwords and shows them to you, because this device cannot lock your keys.", and no password field.
   
   The wallet starts the daily spending limit at 600 seconds. Change it from the Protection card after signup.
2. The wallet checks the name with the network and that the registration service answers for this network and sponsors; only then does it make the keys and lock them, the way the person chose: to the device from birth (the device's key made if this browser has none in use, one request for both keys' secrets, a second request whose answers must open both new records: two or three requests), or under two passwords the wallet makes. A device that fails at any step after the lock was chosen (no PRF, a cancelled request, another key, a different answer the second time) never ends the sign-up: the wallet makes the passwords instead, and says what happened ("The device did not give its key, so your keys are not locked to it. The wallet made your passwords instead, and shows them to you now."). Where the wallet made them, the dialog "Your new passwords" shows them once, before anything is kept (see below), and "I have them" goes on. It makes the two recovery keys, both from five words ("words only"). Then one step, "Write down your recovery words", marked "Keep safe" (its words are in "Recovery: words on paper, and the Recovery Kit as an option" below): the words, written on paper by hand, and nothing to download or print. Nothing is asked back: the person is trusted to have written them down. "I wrote them down. Create my account", there from the start, keeps what is needed in this browser, creates the account, and opens it: "Your account is ready, protected from its first block. This browser checked it with the network. Next time, open it with your name and your login password." The card on Home says "Your account is protected." and "You can pay 600 of 600 seconds now without waiting, fees included."

"Start over", locking the wallet or switching to "Open my account" before "I wrote them down. Create my account" forget the keys and the words: "Nothing was created. The recovery words that were shown will never work: destroy the paper if you wrote them down." Nothing reaches the chain before that button.

**Left alone.** While the step of the recovery words is on the page no account exists, so the lock of an idle wallet has nothing to lock. The same rule closes the step (`signup-ui.mjs`, `PAPERS_ACTIVITY`; the time is `IDLE_LOCK_MS` of `guards.mjs`, which the lock uses too): after five minutes without use the keys and the words are forgotten as by "Start over", the form is back, and the notice says "Your recovery words were hidden after 5 minutes without use, and nothing was created. They will never work: destroy the paper if you wrote them down. Start again to get new ones." Use is what a person does who is writing the words down on the page: a tap or a click, a key, text that comes without a key (pasting, dictation), and scrolling by a wheel, a trackpad, a finger, the scroll bar or the keyboard; each starts the five minutes again. A pointer that only moves over the page, a field that takes the focus and the page being shown again do not count: they happen when nobody is there too, and the lock does not count them. From "I wrote them down. Create my account" on, nothing is hidden: the keys are being kept, and if the step is still on the page after a failure the five minutes start again.

**The account.** A login key, a payment key and the two recovery keys in their roles, two proofs for big changes, and the daily limit and the waits above: what "Protect my account" makes of an account, from its first block. The registration key that signs the registration becomes the account's identity key and holds no role. The newcomer pays nothing.

**What manual signup keeps, and when.** Nothing of the account before "I wrote them down. Create my account" (where the device made its key for the lock, its id and random handle are kept in `timecurrency.device-key.v1` at once: nothing of an account is in it). Then, before anything is sent, all of it or none of it (`signup-ui.mjs`, `keep`): the registration key, with the salt of the name, kept under the new login key (vault version 4: it opens only with that key, once the login password, and the device where the keys are locked, opened it), in the place of the account's login vault; the login key and the payment key, locked to the device with the passwords chosen (version 3) or under the passwords the wallet made (marked as made), kept aside in the places "Protect my account" uses (`security-flows.mjs`, `stageVaults`); and a note of the public half of the sign-up, signed by the registration key: the name, the registration key, the four keys with their acceptances of their roles, and the policy (`signup.mjs`). With the registration key and its salt that is everything `registerProtected` needs to be called again with the same arguments. Before each consent the registration key gives a sponsor leaves the browser, the expiry of that consent is kept too, signed by the registration key (`localStorage`, key `timecurrency.signup-consent.v1:<network id>:<name>`; see "Letting a kept sign-up go"). Once the chain holds the account with these keys, they take the places of the live keys, the registration key is overwritten (`settleStaged`) and the record of the consent removed. No password, no recovery word and no key is ever in the storage in the clear; the kind of recovery keys is kept with their public keys (see "Recovery: words on paper, and the Recovery Kit as an option").

**Where it can stop, and what the person sees on return.**

- *After the passwords, before "I wrote them down. Create my account"* (the page closed or reloaded, the wallet locked, "Start over"): nothing was kept and nothing was sent. The start screen is as before (after a reload, the form with neither way chosen); the words that were shown never work.
- *Whichever way was chosen,* a sign-up that was kept goes on as it was kept: the records say how the keys are locked, and nothing is chosen again. Opening it after a reload asks the device once where the lock was chosen, and takes the login password the wallet made where its passwords were chosen; the start screen of "Open my account" shows no choice.
- *After the button, before the registration service paid, or after it paid and before the registration was sent:* the start screen fills in the name and says "Creating @name was not finished. Open it with your login password: this browser goes on from what the network shows." The login password opens the login key kept aside (with one request to the device where the keys are locked to it), that key opens the registration key, and the wallet calls `registerProtected` again with what was kept; once the account is made it opens with that same login key, without asking the device again. A commitment the chain holds is not paid for again, and the service is not asked while the chain holds the commitment or the account: the wallet then passes `registerProtected` no sponsor. Should the commitment run out between the wallet's read and the SDK's, the SDK says so before it signs anything (`SPONSOR_REQUIRED`), and the wallet asks the service then. The registration is the same bytes as before.
- *After the registration, before the keys were put in place:* when the page opens it sees the account hold the kept keys and puts them in place before anything is typed: "The new keys of @name are active on this browser. Unlock with the login password chosen for them." If it could not read the chain then, the login password opens the registration key, the wallet finds the account on the chain, puts the keys in place and opens the account with the new login key.
- *Any other failure on the way* (a service that does not answer, a refusal the wallet has no sentence for): "Your sign-up is kept in this browser, locked with your passwords. Open your account with its name and your login password to go on. ", then the reason. The wallet is locked.

**Refusals, in the wallet's words.**

- The name was taken meanwhile (SDK code `NAME_TAKEN`): "Someone else took the name @name before your account was made. Nothing of yours is on the network. The recovery words you wrote down for @name will never work: destroy the paper. Create your account with another name." The form for a new account is shown again.
- The network refuses the name as too close to one that exists (`distinct_names`; told as in "A new name the network refuses" below): "That name is too close to @x, which already exists. Choose another. The recovery words you wrote down for @name will never work: destroy the paper."
- The service named a payment that is not for this account (`SPONSOR_MISMATCH`): "The registration service named a payment that is not for your account, so this browser did not use it, and nothing was registered. Try again later: open your account with its name and your login password, and this browser goes on. Your recovery words stay valid."
- The service paid for this key in the last 24 hours (its code `account_limit`), which happens when the commitment it paid for ran out, five minutes after, before the account was made: "The registration service says that it paid for this sign-up in the last 24 hours, and that this payment ran out before your account was made. It pays again 24 hours after the first time: then open your account with your login password, and this browser goes on. Your recovery words stay valid."
- Another commitment of this key on the chain (`COMMITMENT_MISMATCH`): "The network holds a registration of this sign-up's key with other details than this browser keeps, so nothing was signed. That should not happen: this browser's data may have been changed. Wait an hour, then open your account with your login password to try again."
- The account of this sign-up holds other keys or another policy (`REGISTRATION_MISMATCH`): "The account @name was made by this sign-up, but its keys or its limits are no longer the ones this browser made. If you changed them on another device, use that one. If you did not, someone has your recovery words: recover the account with them at once."
- The network does not take such a registration (`PROTECTED_REGISTRATION_UNAVAILABLE`; a genesis cannot lose the setting, so this means that the page is of another network): "This network does not take accounts that are protected from the start, so nothing was sent. Reload this page: the wallet then makes your account the way this network takes it."
- What was kept was changed: "The sign-up kept in this browser was changed after it was made, so this browser does not use it, and sends nothing for it. Create your account with another name." The note is used only when the registration key's signature over it holds, and only with the kept keys it names: whatever can write this browser's storage could otherwise put a payment key of its own into a sign-up that is gone on with. It cannot open the registration key, which only the login key kept aside opens.

**Its limits.**

- A sign-up that is kept holds its name in this browser ("An encrypted vault already exists for this account here. Unlock it instead.") until it is finished, or let go (see "Letting a kept sign-up go").
- The step of the recovery words is closed after five minutes without use (see "Left alone" above): a person who writes the words down slowly without touching the device for five minutes starts again, with new words.
- The account's first recovery words name the registration key in the salt of their key (see "Recovery words" below): a wallet that does not know that cannot use them.
- The wallet asks its gateway whether the name is free (`signup-ui.mjs`) minutes before the commitment of the name is in a block. A gateway that wants the name can take it first. On a test network that costs nothing; on a network with value it would matter, and the way out is to ask for several names at once, or to ask only once the commitment is in a block (web audit of November 2026, F-5; left open).

**On a network, in Chrome only.** The sign-up, each stop and each refusal ran on the stand-in page of `signup.test.mjs`, over a stand-in chain and registration service that apply the rules of `spec/settings.md`, section 4.1, with the SDK's own functions; that the engine takes the registration's bytes is shown in `client-v4/protected-signup.test.mjs`. On 23 November the browser check signed up two accounts this way ("wallet-create", "wallet-second") in Chrome 158 on laboratory networks with `protected_registration`, the faucet as the registration service (campaigns `tc-v5-final8` and `tc-v5-final9`): the card on Home showed the first protected at once, with the daily limit of 600 seconds. `tc-v5-final8` found that after the sign-up the start screen had lost its form, so that "Lock" left the wallet with no way to open it until the page was reloaded; fixed in `ff8fe46` (`signup-ui.mjs`; `signup.test.mjs` now checks the form after the account is made and after a lock), and the lock passed in `tc-v5-final9`. The stops and the refusals above have not run on a network.

## Recovery: words on paper, and the Recovery Kit as an option

A protected account has two recovery keys; the chain asks for both to replace lost keys, and for one of them with the payment password for a few other changes. The wallet makes them in one of two kinds (`papers.mjs`):

- **Words only**, the default at a protected sign-up and in "Protect my account": both keys come from five recovery words that the person writes on paper by hand (`security-flows.mjs`: the first is `passphraseKey`, the second `recoveryWordsSecondKey`, the same words with a salt of its own domain, `TCDEVNET-RECOVERY-WORDS-SECOND-V1`, or `...-REGISTRATION-V1` for the first keys of a protected sign-up). One paper, nothing downloaded, nothing printed: it works the same on every phone, and for someone who does not use files.
- **Words and a Recovery Kit**, offered under Keys, "New recovery words": the first key comes from the words, the second is a random recovery sheet, kept in a Recovery Kit (a file locked with the words, `recovery-file.mjs`; the format, and a vector, in `RECOVERY_FILE_FORMAT.md`) or printed instead (the printed recovery sheet).

**What words only gives up, and what the kit adds.** With words only, whoever has the paper has both recovery keys and can ask to take over the account (a root operation of two recovery keys: `v5/src/security.rs`, `ROOT`). That waits the account's waiting period, with the account frozen. Only the words stop it:

- "New recovery words" (Keys, "Change something"): the payment password and one recovery key, the words. The engine puts such a replacement in force at once (`install`): the waiting replacement is gone, the freeze ends, and the words that were used stop working. This is what the alert and "Waiting on your account" lead to first.
- Cancelling it ("Waiting on your account", or "Recover my account" on another browser): an operation of two recovery keys, so the words again. Whoever asked keeps the words and can ask again (and alternate asking and cancelling, each time with a fee for the account: web audit K-5, an engine rule for a later version).
- The payment password is one vote of the two a root operation needs: alone it stops nothing.

So an owner whose only copy was taken cannot stop it, and loses the account when the wait is over. An owner with a second copy, or whose paper was only copied, can stop it, if they look in time: the Alerts tell them (web audit of November 2026, K-1: the page said "you can cancel it", which was true only for an owner who still had the words). With a kit, the paper alone is not enough: recovering needs the paper and the file. The price is that losing either one ends recovery, where with words only only the paper can be lost; and a download cannot be confirmed, so a person may go on without a kit that was really saved. The wallet makes words only the default because losing the one thing that recovers an account is the likelier accident.

**The step, words only.** At manual sign-up, in "Protect my account", and under "New recovery words" with "Words only" (selected at first), in this order: "Write down your recovery words", marked "Keep safe"; "These five words bring your account back if you lose your passwords or this device. Take a pen and write them on paper."; "Paper is the safest place for them: nothing online can reach it. The step asks for two full copies, not for halves kept in two places. Halves mean that losing either place loses the account, and a half that is found makes the rest easier to guess (three of five words found leave 36 bits); two full copies protect against loss, and the copy that is left is what stops whoever took the other.

**The step with a kit.** Under "New recovery words" with "Words and a Recovery Kit": "Save your new Recovery Kit", "1. Every other form that needs one recovery key has one field, "Recovery words", with "Your recovery words. A printed recovery sheet works too." (freeze offers the payment password or the recovery words); the words give the first key, a printed sheet the sheet's key. The wallet tells them apart by the text (`recoveryPaperKind`).

**What the browser is told about the secret fields** (web audit of November 2026, W-6; `password-fields.test.mjs`). Every field that takes the payment password, the recovery words or a printed recovery sheet is a password field, hidden while typed (`type="password"`), and marked as a one-time code: `autocomplete="one-time-code"`, with `autocapitalize="off"`, `autocorrect="off"`, `spellcheck="false"` and the marks that 1Password, LastPass, Bitwarden and Proton Pass read to leave a field alone (`data-1p-ignore`, `data-lpignore="true"`, `data-bwignore`, `data-protonpass-ignore`). The printed sheet of "Recover my account" was a text field and is a password field now, like every other field that takes a sheet. The login password keeps `new-password` and `current-password`: a password manager holding it is wanted.

Why a password field, and not a text field hidden by a style: a browser never keeps what is typed in a password field in its form history, does not check its spelling, and a phone keyboard learns nothing from it; a text field is learned by some keyboards, and in Chrome the value of every text field of a form goes with a password saved from that form, as other possible user names, into the browser's store. What the marks do, read in the browsers' own code (24 November 2026):

- Chrome and Edge (Chromium, `components/password_manager/core/browser/form_parsing/form_data_parser.cc`): a field marked `one-time-code` is "not a password field" (`IsNotPasswordField`). In a form that also holds the login password, only the login password is offered to be saved. In a form whose only password fields are marked, the parser takes them as a last resort and marks the form "only for fallback": no prompt opens by itself (`PasswordManager::IsAutomaticSavePromptAvailable`), but on a computer the key at the end of the address bar can still offer it to a person who clicks it. Form history never keeps a password field (`autocomplete_history_manager.cc`).
- Firefox (`toolkit/components/passwordmgr/LoginManagerChild.sys.mjs`): every field that is or was a password field counts, whatever its `autocomplete`, and `autocomplete="off"` is ignored by default (`signon.storeWhenAutocompleteOff`). A form whose sending the page stops (all of the wallet's) is not seen as sent, so no prompt opens by itself; but on a computer, typing in a password field puts a closed "Save login" key in the address bar (`signon.passwordEditCapture.enabled`), and leaving the page can bring up the prompt for a field that still holds what was typed. The wallet empties each secret field as it reads it. Form history never keeps a password field (`FormHistoryChild.sys.mjs`).
- Safari: where it decides to offer saving is not in WebKit's open code; Apple's documentation knows `one-time-code` as the field of a code received by message. Not known.

Seen in a real Chrome (version 158, 24 November 2026: the browser check's step `wallet-password-manager` on a trial network, runs `r1124153428` and `r1124160357`, and on the public test network, run `r1124162325`, in a profile that saves passwords, with Chrome's own log of its password manager kept beside each run):

Chrome offered to save nothing by itself in any of them, also once it had taken the form as sent.
- In each of them Chrome did take what was typed as a password and kept it ready (its state `PENDING_PASSWORD_STATE`, no prompt): a person who clicks the key at the end of the address bar and then "Save" saves the payment password or the recovery words. Nothing in a page's markup takes that key away while the field is a password field.
- After the sign-up Chrome took the form as sent only after a request made with `XMLHttpRequest`: the wallet's own requests, made with `fetch`, did not count, and its fields emptied at once read to Chrome as a login that failed. `password-fields.test.mjs` therefore refuses `XMLHttpRequest` in every script the page loads. The forms of secrets only were taken as sent without such a request too (the payment, as soon as it was sent for review), and Chrome's check whether a prompt may open then showed none, as its code says of a form whose only password fields are marked.

Not seen in a real browser: Firefox, Safari, Edge, any phone, any password manager added to a browser.

So no mark is certain in every browser: Chrome by itself and Firefox by itself save nothing without a press of the person, and Safari is not known. Each field of recovery words therefore says "Do not let the browser save them.", and the fields of a new payment password already said so.

Left as it is (web audit of November 2026): the words are typed on the same page as the payment password, in everyday forms ("Pay now with a recovery paper", ending a freeze or a pause, a new payment password, new recovery words). One program that records keys, or one copied form that someone was led to, gets both (K-9).

**Keys.** On an account whose recovery is one paper (words only) and whose proven balance is more than an hour of seconds, the card of the words has one quiet line: "Your recovery is one paper. Add a Recovery Kit so that a paper alone is not enough.", which opens "New recovery words" with "Words and a Recovery Kit" selected (`actions.mjs`, `KIT_HINT`). Nothing blocks, and nothing else asks for it. The cards say what fits the kind of recovery keys this browser made for the account: it keeps the kind with the two public keys (`localStorage`, `timecurrency.recovery-kind.v1:<network id>:<name>`; nothing secret) and uses it only while the account's recovery keys are those two (`recoveryKindOf`). Words only: one card to keep safe, the recovery words. With a kit: the kit's card too. Keys made in another browser: what holds for both.

**What it protects, and what not.**

- New words are five, 90 bits, where they were four: the words can now be the only secret behind both keys. Words of accounts made before, four of them, are read as always, everywhere a person types words.
- Lost words leave an account with no usable recovery key unless it has a kit that was printed: it keeps working with its passwords, but a lost password cannot be replaced, and new words cannot be made (they need one current recovery key). Keys says so on the card of the words.
- The kit alone opens nothing: guessing 90 bits at 256 MiB a guess is out of reach, and the key that locks it is not a recovery key of the account (its salt has a domain of its own, `TIME-RECOVERY-KIT-WORDS-V1`).
- The person is trusted to have kept what the step shows: words written down wrong, or a kit downloaded and lost, are found out only when they are needed.

## Letting a kept sign-up go

Open a saved signup through Time Vault to finish it. With manual setup, use its login password. A person who forgot that password can let it go from the start screen: under "Open my account", when the name typed is that of a sign-up this browser keeps, one line says "Forgot the login password you chose for this sign-up?" with the button "Let this sign-up go" (`signup-ui.mjs`, `letGo`; the rule is `signup.mjs`, `letGoState`).

**When it is offered.** Only when nothing of the sign-up can ever be on the network, so that letting it go cannot lose an account, or keys that an account could still come to have. A state of the network that this browser verifies at that moment, read by the name and by the registration key, must show:

- no account of the sign-up: none whose identity key is the registration key, and none of its name that holds one of its keys;
- no commitment of the registration key that the chain can still use: the registration needs one that has not run out;
- no other account of its name. Such a sign-up can never finish, and letting it go would free nothing; and it is the one case in which an account of that name exists, which whatever can write this browser's storage could pass off as a sign-up of its own (below);
- and the chain's time past the expiry of every consent the registration key gave a sponsor, since a sponsor may still use one to make a commitment until then. Only the registration key signs a consent, and only the login password opens it here: whoever forgot that password gives no new one.

The expiry of the last consent is the record this browser keeps of it (above), when its signature by the registration key holds and it names this network, this name and this key. Without such a record (a sign-up kept before there was one, or a record that was removed or does not hold) the page waits by itself: every consent the SDK signs is dated at most five minutes ahead of the chain (`TimeSDK.SPONSOR_CONSENT_MAX_AHEAD_MICROS`), so ten minutes of the chain's time after its first reading that showed nothing, all that were given before have run out. That first reading is kept in the page's memory only: a page opened again waits again.

The network, and the record of the consent with it, are read once before the review and once again after it is confirmed (another tab that goes on with the sign-up meanwhile keeps a newer record); both must allow it.

**What the person is told.**

- Offered: a review, "Let this sign-up go", with two lines: "@name is free again, for anyone. This browser forgets the keys it made for this sign-up; it cannot be undone.", and "The recovery words you wrote down for @name will never work: destroy the paper." Its button is "Let it go". Then: "This browser let the sign-up of @name go. Destroy the paper of its recovery words." Cancelled: "Nothing was changed. The sign-up is still kept in this browser."
- An account of the sign-up on the network: "The network holds the account @name that this sign-up made, so this browser keeps what opens it. Open it with the login password you chose for it; if you forgot that, recover the account with your recovery words, under "Other ways in"."
- Another account of the name: "The name @name belongs to another account on the network, so this sign-up can never finish, and this browser keeps it as it is. If that account is yours, open it with its login password, or recover it with your recovery words; if not, create your account with another name."
- A commitment: "A registration of @name from this sign-up waits on the network until …, so this browser keeps the sign-up. If you remember your login password, open your account with it before then; if not, come back after that time to let it go."
- A consent that may still be used: "Nothing of this sign-up is on the network now, but a registration of it that was asked for could still reach it until …. Come back after that time to let it go." Without a record: "… This browser does not know when a registration of it was last asked for, so it waits ten minutes to be sure that none can reach the network any more: keep this page open, and press "Let this sign-up go" again after …."

**What is removed.** The five places of the sign-up, and nothing else: the registration key in the login place, the login and payment keys kept aside, the note, and the record of the consent. Each is removed only while it still holds what was checked, under the lock of the name's vault.

**What whatever can write this browser's storage can and cannot do with it.** Nothing is removed on the word of the storage alone: the button, the network read twice and the person's confirmation come first. A note that is not the one the registration key signed, or keys aside that are not the note's, is refused ("The sign-up kept in this browser was changed after it was made, so this browser cannot tell which keys are this sign-up's, and does not let it go. Create your account with another name."). A payment key kept in the live place of the name is an account's, and the sign-up of that name is not let go ("This browser keeps a payment key of an account @name, so it does not let a sign-up of that name go. Open that account with its login password."). A record of a consent that does not hold is not used. What remains:

- Whatever kept an older record of this very sign-up, signed by its key, can put it back in place of the newer one. Letting go is then offered after the older consent's expiry, while a newer consent, given less than five minutes before, may still be used. For that to make an account, a sponsor would have to use that consent, and the registration, which only the registration key signs, would have to reach the chain from a copy kept of an earlier attempt. The account would hold nothing, and its keys would be gone from this browser; the person's recovery words recover it, if they were not destroyed.
- Whatever can write the storage can delete a kept sign-up, as it could before; and it can put a sign-up of its own key in the place of an account's login key, which deletes that key by itself. Letting go is then refused: the network holds an account of that name, and a payment key of the name may be kept here too.
- A sign-up whose name someone else took on the network (the refusal "Someone else took the name …") cannot be let go: its name is not free in any case. Its entries stay in this browser.
- The state that shows nothing is a state of the network this browser verified; it is as recent as such a state is, and no more.

**Not run on a network or in a browser.** It ran on the stand-in page of `signup.test.mjs`, over its stand-in chain and registration service: let go once the consent ran out and signed up again under the same name, refused with a commitment, with the account, and with every change of the storage above; and the record kept with the expiry the SDK gave.

## Protection: freeze, daily limit, pause

Home has one card, "Protection" (`index.html`, `protect-card`; what it says is `protection.mjs`, `protectionView`; `security-ui.mjs` draws it and signs). What it shows is read from the account's security row in proven state at every reading of the account, never from what this browser remembers. A switch that is pressed goes back at once to what the chain says, and opens a small step that asks for what the chain needs for it; the switch moves when a verified state shows the change. One step is open at a time, and a step that closes takes what was typed into it along.

- **Not protected yet.** An account with one key: "One key can move everything, and a forgotten password cannot be replaced.", and one button, "Protect my account", which opens the form on Keys with the focus on its first field. The form suggests a daily limit of 600 seconds, written "10 minutes of seconds a day (600)"; the number is in one place (`security-flows.mjs`, `DEFAULT_DAILY_LIMIT_SECONDS`), and a limit the person typed is not written over. Nothing about the account is held back until it is protected. On a network that takes protected sign-ups a new account of this wallet is protected from its first block and never shows this; an account made before, or by another wallet, can.
- **Freeze.** A switch. On: the payment password or one recovery paper, one review, at once.
  - A freeze lasts the account's freeze length (48 hours unless another was chosen) from the block that holds it, and ends by itself. Nothing keeps it on. It can be placed again only in its second half, and then lasts the whole length from that moment: the card names the time from which that is possible, and offers "Freeze for another 48 hours" from then.
  - While frozen, the payment password alone pays nothing and starts nothing, websites that use the login library take no sign-in, and what was waiting was dropped when the freeze began. Seconds sent to the account arrive. The payment password together with one recovery paper can still pay, and ends the freeze early: that is "End the freeze", which the switch opens when it is pressed while on.
  - An account that is frozen because a change of all its keys waits is shown as that, and its switch cannot be used: that change is finished or cancelled under "Waiting on your account".
- **Daily limit.** A bar and one sentence: what can be paid now without waiting, out of the limit. The numbers are the chain's allowance, which refills evenly over 24 hours (`/sdk/account-security.mjs`, `allowance`), and the limit in force (a raise whose wait is over counts, although the stored row shows it only after the account's next action; `protection.mjs`, `effectiveLimit`). "Change" opens the change of the limit: a lower limit applies at once; a higher one waits the account's waiting period and can be cancelled, or applies at once when a recovery paper is typed as well. Which of the three the typed number will be stands under the field while it is typed, and again in the review.
- **Pause payments.** A switch; see the next paragraphs.
- **Waiting.** The card counts the payments and changes that wait. The list itself, each item with when it takes effect and its "Cancel", is the card "Waiting on your account" directly under it; it was on Keys. The count that stood on the Keys button stands on the Home button. A payment that waits is listed as "@name · account N": the chain names only the number, so the page reads the name with a proof, once per page (`security-ui.mjs`, `askNames`), and shows the number alone until then. "Started in this browser" is said only when this browser's own record (its activity) holds the waiting payment it signed (web audit of November 2026, W-5).
- The card ends with one sentence: switching protection on is immediate; switching it off waits, or needs a recovery paper, because nobody can reverse a theft here.

**What "Pause payments" is on the chain.** The engine has no pause. The wallet lowers the daily limit to exactly the fee of one action (kind 77, a lowering, at once; `security-flows.mjs`, `buildPause`), and an account whose limit is not above the fee is what the card calls paused.

- *Why not zero.* The engine takes a limit of zero (`v5/src/security.rs` puts no lower bound on `daily_limit_micros`). But it charges every action the payment key signs with a nonce to the allowance, fee included: a payment, both steps of a payment that waits, a change of the limit, a change of login keys. With a limit of zero the payment key alone could then not even start a payment that waits or ask for a higher limit, and the one way back would be a recovery paper. With a limit of one fee no payment of any amount fits (a payment costs its amount and the fee), and the payment key can still do one thing a day that costs a fee.
- *What still can happen while paused.* The payment password can start a payment that waits the waiting period, and send it after that if nobody cancelled it; ask for the limit back, which waits as long; freeze; cancel what waits; replace a login key. Each of the first, the second and the last costs a fee, and the paused limit covers one fee a day between them. A pause does not stop a payment that waits: whoever does not look at "Waiting on your account" for a whole waiting period is not protected by it. A freeze drops what waits; a pause does not.
- *Switching it on* costs a fee that is charged to the allowance, like every change of the limit. With less than one fee left of the day's limit the chain refuses it; the wallet says so before anything is signed, and names the freeze, which is charged to the balance. A raise of the limit that was waiting is dropped by the pause, and the review says so. A frozen account cannot be paused.
- *Switching it off by waiting* is a raise of the limit (kind 77): it waits the waiting period, is listed under "Waiting on your account" and can be cancelled there by the payment key or a recovery paper. It costs a fee charged to the allowance, so it is not possible while the day's one fee is used up: the step then says when it will be.
- *Switching it off at once* takes the payment password and one recovery paper. The engine has one operation that sets a limit at once: a replacement of the keys and the policy approved by the payment key and a recovery key (kind 73). The wallet signs one that keeps every key in its role and changes the limit only (`buildLimitNow`). Being a replacement, it also cancels everything that waits on the account and moves its authority epoch on, so websites end their sessions; the review says both. It would end a freeze as well, so it is not offered for a frozen account. The same operation raises an ordinary limit at once from "Change".
- *After a pause the allowance is not full.* By either way the allowance keeps what was left under the paused limit, at most one fee, and refills over 24 hours under the limit that is back. Right after a pause ends almost nothing can be paid at once. The steps, the reviews and the card say so.
- *The limit before the pause* is remembered in this browser so that ending the pause can offer it: `localStorage`, key `timecurrency.pause.v1:<network id>:<account name>`, `{"limit": "<microseconds>", "at": <time>}`; nothing secret. It is written before the pause is sent, removed when the pause was ended at once, and dropped when a reading shows the account not paused and the record is older than ten minutes. It is a suggestion in a field that can be changed, shown again in the review. A browser that holds no record asks for the number.
- *A limit below the fee*, zero included, is not something this wallet sets. An account that has one (from another wallet) is shown as paused, with what that means: the payment password alone starts nothing, and only the way with a recovery paper is offered.

**How the card can mislead, and what it does not show.**

- "Paused" is not "nothing can leave". A payment can be made to wait and sent after the waiting period, by whoever has the payment password. The card says so in one sentence; it does not watch the list for the person.
- "Frozen" does not hold against the payment password together with a recovery paper, and it ends by itself.
- The card shows the state of the last verified reading, which the open wallet repeats every five seconds. While the wallet says "Verification paused" the card shows the last state it could verify, without a mark of its own.
- The bar is what the payment password can pay at once. What can leave in 24 hours is up to about twice the limit, which Keys says and the card does not.
- The times of a freeze and of a waiting change are the chain's times written in this device's time zone and format. On a network whose waits survive a stop they are the earliest times, said "not before" (see "The clock of the waits").
- The remembered limit is not locked as a key is: whatever can write this browser's storage can change the number that ending a pause suggests. The number is shown before anything is signed.
- "Not protected yet" says that a forgotten password cannot be replaced. An account with guardians, or with a key in another browser, has those ways in besides; the card does not list them (Keys does).
- A switch that is greyed says nothing by itself; the sentence under it says why.

**On a network, in Chrome only.** The card, its steps and both ways of ending a pause ran on the stand-in page of the tests (`protection.test.mjs`, `protection-card.test.mjs`) and, on 2026-11-22, in headless Chrome 157 against the local preview (`tests/preview-server.mjs`, `tests/preview-drive.mjs`) at 360 and 1280 px, with the selectors and in the order of the browser check's steps: the card of a new protected account, a press of the space bar on a switch, pause on, a payment that then waits, pause off with the recovery sheet, the freeze from the switch and its end. In both, the chain is `tests/mock-security-chain.mjs`: a reading of the engine's rules, written for these tests, and not the engine. On 23 November the steps of `campaign/lib/browser-check.mjs` ran on a laboratory network, in headless Chrome 158 on Linux (campaign `tc-v5-final9`, 38 of 38): "protection-limit-lower" (a lower limit from "Change", in force at once), "protection-card" (the card of an account protected from its first block, read from proven state, its switches real switches with labels, and nothing that needs sideways scrolling at a phone's width), "protection-pause" (the verified limit was one fee, and a payment made to wait waited on the chain), "protection-pause-off" with the recovery sheet (the verified limit was back at once and what waited was cancelled), and "security-freeze" from the switch. A node took a pause signed by this wallet, and a replacement that changes the limit only. "protection-not-yet" did not run: on a network with `protected_registration` the check's accounts are protected from their first block. In `tc-v5-final8` these steps could not run (see "Signing up protected", the form after a sign-up). A person must check on a phone: that each switch can be tapped anywhere on its row and shows a focus ring from the keyboard; that a screen reader says "Freeze, switch, off"; and that the card reads well in the dark colours.

## Alerts: what needs your attention

At the top of Home, before the balance, the panel "Alerts" (`index.html`, `alerts-panel`; `alerts-ui.mjs` draws it, `alerts.mjs` works out what it says). It is never behind a menu, and nothing of it is on the start screen or on a locked wallet.

- **The list.** Every alert, most urgent first, each with its title, "Urgent" where it is, and one or two sentences that say what happened and where to act, in the words the page uses there. With nothing to say: "Nothing needs your attention."
- **The banner** above the panel (`alerts-banner`, read out at once by a screen reader) shows the title of the first urgent alert this browser's owner has not confirmed yet.
- **"I have seen these"** stores what was shown (below). The banner goes; what is still true, such as a payment that still waits, stays in the list; a change that was new since the last look is gone from it. Nothing is confirmed by itself.
- **The status line** says "Watching live" while the hint below is open, else "Checked a moment ago".

**What an alert can be.** All of it comes from the state the wallet has just verified, read with the account's security row; the panel takes no word from a gateway or a service.

- *True now:* the keys are being replaced (someone used the recovery words to ask for new keys: make new recovery words now, under Keys, "Change something", which ends it at once; cancelling it "on Home, under “Waiting on your account”" needs the same keys, and whoever asked can ask again; "Without your recovery words you cannot stop this."); guardians are recovering the account (More, Guardian recovery); the account is frozen; a payment waits; a higher daily limit waits (cancel each "on Home, under “Waiting on your account”", then freeze with the Freeze switch on Home).
- *Changed since this browser last confirmed:* the account was protected with new keys; the keys or the recovery words were replaced; the rules changed at once; the daily limit was lowered, raised at once, or raised after its wait; a login key was added or removed; a key was added or removed (an account with one key; More, Devices); the account was frozen and the freeze is over; and "Something changed that this wallet cannot show" where the counters of the account moved by more than what it can name; and actions signed with the account's keys that this browser did not send: "N actions were signed with your keys since you last looked here. This browser sent M of them. If the others were not you on another device, make a new payment password under Keys, “Change something”.", urgent when M is none and N is three or more (web audit of November 2026, K-4: payments wake nobody, so a stolen payment key is seen here or nowhere). The page keeps the nonce of each action it sends with its activity (`app.js`, `recordReceipt`) and passes how many it holds above the marker's nonce; a change of keys put in force at once moves the nonce by one more and is not counted. The activity holds the last 40 actions: beyond that, the count is short and the alert may come though it was all you. An activity this browser could not read (its login key cannot open what was kept: keys made by a recovery, a backup of another key; see "What this browser keeps") starts again at the account's nonce of its first verified reading and says so: actions are then counted only against a marker at or above that nonce, so that the actions this browser did send are never called signed elsewhere; until the next "I have seen these" nothing is said about actions (`locked-store.mjs`, `sentSinceOf`).

**The marker.** What the panel compares with is a marker of the account as this browser last confirmed it: counters, keys and rules, nothing secret (`localStorage`, key `timecurrency.alerts-seen.v1:<network id>:<account>`). Since 24 November it is version 2: the same fields, written by a page that keeps the nonce of what it sends. A version-1 marker is read as before and gives no alert about actions signed elsewhere, until the next "I have seen these". The marker and the ids confirmed are only as safe as this browser's storage: whatever can write it, another program on the device or a browser extension, can hide a change since the last look (web audit, W-4). The list of names paid (below) is encrypted: such a program can empty it, not write a name into it. The first time a browser opens an account there is none: the marker of what it sees is stored at once, and only what is true now is shown; that browser cannot know what happened before it. Afterwards only "I have seen these" stores it, together with the ids of the alerts shown (`timecurrency.alerts-shown.v1:<network id>:<account>`, at most 64), which keep the banner away from an alert already confirmed while it stays true. Against a marker that goes back (another chain under the same name, or one written by something else) nothing is said about changes, as on a first look.

**Live while the wallet is open.** While the wallet is unlocked the page keeps one `EventSource` on `/v1/touches?account=N` under its own address: the API's hint (`client-v4/api.mjs`, `GET /v1/touches`), which the test network's proxy routes there. A hint says only "this account was named at this height" and is not believed: it asks for one verified read, at most one every two seconds, so that hints that come together are one read; the stream coming back after a break asks for one too. It is closed when the wallet locks and when another account is opened. Where the route is absent (a lab gateway) the stream fails once and the wallet goes on with its read every five seconds, without a word about it. The page's policy is unchanged for this: `connect-src 'self'`.

**The switch: alerts while the wallet is closed.** Under the list, what this browser can do, in the words of `alerts-push.mjs`:

- "Alerts are off for this browser." with why not: the page is not on `https`; "On an iPhone or iPad, alerts work only from the Home Screen. In Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and open the wallet from there. Then turn alerts on."; the browser has no push; notifications are blocked in its settings; or "This wallet's service does not send alerts." (no alert service under the wallet's address).
- "Alerts are off for this browser. Turn them on to hear at once when someone asks to take over your account, even with the wallet closed.", with "Turn on alerts".
- "Alerts are on for this browser.", with "Turn off alerts". It is said only while the browser holds a push subscription and this browser's record says it gave that subscription to the alert service for this account.
- Under both, "Turn off alerts on every browser of this account", and what a person will see: the fixed sentence "Open your wallet to check your account.", never more, and what the service keeps.

"Turn on alerts" asks the browser for notifications first, within the press; nothing is sent before the person allows them. Then it registers `sw.js`, subscribes with the alert service's key, asks the service for a challenge bound to that subscription, signs it with the login key as a login to this wallet's own origin and network (`alerts-push.mjs`, `proveFor`: a challenge that names any other origin is not signed), and sends the subscription. A step of the browser's push that fails or does not answer within 30 seconds ends with "Alerts could not be turned on in this browser. You can try again; if it keeps failing, this browser cannot receive them.", and nothing was sent. A frozen account is refused by the service, and told so. "Turn off alerts" asks the service to forget this browser by its push address and ends the subscription in the browser; "on every browser" signs a proof, as for turning on, and the service wakes every browser of the account, then forgets them. A proof that the service refuses with 403 because its challenge is stale (given out before the service restarted, or used already) is made once more with a new challenge; a frozen account is not asked again.

**Whether the service still has this browser.** The login key alone can remove every subscription of the account at the alert service (web audit of November 2026, K-3), and the switch used to go on saying "on" from this browser's record. While the wallet is unlocked, once when the account opens and then at most once a minute, a browser whose record says alerts are on asks `POST /alerts/v1/status` with `{"endpoint": <its push address>}`. On `200 {"subscribed": false}` it forgets its record, the switch says off, and the panel shows at its top, urgent, until alerts are on again: "Alerts were turned off for this browser, and this browser did not do it. Someone who can sign in as you can do that. Turn them on again, then make a new login password under Keys." A service without the route answers 404, and nothing changes. A subscribe that the service refuses with 409 because the account already has alerts on eight browsers: "This account already has alerts on eight browsers. Use “Turn off alerts on every browser of this account”, then turn them on here."

**What this browser keeps** for the switch: the push service's address of this browser for each account it subscribed (`timecurrency.alerts-push.v1:<network id>:<account>`). No key, no password. Turning off removes it.

**What the services learn.**

- *The API*, from the hint: which account an open wallet follows, from which address, while it is open. It learns nothing it could not see by watching the chain for that account.
- *The alert service* ([`deploy/alerts/README.md`](../../deploy/alerts/README.md)): the account number, once proven by its login key, and this browser's push address and its two public keys, to encrypt for it. It never learns what happened to the account, and it cannot send words: every push is the constant `{"v":1}`, and the browser shows its own sentence.
- *The browser's push service* (Google's, Apple's, Mozilla's or Microsoft's, by the browser): when this browser is woken. The pushes come a few seconds after public blocks that hold an action of the account, so over a few events it can tie the person it knows as this browser's user to the account number. The content is encrypted for the browser and tells it nothing.
- A push can be lost, and the service keeps no queue: the panel, read when the wallet opens, is what counts.

**On a network, without a push.** The panel, the marker, the live read and every state of the switch ran on the stand-in page of the tests (`alerts.test.mjs`, `alerts-ui.test.mjs`, `alerts-page.test.mjs`, `sw.test.mjs`) and, on 2026-11-23, in headless Chrome on the local preview at 320, 390, 768 and 1280 px, light and dark (`tests/preview-drive.mjs`, `--only alerts`): a payment that waits and a replacement of the keys arriving by the hint with nothing pressed, "I have seen these", the start screen and a locked wallet without the panel, and the switch with no service, off, pressed, on (with a stand-in subscription), on an iPhone and with notifications blocked. Pressed in headless Chrome it said "Alerts could not be turned on": Chrome does not offer push in a browser context of its own, as the preview and the browser check use. The browser check has three steps for it ("alerts-second-browser", "alerts-waiting", "alerts-switch"; see `campaign/lib/browser-check.mjs`), which first passed on a trial network and on the public test network on 23 November and are part of every run since: a second browser showed a payment made to wait 1.3 seconds after the first said it waits, and the switch, pressed in that headless Chrome, said that alerts could not be turned on (`AUDIT-2026-11.md`, "Recovery on paper, alerts and a check of the device (evening of 23 November 2026)"). No push has reached a real phone.

## The clock of the waits

On a network whose genesis has `waits_survive_stops` the waits and the freezes of a protected account do not run while the chain is stopped (`spec/settings.md`, section 3). Every moment its security row stores is then a moment on the clock of the waits: the chain's time since genesis less the total of its stops, which every proven state carries (`stopped_micros`). Read on the chain's time, after a stop of more than five minutes a freeze would look over, a payment that waits ready, a raise of the limit applied and the allowance refilled, each too early by the stops.

- **Compared with that clock** (`TimeSDK.securityNow`; `security-flows.mjs`, `waitsOf`): the Protection card (the freeze and when it can be placed again, what can be paid now, a raise that waits, the pause and its end), Keys (the limit in force, the payment card), Send and its choice above the limit, staking, the operation of a validator and the moment its limit has come back, a validator's signal, a website's sign-in, what waits on the account and the moment it can be sent, the end of a freeze and a raise of the limit, a recovery on a new browser and its end, and a browser whose login key was removed.
- **Shown as a date** with the stops added (`TimeSDK.securityDeadline`; `momentOf`). While a later stop can still move it the date is the earliest, and the page says so: "Your account is frozen. It ends not before …", "freeze again not before …", "it applies not before …", "Can be sent not before …", "It can finish not before …", "The pause ends not before …", in a review "48 hours: it can be sent not before …" and "Not before …". The screen "Validator signal" adds the stops of the state its status was read from, which the SDK's status of a signal carries (`waits`): "This account is frozen; the freeze ends not before … UTC."
- **Left on the chain's time:** what a key signs. The expiry of a freeze, of the approvals of an operation with two keys and of a login request for another browser, and the check that retires such a signed action once its expiry has passed (`security-ui.mjs`, `retired`).
- **On a network without the setting** the two clocks are one, every date is exact and no "not before" is said: nothing changed.

**Not run across a stop on a network.** On 23 November the page ran in Chrome on laboratory networks with the setting (campaigns `tc-v5-final8` and `tc-v5-final9`), on which the chain did not stop, so that the two clocks were one there. `waits-clock.test.mjs` runs the real page on a stand-in chain that stood still for six hours; ten of its eleven tests fail on the page before this clock, and the eleventh is the network without the setting. The check of staking and of a validator's operation against the daily limit (`security-ui.mjs`, `limitOf`, which `stakeLimit` and `stakeAction` use) is held by the last but one test of `validator-protected.test.mjs`. One place has no test that fails without it: the lookup of an item that waits when it is acted on (`actOnEvent`), whose result is the same on either clock. No chain with the setting has stopped under this page.

## A name that looks like one you have paid

An account name is 2 to 15 characters of `a-z`, `0-9` and `_`, so no capital letter, accent or space can imitate a name. A name can still read like another one: `paypa1` for `paypal`, `alice_` for `alice`, `barn` for `bam` in some fonts, `b1nance` for `binance`. Before a payment is signed, its review compares the name it goes to with the names this account has paid from this browser and with the account's own name (`names.mjs`; `app.js`, `review`, `payeeLines` and `paidNamesNow`).

- **A name this account has paid:** the review is as it was, unless another name it has paid looks like it (the same looser key, below). Then: "You have paid @b1nance before, and also @binance, which looks like it. One of them may not be who you think. Check every letter.", with the box. One of the two may have been paid by mistake, or written into this browser's list by someone else (web audit of November 2026, W-4).
- **A name it has not paid, which looks like one it has:** a warning that shows both names in the page's monospace font, where `1` and `l`, `0` and `o` differ: "You have not paid @paypa1 before. It looks like @paypal, which you have paid. Check every letter." The review has a box, "I checked the name, letter by letter.", and the button that signs stays disabled until it is ticked, also after the review's first 0.7 seconds. A review confirmed without the tick counts as cancelled.
- **A name it has not paid, which looks like the account's own name:** the same warning and the same box, with the words "It looks like @alice, your own name." A request to send seconds "back to yourself" may name such an account. When a name looks like the own name and like a paid one, the stronger likeness (by the order of the rule below) is the one told, and the own name when both are equally strong.
- **Any other name it has not paid:** one plain line, "First payment to @name."

**When two names look alike.** Two different names, by the first of these that holds:

1. Their skeletons are equal. The skeleton of a name is the name with every `_` dropped, `0` read as `o`, `1` as `l` and `w` as `vv`, and then `rn` read as `m` and `vv` as `w`.
2. Their looser keys are equal: the skeleton with `cl` read as `d` and `nn` as `m` (`aclmin` for `admin`, `annazon` for `amazon`; web audit of November 2026, F-3), then `i` read as `l`, and the digits that are written for letters read as those letters (`3` e, `4` a, `5` s, `7` t, `8` b, `2` z, `9` and `6` g). The chain's own rule against such names does not read `cl` and `nn`: it is a setting of the network's genesis (`v5/src/names.rs`) and changes only at the next reset. Until then the chain registers `aclmin` beside `admin`, and only the wallet warns.
3. Both skeletons have four characters or more and differ by one edit: one character added, removed or changed, or two neighbours swapped.

**Which payments.** Send, for an account with one key and for a protected account (within the limit, "Pay after the waiting period" and "Pay now with a recovery paper"). A payment a website asks for: the name is that of the account the website names by its number, read from proven state. Not staking: a validator is chosen from a list by its account number, and no name is typed or shown. The sending of a payment that has waited (Home, "Waiting on your account", "Send now"): it goes to the account number that was signed when it was made to wait; the review reads that account again with a proof, shows "@name · account N", and checks the name as every payment's (web audit, W-5). Its line "Started" says "In this browser" only when this browser's own record holds it, else "Not in this browser. Send it only if you started it on another device."

**The names typed for guardians.** Guardians can replace every key of an account, so a name typed for one that reads like another may be someone else's account (More, Guardian recovery; `recovery.mjs`; `names.mjs`, `guardianChecks`). Each typed name is compared with the account's own name, with the names it has paid from this browser, and with the guardians typed before it in the same list. For each likeness the review of the policy shows one warning with both names, in the same font: "@d4na looks like @dana, your own name. Check every letter.", "@karol looks like @carol, which you have paid. Check every letter.", "@b0b looks like @bob, another guardian in this list. Check every letter." The review then has the box "I checked the guardian names, letter by letter.", and nothing is signed without the tick. A typed name that is itself one the account has paid is known, and is not compared with the other paid names. The guardians of a policy the account already has are not compared with: a new policy replaces the whole list.

**What it compares with.** The names this account has paid from this browser on this network, the account's own name, and nothing else. The list is kept in the browser beside the activity, encrypted, readable only while the wallet is unlocked (`localStorage`, key `timecurrency.names.v2:<network id>:<account name>`; see "What this browser keeps"), and nothing is sent anywhere. A name enters it when this browser has verified the receipt of a payment to that name that was signed here; a payment that waits counts once the action that makes it wait has its receipt. The account a website was paid at is added when that payment is made; if its receipt is only checked later, from Activity, it is not. The payments that Activity kept from before this list existed fill it too (Activity keeps the newest 40 actions, and names no website's account). 500 names are kept; after that the one paid longest ago goes.

**What it cannot do.**

- It knows nothing about a name this account has never paid from this browser: a name you only heard of, a name you paid from another browser or another wallet, or before this browser's data was cleared. In a new browser every payment is a "First payment", and no look-alike is found.
- It does not know who has paid you. The wallet keeps no record of payments that came in, so a name is not compared with the names of senders.
- If your first payment went to the look-alike, the look-alike is the name this browser knows, and the warning then stands on the right name.
- It does not find every look-alike: not two changed letters, not one changed letter in a short name (`bob`, `rob`), not a name with a word added (`alice_shop` for `alice`), not a digit written for a letter together with a changed letter, and no pair of letters that look alike in some font and are not in the list above.
- The list is encrypted under a key that exists only while the wallet is unlocked. Whatever can change what this browser stores while it is locked, such as another program on the device, can remove the list (it is then empty, as in a new browser), and cannot put a name on it. A browser extension that runs in the wallet's page while it is unlocked can do both.
- It is a warning, not a block. With the box ticked the payment is signed, and from then on that name is one you have paid. It is said again only while another paid name looks like it.
- "First payment" means the first that this list knows of.
- A list that cannot be read is an empty one, and one that cannot be stored is kept for as long as the page is open. No payment fails for it.

**A new name the network refuses.** A network may refuse to register a name that looks like a name it already has. Its refusal reaches the wallet through the gateway, and holds the words "too close to an existing name", or "too close to the existing name", followed by that name. The wallet takes the name only as where to look. When, by the rule above, it looks like the name you chose, and proven state holds an account of that name, the wallet says "That name is too close to @x, which already exists. Choose another." and shows the form for a new account again (`names.mjs`, `tooCloseName`; `app.js`, `nameRefusal`). Otherwise the refusal is shown as every gateway's words are. The encrypted vault that was made for the refused name stays in this browser; it opens no account.

**Not run in a browser.** The line, the warning, the box and the refused name ran on the stand-in page of the tests (`names.test.mjs`, `name-warning.test.mjs`; the own name and the guardians in `protection-card.test.mjs`), never in a browser and never against a network that refuses such names. A person must check in a browser: that the two names of the warning are drawn in a font in which `1` and `l`, `0` and `o` differ, each on one line, also on a phone; that the box stands beside its sentence and the button stays grey until it is ticked; that the review of a first payment and of a repeated one read well; and the same in the window a website opens.

## Recovery words: made by the wallet, 90 bits, a 256 MiB calculation

A secured account has two recovery keys that are never stored by the wallet. Both come from the recovery words, which the code calls the passphrase, or the first does and the second is the recovery sheet of a Recovery Kit (see "Recovery: words on paper, and the Recovery Kit as an option").

The wallet makes the passphrase; nobody chooses it. Its key's public half is on the chain, where anyone can test guesses offline, so a chosen phrase is too weak. It is 90 random bits written as five pronounceable words of six letters (for example `kerine netoji nesimo vebine bipori`): each word is three syllables, each syllable one of 16 consonants (b d f g h j k l m n p r s t v z) followed by one of 4 vowels (a e i o), 6 bits per syllable. It is shown once and written on paper; it is not asked back. Words made before the change of 23 November 2026 are four, 72 bits, and are read as always: a typed passphrase may have four or five words. Reading ignores case, spacing and dashes; a word that the wallet could not have made is named, and any other typo shows as "not one of this account's recovery keys". No word list is used: no permissively licensed list (such as BIP-39 English) was on this machine (the cargo registry has none), and nothing was downloaded. The passwords the wallet makes where the device cannot lock the keys use the same syllables: four words, joined by dashes rather than spaces so that the two are not mixed up (see "No key under a password you chose alone").

The key is recomputed every time the passphrase is used (securing the account, freezing, paying above the limit, replacing a key, recovering a lost device) with Argon2id at 256 MiB of memory, 4 passes and 1 lane, from the passphrase and a salt naming the network and the account (`security-flows.mjs`, `PASSPHRASE_KDF`). The first keys of an account signed up protected are made before the account has a number, so their salt names the network and the account's identity key, the registration key, which the chain shows as `public_key` (`registrationPassphraseSalt`). On a network that takes such sign-ups, a passphrase that does not give a recovery key of the account by its number is tried by its identity key too: a mistyped passphrase there costs two calculations. New words made later name the number, as always. The second key of words only is made the same way, with the salts `secondWordsSalt` and `secondWordsRegistrationSalt`: a person who recovers with words only computes both.

On a phone or browser with little memory the calculation can fail. The wallet then says so and stops. Finish the step on a computer, in a current desktop browser. There is no lighter version: other settings would give a different key, which the account does not know. The recovery sheet needs no such calculation, but the steps that need two recovery keys (recovering a lost device) need the passphrase too. Locking and opening a Recovery Kit is one more calculation of the same size, with a salt of its own (see "Recovery: words on paper, and the Recovery Kit as an option").

## Using a second browser

A secured account adds a browser as a login with its payment key. The new browser's login key is sealed under a login password the wallet makes and shows once, before the request (the rule, below); where that browser still keeps keys of the account locked to its device, the login password is chosen and the new key is locked to the device too. "Add this browser to an account I have" first makes a key of its own for that browser, under a password the wallet makes too: it opens that browser until its login is added. The new browser shows a request (its new login key, that key's acceptance of the role, and the account's authority epoch and an expiry six hours ahead, signed by the same key) and a fingerprint of the key. The device with the payment password checks both signatures, the epoch and the expiry, shows the same fingerprint, and adds the key. A request is refused once the account's keys have changed or after six hours, so a request saved in a chat cannot bring back a key the owner removed. The request waits in its own slot on the new browser: it blocks nothing, can be withdrawn, and is dropped, never promoted, once that browser holds a live key of the account. To move the account to a new browser instead, so that the old one stops working, see "Move my account to another device".

## No key under a password you chose alone

**The rule** (since 24 November 2026; `vault.mjs`, `RULE`). The wallet never seals a key under a password a person chose, alone. Every vault record and every file it writes is sealed (i) with the device's secret and the password together (vault version 3, "Lock my keys to this device"), or (ii) under a password the wallet made, of 72 random bits, or (iii) under a key derived from a key that is itself sealed one of those ways (vault version 4). Why: a password a person chooses is the one thing that someone with a copy of this browser's storage, or of a backup file, can guess on their own computer (`AUDIT-2026-11.md`, O-3); the device's secret and a made password cannot be guessed. The rule's checks of a chosen password (`checkPasswords`) still apply where a chosen password goes with the device.

**By design, not by care.** `sealVault` itself refuses to seal a key without the device unless it is given a password `makePassword` has just made and that has sealed nothing yet (an object only `vault.mjs` can make; one made password seals one record, once, with the device or without); a text, a made password read back from what someone typed, or one used already, is refused with the rule before anything is derived. `makePassword` takes no argument: its 9 bytes come from the browser's own `crypto.getRandomValues` into an array of its own, whatever that function returns. `sealedKeys` refuses a typed password without the device. `sealing-rule.test.mjs` reads every script the page loads (`web-v4/wallet/` and `web-v4/shared/` at any depth, and the SDK files it loads from `/sdk/`), without their comments, and counts in each every mention of what seals or derives a key (`sealVault`, `sealUnder`, `makePassword`, `argon2id`, `hashwasm`, PBKDF2, scrypt, and WebCrypto's encrypt, wrapKey, deriveKey and deriveBits, in code or in quotes) and of every local name given to one of them; the counts of each module are pinned with one line of reason, and so is who imports a sealing name under which name. A module on no list that mentions one, or one more mention in a listed module, fails with the rule in its message: a module that would add a file of the payment key opened by the payment password alone stops there at merge, and `sealVault` refuses it at run time. What the guard cannot see is said in the test's header (a name built from pieces at run time, a function handed on under another name as an argument, the output of Argon2id kept as it is, a cipher written in JavaScript).

**A made password.** Four words of three syllables, the syllables of the recovery words (16 consonants, 4 vowels: 6 bits each), 18 bits a word, 72 bits, from 9 bytes of the browser's `crypto.getRandomValues` cut into twelve 6-bit values: every value is reachable and none is likelier. It is written with dashes between the words (`kerine-netoji-nesimo-vebine`), so that it is not mixed up with the five recovery words, which are written with spaces. It is read forgivingly: small or capital letters, spaces and dashes do not matter, and Argon2id is given its one canonical form (lower case, one dash between the words). The record says in its authenticated header that its password was made (`"password": "made"`, `BACKUP_FORMAT.md`): the wallet then reads what is typed that way, and says when it cannot be one ("Your login password is one the wallet made: four words of six letters, joined by dashes. Check it against what your browser saved, or what you wrote down."), before anything is derived. Guessing one at the vault's Argon2id costs about 13,600 years of a million H100 cards (`AUDIT-2026-11.md`, "Ways to close O-3", B).

**How it is shown.** A dialog of its own, "Your new passwords" (`made-dialog.mjs`, `showMade`; `index.html`, `#made-dialog`), before anything the passwords seal is kept or sent: the login password, marked "Every day", "Opens this wallet and signs you in to websites. It cannot pay. Let your browser save it, or write it down.", and in the dialog's form beside the account's name as the user name (`autocomplete="username"` and `"new-password"`), so that the browser's password manager may offer to keep it (web audit W-6 keeps the login password saveable); the payment password, marked "When you pay", "You type it at each payment. Write it down now, on another paper than your recovery words: it is shown only now. Do not let the browser save it.", in no field at all. Neither is asked back: no quiz, no tick box; "I have them" closes the dialog, and what it showed leaves the page. Only that button goes on: closed any other way (Escape, a phone's back gesture, the wallet locking), the step keeps nothing and sends nothing, forgets the keys it made, and says so: "Nothing was kept: the passwords were closed without "I have them". Start again for new ones." Where a flow opens keys to seal them again (turning the lock off, "Let the wallet make my passwords", restoring a file of a chosen password), the new records are opened with the made passwords, the device's secrets and the opened keys are wiped and the lock of the account's vaults is let go before the dialog shows; the records are then written only if this browser still keeps the account's keys as they were ("Nothing was changed: the keys of this account in this browser changed while the new passwords were shown. …").

**The person chooses** (since 25 November 2026; the owner: "up to the user to decide"). Wherever new keys of an account are made and the device can lock them (a protected sign-up, "Protect my account", "Recover my account", "Move my account here"), the form shows one choice of two ways, as two radio buttons in a group with a legend (`index.html`, `<form>-ways`, styled as the wallet's other choices: `styles.css`, `.kinds` and `.kind`), and neither is chosen beforehand: the wallet does not choose for the person (`device-lock-ui.mjs`, `wayChoice`; the words are `LOCK_WORDS.ways` of `device-lock.mjs`, the one place the page, the tests and the browser check read them from):

- the legend: "Choose how to lock your new keys" ("3. Choose how to lock your new keys" in "Recover my account", whose fields are numbered);
- "Lock them to this device": "They open with your fingerprint, face or screen lock and passwords you choose. If this device loses its key, your recovery words get you back in after the waiting period; to change devices, use “Move my account to another device”.";
- "Let the wallet make the passwords": "Nothing of this device is used. The wallet makes two passwords and shows them to you once: keep both, and type the payment password at each payment."

The fields of the chosen passwords appear only once the lock is chosen, and go, emptied, when the other way is. A form sent with no way chosen goes no further and says "First choose how to lock your new keys: to this device, or with passwords the wallet makes."; nothing is made, kept or sent, and the device is asked nothing (in "Recover my account" the fields of the recovery words are emptied, as after any step that stops). Each time a form is drawn again, neither way is chosen. Where the device cannot lock keys, nothing is shown to choose: the form says that the wallet makes the passwords ("The wallet makes your passwords and shows them to you, because this device cannot lock your keys."). Until the browser has said what it can do (a moment after the form is drawn), the form shows neither the choice nor that sentence, and a form sent then goes no further and says "One moment: checking what this device can do."; nothing is made, kept or sent. A browser that has not answered after four seconds (`WAY_ANSWER_MS`) is taken as one whose device cannot lock keys, and an answer after that changes nothing. The keyboard reaches the group with Tab and moves between the two ways with the arrow keys; "Protect my account" on Home puts the focus on the first way when the choice is shown.

## Your own randomness, added to the passwords the wallet makes

Before the wallet makes a password, it asks the person to add randomness of their own: a dialog, "Add your own randomness", with a box to move a mouse or a finger around in and a meter that fills as they do, the way a phone asks for a finger again and again until it has the whole fingerprint. Keys pressed count too, for a person who uses no pointer. When the meter is full the dialog closes by itself and the step goes on. (`entropy.mjs`, the gathering and the pool; `entropy-dialog.mjs`, the dialog; `entropy.test.mjs`.)

**The words.** "Move your mouse or your finger around inside the box, any way you like, until it is full. You can press keys too. The wallet mixes what you do into the passwords it makes, on top of this device's own randomness. Nothing of it leaves this page." The meter says "Not started yet.", then "N% full. Keep going." in steps of ten, then "Full. The wallet goes on."

**Mixed in, never instead.** A made password is still 9 bytes of the browser's own generator (`crypto.getRandomValues`), as before. What the person did (where the pointer was and when, to the finest time the browser gives, with the samples a browser merged into one event; for a key, its time only, never which key) goes through SHA-512 into 64 bytes, the pool, and 9 bytes of the pool are XORed into the 9 bytes of each password (`vault.mjs`, `makePassword`; `entropy.mjs`, `mixInto`). Bytes of a good generator XORed with anything that does not depend on them are exactly as random as they were. So nothing a person does in the box, and nothing a script could put in the pool, can make a password weaker or known; and if the browser's generator were ever weak, what the person added is what the password would still have. That is the only reason for this step: the browser's generator alone is strong, and every password was made of it alone before.

**The meter is an estimate.** Nobody can measure how unpredictable a hand is. The meter credits little (2 bits for a pointer sample that moved at least 6 pixels and came at least 4 milliseconds after the last credited one, 1 bit for a key pressed at least 25 milliseconds after the last) and asks for 256 bits: about 130 movements, a few seconds. Every sample goes into the pool, credited or not. No password's strength is claimed from the meter: a made password is 72 bits, as before.

**"Go on without this"** goes on with the browser's own randomness alone, and says so: "The wallet then uses this device's own randomness alone, which is strong too." Nobody is kept out of the wallet by a hand, a device or a screen reader that gives no movement. Closed any other way (Escape, a phone's back gesture, the wallet locking), the step ends: "Nothing was made: the box was closed before it was full. Start again." Where the page cannot draw the box (a browser without a canvas), nothing is asked.

**When it is asked.** Before anything secret is opened or made, wherever the wallet makes passwords: a sign-up, "Protect my account", "Recover my account" and "Move my account here" with "Let the wallet make the passwords" chosen (or after a device that failed); a new login or payment password and a login for a second browser, where the keys are not locked to the device; turning the lock off; "Let the wallet make my passwords"; restoring a backup file of a chosen password; an account with one key. Not where the keys are locked to the device with passwords of the person's own. What was gathered serves seven passwords (64 bytes, 9 for each, each byte used once and wiped as it is used), is forgotten after two minutes and when the wallet locks; a step that needs passwords again within that time does not ask again.

**Offline.** Neither module reaches a network, a storage, another window or another script, and a test reads their source for it. What is drawn in the box is where the pointer went, and is wiped when the dialog closes.

**Its limits.** The recovery words are not mixed this way: they come from the browser's generator alone, as before. A script running in the wallet's page sees the movements as it sees everything else. The meter's credits were chosen, not measured.

**Run so far:** unit tests (`entropy.test.mjs`: the meter, the pool, the XOR with the browser's bytes known to the test, when the person is asked, the dialog on a stand-in page from the first movement to full, with keys alone, "Go on without this", Escape, the wallet locking, a page that cannot draw). The browser check moves a real mouse through the box wherever the wallet makes passwords, and goes on without once (`campaign/lib/browser-check.mjs`, `addRandomness`, step `wallet-own-randomness`).

## Lock my keys to this device

Under Keys, a protected account can lock its login key and its payment key to the device: each then opens only with the device's own check of the person (fingerprint, face or screen lock) and the password, together. A copy of this browser's data is then worth nothing elsewhere, whatever the passwords: a password can be guessed offline, the device's secret cannot. Offered on a protected account whose login key and payment key are both in this browser and not locked, and only where the browser does not say it cannot (`device-key.mjs`, `deviceSupport`: a secure page, WebAuthn, a built-in authenticator not refused, PRF not refused). Since the rule (see "No key under a password you chose alone"), a new account's keys are locked from birth where the device can and the person chooses it (since 25 November 2026 the person chooses, and neither way is chosen beforehand); the card is turned on here for keys made before, or under passwords the wallet made where the person chose them or the device failed (the made passwords stay the passwords, typed any way, and the records keep their mark). The decisions are in `device-lock.mjs`, the card in `device-lock-ui.mjs`, the format in `vault.mjs` (version 3; `BACKUP_FORMAT.md`).

**What the card says before turning on:**

- "A copy of this browser's data will no longer open your keys, even with your passwords."
- "If this device loses that key (after a reset, or when it is removed in the device's settings), you get back in with your recovery words, and that waits the waiting period. To change devices while this one still works, move your account under Keys: nothing waits."
- "On phones, Apple or Google keep that key in your account with them and bring it to your other devices."
- "This browser then makes no backup file of your keys: a file would open with a password alone."

**How.** One key of the device per browser and site, for every account there: its id and a random user handle are kept in `timecurrency.device-key.v1`, and the device lists it under the fixed label "Time wallet", never an account's name. Each vault asks it for its own 32 bytes (WebAuthn PRF) with a salt that names the purpose (login or payment), the network, the account and the vault's public key, so that one account's answer opens nothing of another's, nor of another network (web audit, K-8). The vault key is HKDF-SHA-256 of the Argon2id output of the password and those 32 bytes together; the record's header names the device's key and is the authenticated data. A version-3 record that comes without the device's answer is refused before anything is derived, a record changed to look like version 2 does not open, and a wallet from before version 3 refuses it. `vault.mjs` asks no device: the answer comes in as an argument. Every request is discoverable (no key named in it), requires the person's check and checks the UV flag; an answer from another key than the one the records name is refused (`device-key.mjs` outcome 7).

**How often the device asks.** Before each request the page says "Your device will now ask for your fingerprint, face or screen lock." Opening the wallet: once (going on with a kept sign-up: once, and the account then opens with the same login key, no second request). A payment or any action with the payment password: once. Locking keys from birth (a sign-up, "Protect my account", "Recover my account") and turning on: twice with the device's key already in use by another account of this browser, three times with a new one ("…, two or three times."). Turning off: once. A new login password, a new login key after this browser's was removed, or a new payment password (with the new login key): twice (the payment key in one request, the new keys in another). A login asked for a second browser while that browser's keys are locked: once. Moving the account to another device: on the new device two or three times, as a sign-up; on the old device once, for the payment key (see "Move my account to another device"). A password typed wrong costs no new request: the device's answer is held in the page's memory until the key opens, for at most 60 seconds, and wiped at once on Lock, when the form changes and when the page hides.

**Turning on** needs both passwords and the device. Both passwords are checked first (nothing is asked of the device for a wrong one). Then the device's key (kept, or made), one request for both answers, both keys sealed, and a SECOND request: both new records must open with its answers before anything is replaced. The two places are then replaced together through a journal (`timecurrency.vault-lock.v1:`): a page closed half way leaves either both new records, which the next start puts in place, or one, which goes. A device that refuses, an answer that differs the second time, a wrong password: nothing changed, in those words. Refused while a change of keys from this browser waits for the network, or a login is asked of another device.

**Turning off** needs the device and both passwords, and says first what changes: "The wallet then makes new passwords for your keys and shows them to you once. Your passwords of now stop opening them, and the backup file is offered again." One request; both keys sealed under two passwords the wallet makes (the rule) and opened again with them; the dialog "Your new passwords" shows them, and only once "I have them" was pressed are both places replaced, the same way: "Your keys are no longer locked to this device. They open with the passwords the wallet just made."

**While it is on, every place a vault is written:**

| Place | While the lock is on |
|---|---|
| Login place, payment place | Version 3, both. |
| A new login password, a new payment password (keys kept aside until the network has them, then moved into the places) | Sealed with the device too, both new keys in one request. |
| A login asked of another device from this browser | Sealed with the device too. |
| The swap journal | Version 3 on turning on, under the passwords the wallet made on turning off; there only for the moment of the swap. |
| "Recover my account" on this browser | The way back when the device lost its key. The review says "Its keys of @name locked to this device are replaced by the new ones." The new keys follow the rule: locked to this device from birth where it can (a new device key when only the replaced records used the kept one), else under passwords the wallet makes. |
| A backup file | Not made: export is not offered, and the backup card says "Your keys are locked to this device, so this browser makes no backup file of them: a file would open with a password alone. To change devices while this one still works, use “Move my account to another device”, below. If this device loses its key, recover your account with your recovery words." Importing a file for an account whose keys here are locked is refused; a file can never carry a locked key. |
| A sign-up | Time Vault keeps the generated role passwords behind a master password and device verification. Manual signup also offers device locking of the wallet keys (see "Signing up protected"). |

**When the device's key is gone.** Opening the wallet then ends with one of three sentences and the button "Recover my account", which opens the recovery with the name filled in:

- another key of the device answered: "This device no longer has the key your keys here are locked to, or it answered with another one. Your passwords alone cannot open them. Recover your account with your recovery words (it waits the waiting period), or use it on another device where it is open."
- the request was cancelled or timed out (a device that no longer has any key for the site looks the same to a page): "Nothing was opened: the device did not give its key. Try again. If this device no longer has that key (after a reset, or when it was removed in its settings), recover your account with your recovery words."
- a browser that cannot ask the device (a copy of the data on another machine): "This browser cannot use this device's fingerprint, face or screen lock, and your keys here are locked to it. Open the wallet in the browser where you locked them, or recover your account with your recovery words."

**Which site the key belongs to.** Requests leave `rp.id` out, so the key belongs to the wallet's exact host. A wallet reachable under two addresses (the test network's names and an older address kept working) has two separate locks: each address has its own storage and its own device key, a lock turned on under one is not seen under the other, and a person who uses both sees "Time wallet" twice in the device's list. Moving the wallet to another address leaves the locked keys behind, as it leaves every vault: the way back is the recovery words.

**What the lock does not protect.** A script running in the wallet's page (it can ask the device as the page does, and read what the person types); whoever serves the page (the same); a person at the unlocked device who passes the device's check; copies of the storage made before the lock was turned on (they open with the passwords of then: made by the wallet since the rule, or chosen before it); old values on the disk that the browser's storage engine has not yet overwritten. Synced passkeys are taken, and said: on a phone the key lives in the person's Apple or Google account and comes to their other devices, which is also what brings a lock back after a phone is replaced.

**Run so far:** unit tests with a fake authenticator (`device-lock.test.mjs`: every place above, a copied storage without the device, a record changed to look like version 2, turning on stopped at each step, turning off, the key gone, two accounts, the locked store through on and off; `security-review-ui.test.mjs`: a new login password and a payment with the lock on, "Protect my account" and "Recover my account" sent before the browser said what the device can do, with a browser that never says, with no way chosen, locked from birth, under the passwords the wallet makes, and with a device that fails after the lock was chosen; `sealing-rule.test.mjs`: keys locked from birth, each failure of the device falling back to made passwords; `signup.test.mjs`: a sign-up sent before the browser said what the device can do, with a browser that never says, with no way chosen, locked from birth, under the passwords the wallet makes, with the way changed before sending, kept and gone on with after a reload either way, and with a device that cancels its second request). The browser check ran its four steps with Chrome's virtual authenticator on 24 November (turning the lock on); since the rule it locks the second account from birth instead ("wallet-second", with the authenticator added before the sign-up; since 25 November it first sends the form with no way chosen, which must refuse, then chooses the lock: that form of the step has not run yet), then `device-lock-on` reads that, `device-lock-unlock-pay`, `device-lock-copy`, and `device-lock-off` with the passwords the wallet makes: in that form they passed on 24 November on the trial network `tc-v5-trial18` (`r1124222546`, 50 of 50; `r1124223528`, 54 of 54) and on the public test network (`r1124224401`, 54 of 54), in Chrome 158 with its virtual authenticator. Nothing was tried on a real phone, a real Windows Hello or Touch ID, or any browser but Chrome (no Safari, no Firefox).

## Move my account to another device

For a new phone or computer, while the old one still works. The authority of the account moves; keys never travel. The new device makes its own login key and payment key. The old device signs one replacement of the account's keys that puts them in. Nothing waits: the replacement is in force in the block that takes it. Only a protected account moves this way, and only from a browser that holds both its login key and its payment key. The decisions are in `device-move.mjs`, the pages in `move-ui.mjs`, the engine's side in `v5/tests/security.rs` (a root replacement, kind 73).

**On the new device** (the start screen, "Other ways in", "Move my account here"):

1. The name. The wallet reads the account in a state it verified. A name without an account is refused.
2. The wallet asks the browser whether what the page keeps will last (`navigator.storage`: `persisted()`, then `persist()`; `device-move.mjs`, `lastingStorage`). Where the browser does not say yes (a private window, or a browser that clears storage when it needs room), a review titled "This browser may not keep your new keys" says what can happen (below), and the person goes on or stops.
3. The new keys are made by the rule, as a sign-up makes them (`device-lock.mjs`, `newKeysByRule`). Where this device can lock keys, the person chooses one of the two ways, neither chosen beforehand (see "The person chooses" under "No key under a password you chose alone"; sent with none chosen, the form says so, and nothing is asked, made or kept): locked to this device from birth with new passwords chosen here, whose fields appear once the lock is chosen; or under two passwords the wallet makes, shown in "Your new passwords" before anything is kept. Where it cannot, no choice is shown and the wallet makes them. That dialog goes on only with "I have them": closed any other way (Escape, a back gesture, the wallet locking), nothing is kept, the keys made are forgotten, no request is shown, and the wallet says "Nothing was kept: the passwords were closed without "I have them". Start again for new ones." No lock of the vault is held and no key of the account is open while it is shown. Each record must then give a key that signs for it: keys under made passwords are opened again with them; keys locked from birth were opened already by the device's second answer, so the device is not asked a third time. Both records are written, read back, and only then is a request shown.
4. The request, in one field with a Copy button ("It holds no secret."), and the code, in four groups of four. One sentence says what to do with each: paste the request under Keys on the other device; type the code there, nowhere else, and never send it to anyone.
5. While the request waits, the page reads the network every ten seconds and says what it found in one sentence: waiting (until when the request works), stale (the account's keys changed since the request was made: withdraw it and make a new one), closing (expired, but a move signed just before can still arrive until a given time), withdrawn, the move landed, landed with other keys on the account ("mixed"), damaged (this browser cannot read what it kept: "Do not finish the move on your other device"), expired (its keys forgotten), or unread (the network cannot be read: nothing changes). "Withdraw" stays while the request could still be used. Once no request can be shown any more, the form for a new one is there.
6. When the move lands, the account opens as after a sign-up, with the new login key the page still holds: "Your account is here now. The keys of your other device no longer work." After a reload it opens with the new login password: "Your account is here now. Open @name with your new login password." A request that waits is shown again when the page starts, before any unlock.

**On the old device** (Keys, the card "Move my account to another device", on a protected account whose login key and payment key are both in this browser, locked to the device or not):

1. The request is pasted and checked (below). What was checked is said in sentences built from what the wallet read, never from the request's own text: the account moves; how many payment and login keys stop working at once, this device's included; the recovery words, the balance and the daily limit stay; which waiting payments, or which waiting raise of the daily limit, are cancelled; until when the request works.
2. Above the field of the code: "Type only the code your own new device shows you right now: if anyone sent you this code or this request, stop, because whoever made the request would get your account." A wrong code stops here, before any password or recovery word is asked.
3. The payment password (and the device, where the keys here are locked), and the recovery words or a printed recovery sheet.
4. A review, "Move my account to another device": at once, the account works on the other device and this device's keys stop working; websites ask you to sign in again; what is dropped (waiting payments, a waiting raise of the daily limit), or "Nothing is waiting."; what is kept; the fee.
5. The replacement goes through the wallet's path for security actions. Just before it leaves, a note of what was signed is kept, so that a page closed meanwhile still finishes. After the receipt the wallet reads the network until it shows the move on the account, then forgets this browser's keys of the account and ends on the locked start screen: "@name is on your other device now. This browser forgot its keys, and they no longer work."
6. The page never shows the code it expects, and never shows the request back.

**What the old device checks** (`MOVE_CHECKS`, in this order, and all of it again on the state it signs on): the text (at most 4096 characters, JSON, exactly the fields of a move request, its type and version); every field well formed; the network; the account, by number and by name; the account's authority epoch; the expiry (not passed, and at most an hour and five minutes ahead); two different keys; neither key a key of the account already; the account protected; at most two proofs needed; no replacement of its keys waiting; not frozen; the network fee in the account; each key's acceptance of its role and each key's signature over the request; then the code the person typed, before any secret is asked.

**What never leaves a device.** No private key, password, recovery word or device secret leaves either device. The request is public data only: the network, the account, its authority epoch, an expiry an hour ahead, the two new public keys, each key's acceptance of its role, and each key's signature over all of that. The code is shown on the new device and typed on the old one; neither device sends it anywhere. The move on the chain carries the two public keys and their acceptances, approved by the payment key and one recovery key.

**The code.** The first 80 bits of SHA-256 over the request's fields, written as 16 characters of the recovery sheet's alphabet. It protects against a request swapped on its way: someone who changes the request in a clipboard, a chat or a shared note to name their own keys cannot give it the code the person reads on their own new device (making a request of other keys with a given code takes about 2^80 tries), and nothing is signed. It does not say who made a request: anyone who holds a request can work its code out. A person who is sent a request and a code by someone else, and types them, gives that person the account; the sentence above the field says so. Nor does it protect against a script running in the wallet's page, or whoever serves the page, on either device.

**Storage that may not last.** Until the move lands and the new device puts its keys in place, the new keys exist only in that browser's storage. If the browser deletes it before then (a private window closed, storage cleared for room), nobody holds the new keys once the move lands, and the account needs its recovery words and its waiting period. That is why the browser is asked first, and the person told when it does not say yes.

**Open the new device again soon.** It puts its new keys in place the next time it reads the network (every ten seconds while the page is open). Until then they wait in places kept aside for the move. The old device forgets its keys once it sees the move; a page closed before that forgets them when it next starts.

**Withdrawing.** "Withdraw" means the other device can no longer approve the request. The new device keeps its keys until a move signed before can no longer land: the request's expiry plus the ten minutes the approvals are dated ahead (`moveWindowEnd`). Then it forgets them.

**How often the device asks.** The new device, keys locked from birth: two or three times, as a sign-up (the device's key made where none is in use, one request that seals both keys, one that must open both again); the keys that last request opened are the ones checked, so there is no fourth. Under passwords the wallet makes: never. The old device: once, for the payment key, where its keys are locked; else never.

**Limits.**

- The engine takes a move while the account is frozen, or while a replacement asked for by two recovery keys waits, and ends both. The wallet refuses both and says why: that refusal is the wallet's, not the engine's. Whoever holds the payment password and a recovery key can replace the keys in any case.
- A signed move lands until its approvals expire, even if the account's login keys change in between: that moves the authority epoch, not the security version the approvals name. This is why a withdrawn request keeps the new device's keys until then.
- An account that needs more than two proofs for a change of its keys does not move this way (a move gives the payment key and one recovery key). The wallet says so when the request is checked.
- After a move the account may hold other payment or login keys than the new device's (a key added meanwhile). Where it holds the new login key, the new device opens the account (with the key the page held, or with the new login password) and says to look at the keys under Keys. Where it holds only the new payment key, no key of this browser opens the account: the new device says so ("The move arrived, but @name does not have the login key this browser made for it, so this browser cannot open the account. …") and points to the recovery words, rather than showing an unlock that could only fail. The old device keeps its keys while one of them is still on the account.
- A new device that cannot read a record it kept says so and changes nothing. If the move landed, it asks the person to leave the browser as it is and try again later, and points to the recovery words if it stays so.
- The Copy button needs the browser's clipboard; the request can also be selected in its field and copied by hand.

**Run so far:** unit tests (`device-move.test.mjs`; `move-ui.test.mjs`: both devices with the real `security-ui.mjs`, the stand-in chain and a fake authenticator, the new device's form sent before its browser said what the device can do, with a browser that never says, with no way chosen, the lock chosen after the other way, the passwords the wallet makes chosen after the lock, and a device that fails after the lock was chosen, a reload at each step, the wallet locked while the made passwords are shown, a wrong code, a wrong recovery word, the old device opened again after the move, both mixed cases; `move-page.test.mjs`: the new device through the real `app.js` and its dialog, closed by a script and by Escape; every record the new device keeps is proven by opening it, `tests/legacy-vault.mjs`, `provenSeals`) and the engine's tests (`v5/tests/security.rs`: the move and its fixture, a freeze and a waiting replacement, the epoch). The browser check has four steps for it at the end of its run (`move-request`, `move-old-device`, `move-landed`, `move-old-reopened`), which passed on 24 November in Chrome 158 on the trial network `tc-v5-trial18` (`r1124223528`, 54 of 54) and on the public test network (`r1124224401`, 54 of 54): the request and its code on a fresh browser with Chrome's virtual authenticator, its new keys locked from birth (since 25 November `move-request` chooses the lock in the form's choice first; that has not run yet); on the old browser, whose keys were then under passwords the wallet made, a wrong code refused before any secret was asked, then the move sent and the keys forgotten; the network holding the two new keys and neither old one; the old browser, loaded again, opening nothing with its old login password. Covered by stand-ins only: an old device whose keys are locked to it, a new device under made passwords, the Copy button, the mixed and the damaged stages, real eviction of storage, two real tabs. Not tried: the new device's reading every ten seconds in a tab in the background. Nothing on a real phone, a real Windows Hello or Touch ID, or in a browser other than Chrome (no Safari, no Firefox).

## Validator operator

More, Validator operator (`pos-ui.mjs`) registers the account's validator from the registration text that the join script prints (`testnet/join.sh`), adds to its bond, takes the bond out and retires it. A bond taken out is listed there with "Withdraw" once its 14 days are over, as it is under Staking. An account with one key signs each of these with this browser's key, and its screen and its reviews are as they were.

**A protected account** signs each with its payment key, unlocked for that one action with the payment password typed on the screen ("Payment password", shown only for a protected account; `security-ui.mjs`, `stakeAction`). What the engine allows it, read on 2026-11-23 (`v5/src/engine.rs`, `apply`, the branch of kinds 5 to 7 and 17 to 24; `v5/src/security.rs`, `Security::spend`):

- Only a key that may spend signs these actions: the payment key. The login key and the recovery keys cannot ("spending device required").
- What an action takes out of the balance, fee included, is charged to the daily limit: the bond and the fee of a registration, the amount and the fee of an addition to the bond, the fee of taking the bond out and of retiring. The bond of a registration is the network's least bond (`pos/src/lib.rs`, `Profile::min_self_bond`): one second on a laboratory network such as the test network, a day of seconds (86,400) on a production one. The review of "Register my validator" names it for every account, protected or not ("1 seconds, the network's least bond, taken from your balance now"; web audit, W-7), and a network whose least bond the wallet does not know gets no review. A withdrawal brings the seconds back, and the engine charges it only what it brings back less than the fee.
- Each that is charged anything is refused while the account is frozen, and above what is left of the day's limit. None of it waits, and no recovery paper pays it: what waits is a payment (kind 75), and the operation the payment key signs with a recovery paper pays transfers only (`Operation::Pay`). The limit itself can be raised on Home: that waits the waiting period, or applies at once with a recovery paper, and either way what can be used fills up under the new limit over 24 hours.

**What the person is told** (`protection.mjs`, `validatorLimit`; read on the clock of the waits):

- Before the review, when it cannot be signed now, and nothing is unlocked or signed: "Your account is frozen, so registering a validator is refused until the freeze ends. See Home." (and "adding to a validator's bond", "taking a validator's bond out", "retiring a validator"); "The amount and the fee, 150.001 seconds, are more than the 100 seconds left of your daily limit today, and the network lets none of it wait or be paid with a recovery paper: try again after …, when enough of it has come back."; and, above the limit itself, "The bond and the fee, 86400.001 seconds, are more than your daily limit of 600 seconds, and the network lets none of it wait or be paid with a recovery paper: raise the limit on Home to at least 86401 seconds (it waits 48 hours, or applies at once with a recovery paper), then try again once it has filled up, within a day."
- In the review, two lines more: "Daily limit", "The bond and the fee, 1.001 seconds, count against your daily limit: 600 seconds is left today, 598.999 seconds after this." (or "The amount and the fee, …", "The fee, …, counts against …"; for a withdrawal "The seconds come back to your balance: nothing of your daily limit is used, unless less than the fee comes back."), and "Signed by", "Your payment key, with your payment password".
- Unbonding and withdrawing under Staking get the same two lines; their refusal while frozen is said as before.

**What the wallet asks beyond the engine.** A withdrawal is checked as Staking always checked it: its fee must fit what is left, and the account must not be frozen. The engine would take a withdrawal of a frozen account that brings back more than the fee; the wallet does not sign it until the freeze ends.

**What does not apply here.** No name is typed or shown: the registration text names the account by its number and the validator by its key, so the review compares no name with the names this account has paid (`names.mjs`). A registration text is taken only for this account and this network, as before.

**On a production network** a registration needs a daily limit above a day of seconds, and that much left of it: a protected account with the suggested 600 seconds is told to raise its limit first. On the test network the bond is one second.

**On a network, in Chrome only.** `validator-protected.test.mjs` runs the real page on a stand-in chain that applies the engine's rules as read above (its head says which lines), for a protected account and for an account with one key. On 23 November the browser check ("wallet-validator-protected") registered a validator from a protected account in Chrome 158, on laboratory networks with protected sign-up (campaigns `tc-v5-final8` and `tc-v5-final9`): the review said what the bond and the fee take of the daily limit, the bond was taken from the balance and from the limit and taken out again with the payment password, and a text made for another account was refused in words. The refusals while frozen and above what is left of the limit ran on the stand-in chain only.

## Validator signal

The owner account of a validator supports a change of the network's rules, or withdraws its support, from the screen "Validator signal" under More (`upgrade-signal-ui.mjs`, `upgrade-signal.mjs`; the signature is made in `signing.mjs`). The rules are in `../../docs/design/upgrade-mechanism.md`; the action is kind 80, built by the SDK (`../../client-v4/README.md`, "A validator's upgrade signal").

**When the screen is there.** Its row under More is shown only while proven state holds a validator candidate of the unlocked account that is not archived. Nothing a gateway says brings it up.

**What it shows.** What the screen says about the network, the plans and your validator is read with proofs the wallet verified itself. The screen shows nothing a gateway answered, neither as a sentence of its own nor as a quotation:

- the version in force, the scheduled plan and its start in UTC, and whether a validator safety hold is on;
- your validator's votes, its signal, when it was sent, and when you may change it again (one change every 10 minutes);
- whether your account is frozen or in a recovery: its signal then does not count, and it can neither signal nor withdraw;
- every plan that validators of the current validator sets support, with the hash in eight groups, its supporters, and its share of the votes against four fifths (to be scheduled) and three quarters (to stay scheduled). The share is the lowest over the validator set in force and the announced ones, as the network counts it, and is rounded down;
- every validator with its votes and its signal. A plan is named there by its whole hash, in groups: two hashes that begin and end alike are two plans.

**What one proof cannot hold.** Whether another validator's signal counts depends on its owner account (frozen, or in a recovery) and, during a safety hold, on which validators are jailed. A proof holds eight accounts and sixteen validators. The wallet reads the rest in further proofs, a few blocks after the first, and says from which blocks it read. Where a read failed, the count is one the network may not share, in either direction. A signal that does not count may have been counted: the share shown is then too high. During a safety hold a jailed validator that does not support the plan may still be in the total: the share shown is then too low. The screen then states no threshold as reached or missed. It says what proven state itself says (scheduled or not), the rule, and that it could not check the count. Every sentence that gives that share carries the same caveat: the list of plans, the line under the form, the review and the result. With a hundred validators the status is more than a dozen requests, so it is not read at every refresh of the wallet. It is read when the screen appears, when it is opened, on "Read again from proven state", and whenever the account's action number has moved since the last reading. Before anything is signed it is read again.

**Supporting a plan.** You type or paste the version, the specification hash and the start time from the release announcement of the software your validator runs.

- The wallet fills in none of the three, ever: not from the scheduled plan, not from what other validators signalled, not from the gateway, not from an address or from storage. The fields are emptied once a signal has been signed and when the wallet locks.
- The start time is the number the announcement gives: microseconds of network time. The wallet shows it as a date in UTC before you sign. Compare both with the announcement.
- The wallet compares what you typed with proven state and says which it is: the scheduled plan, a plan that validators support (and with how much of the votes), or a plan nobody has signalled. If a proven plan agrees with yours in version and start time and differs in the hash, the review shows a warning with that plan's hash beside yours. A plan with another start time or another version is another plan and brings no warning: whoever signals could otherwise put a warning into every owner's review.
- You tick that you checked your validator's node: it must run a release that implements this version with this hash, or it stops at the start. The node's own status lists what its program implements, each version with its hash (`upgrade.implemented`). On the validator's host it is the answer to the query `/status` of the node's local RPC (`http://127.0.0.1:26657/abci_query?path="/status"`, whose `response.value` is that status in base64); `node deploy/operator.mjs probe SETTINGS` makes the same query and prints the versions without their hashes. This sentence is from reading the kit's code; the query was not run against a live node here.

**Why you type the hash, and there is no button "support this plan".** The hash decides which rules the network will run. A signal in proven state proves that some validator sent it, not that its plan is the right one: any owner of a validator can signal any plan, and a button would put that plan one click away for every other owner. Comparing 64 characters by eye fails in the middle of the string. So the source is the release announcement, which the gateway does not control, and the comparison is the wallet's, over all 64 characters. Proven hashes are shown in groups of eight and the form takes a hash only as one run of 64 characters, so a hash copied from the proven lists of this page is refused, with the reason. An owner who removes the spaces by hand can still paste it: the form stops a careless copy, not a deliberate one. A gateway cannot put a hash on this screen: the line under the form takes only refusals the wallet states itself, and where the gateway refused a request the screen says one fixed sentence in place of the gateway's answer.

**Before you sign,** the review states: the version; the hash; the start in UTC and as the number you typed; that every program that does not implement the version with exactly these rules stops at the start; that your own node must run a release that implements it; your votes; what the plan has now; any warning; when you can change the signal again; which key signs; the fee. The review of this screen has a box to tick ("I compared the version, all eight groups of the hash and the start date with the release announcement"). The button that signs is disabled until it is ticked, and a review confirmed without the tick counts as cancelled. The withdrawal and the discard have a box of their own. While one step of the screen runs, a second one does not start: a key held down on the form opens one review.

**Which key signs.** A key of the account that may spend. For an account with one key that is this browser's key. For a secured account it is the payment key, unlocked with the payment password for this one action; the login key and the recovery keys cannot signal. The fee counts against the daily limit.

**Afterwards** the wallet waits for a receipt that it verifies itself, then reads proven state of that block and shows your validator's entry and the plan's share from it. If proven state does not show what was signed, it says so.

**Withdrawing** is a button of its own. Its review names the plan and says what the withdrawal does to a scheduled plan.

**A signal you did not send.** Only a signed action of the account creates or changes its signal. The wallet remembers, in this browser, what proven state last showed for the account and which signals were signed or discarded here (`localStorage`, key `timecurrency.signal.v1:<network id>:<account id>`; nothing secret). At every reading it compares.

- The first reading of an account in a browser is taken as it is: there is nothing to compare it with.
- A change the wallet signed itself, or still holds as pending, says nothing.
- Any other change is told at once in the notice, and a warning stands above the status for as long as proven state shows that entry. It names the plan with its whole hash and the date, and says what to do. If you sent it from another device or another wallet, nothing is wrong. If you did not, someone else holds a key that may spend: on a secured account freeze it on Home (the switch "Freeze" under Protection), then make a new payment password under Keys ("Change something", "New payment password"); on an account with plain keys remove every key you do not know under More, Devices. Then withdraw the signal, or signal again, once the network takes a change (one every 10 minutes; a frozen account can do neither).
- An entry that is gone was dropped by the network (its start came closer than the notice, its version is no longer the next one, or the validator was retired): said once, not as a warning.
- The wallet only sees this while it is open. The alert watch (`../../deploy/watch/README.md`) tells the owner of a watched account by its own channel.

**Refusals.** Before anything is signed the wallet applies the network's rules to proven state and says what to do:

- too early to change again: when you may;
- a start that is not far enough ahead (21 days, and 30 seconds for the node) or too far (120 days): the earliest or latest date as of now, and that the start is never chosen by one owner;
- a version that is not the next one: which one is;
- a frozen account, or one in a recovery: until when, and where to lift it;
- a plan your validator already supports, or a withdrawal with nothing to withdraw;
- less than the fee in the account, or, for a secured account, less than the fee left of the daily limit.

**A signed signal without a receipt.** A refusal from the gateway can mean that the node did not take the signal, or only that the gateway did not serve its receipt. The wallet does not go by the gateway's words. It reads proven state for a few blocks:

- if the account's action number has moved and proven state shows the signal, it says that the signal was included and that it holds no receipt for it;
- if the action number has moved and proven state shows something else, it says that another action of the account is in a block in the signal's place, and, if proven state shows a signal you discarded earlier, that it was that one;
- if nothing moved, it applies the rules to proven state again and names the rule that refuses the signal now, or says that the node did not take it (next paragraph);
- if no status could be read with a proof after signing, it says only that: it does not know whether the signal reached the network, and it claims nothing about proven state or about the node.

In each case the signed signal stays pending until Activity has checked or cleared it.

**A node that does not take the signal.** A node admits a signal of support only if its own program implements that version with that hash. Proven state does not show which program a node runs, so the wallet learns this only when the node refuses. It then says, in its own words, to check the hash against the announcement and to use a gateway in front of a node that runs the release. What the gateway answered is not shown.

- The signed signal stays pending, like every signed action. It stays valid until another action of the account is in a block. Signing another action does not end it: the gateway has the bytes of both, and whoever has them can have either one included.
- Send the same bytes again from Activity ("Retry the exact signed action") once the gateway's node runs the release.
- To end it for certain, use "Cancel pending action" in Activity, where this browser's key may manage the account: it puts another action of the account in a block. A secured account's login key cannot; there the signal ends only with the next action of the account that is in a block.
- Or discard it in the screen, to sign something else. The review of the discard says that discarding does not make the bytes invalid, and until when they stay valid. The wallet remembers what you discarded. If proven state later shows the discarded signal, the screen says that it was included after all, and to withdraw it if you do not want it.
- In release 1 no program implements a version 5, so every node refuses every signal of support this way. A withdrawal is not subject to that policy.

**Not verified.** These screens were run on a fake page with a mock chain (`upgrade-signal.test.mjs`, the reviewer's `review-signal.test.mjs`), and the real `app.js` was run on a stand-in page with a stand-in for the compiled verifier (`app.test.mjs`; which key may sign what is tested in `signing.test.mjs`), never in a browser, and no signal has been sent from them to a live network. The bytes of the action are compared with signals the engine accepted (`../../client-v4/upgrade-signal.test.mjs`). A person must check in a browser:

1. The screen appears for the owner account of a validator and for no other account, and switching to and from it works.
2. The checkbox stands beside its label, can be ticked, and is empty again after every attempt (`styles.css` has one new rule for it).
3. The review dialog with its twelve or more long lines can be read and scrolled on a phone, and the hash in groups wraps without losing a group.
4. Pasting a 64-character hash into the field keeps all of it; a phone keyboard does not capitalize or correct it.
5. The list of plans and the list of validators can be read with five validators and with a hundred.
6. On a secured account the payment password field is shown, is not filled by the browser, and is empty after the attempt.
7. The first reading when the screen appears, its failure text, and "Read again from proven state" while the wallet is busy.
8. A signal that the node refused: the text, the pending card, the discard, and "Retry the exact signed action" in Activity.
9. Locking and unlocking: the screen, the form and the result are empty again.
10. The review of a signal: when it opens, the focus is on the box and not on "Sign the signal"; "Sign the signal" is greyed until the box is ticked; pressing Enter with the box unticked does nothing; the box is empty again in the next review. Then hold Enter down in the form with all fields filled: one review opens and nothing is signed. The same for "Withdraw support" and "Discard" pressed with a held Enter. A review of another screen (a payment) has no box and works as before.
11. A signal sent with another key of the account while the wallet is open: within a few seconds the notice shows the warning and it stands above the status. Close and open the wallet: the warning is still there; after your own withdrawal it is gone.
12. A signal discarded and then included by whoever had its bytes: the screen says so after the next reading.
13. Trigger a refusal on another screen. Check that the wallet shows its own explanation and next step, including checking Activity when the user already approved an action.
14. On a network whose nodes run a rehearsal build with a version 5: a signal of support from the screen is included, the plan is scheduled at four fifths, and a withdrawal is included.

## A test network that was reset

A test network can be replaced by a new chain under the same addresses (`testnet/README.md`, "Resetting a network"). The wallet keeps all it stores under the network's id: the vaults of its accounts, their activity, the names they paid, the checkpoint of its light client. After a reset that stays in the browser under the old id, and opens nothing (`reset.mjs`).

- **The start screen** of a browser that keeps accounts of another network and none of the one the site serves now says, above the form: "The test network was reset. Your old account @alice and its test seconds no longer exist. Create a new account. This browser still keeps the old keys; they open nothing now." One button, "Remove the old account from this browser", opens a review ("Remove the old test network from this browser": the accounts, how many entries go, that everything of the network that runs now stays, that it cannot be undone) and then removes every entry of the wallet kept under another network's id, and nothing else: no entry of the network the site serves, and nothing that is not the wallet's. "Removed. This browser keeps nothing of the test network before its reset."
- **"Open my account"** with a name of the old network says "The test network was reset: @alice and its test seconds no longer exist. Create a new account.", and with any other name "The test network was reset: the accounts this browser kept are of the old one, and no longer exist. Create a new account." Not "No encrypted vault …, Choose New device", which stays for a browser that keeps no account of an old network.
- **A page that is open** during the reset fails every verification. After a failure (the reading every five seconds, a button, opening an account) it reads the site's trust file again, at most once every half minute; when that names another network, the notice says "The test network was reset. Reload this page." in place of the failure, and nothing can be signed. A failure while the trust file names the same network is said as before ("Verification paused. …").
- What this does not know: a reset is read from the trust file of the site, which the site serves with the page itself. A site that serves another network's trust file serves another page too; nothing here adds to what it could do. The old keys are removed only when the person presses the button and confirms.

The explorer says the same in place of a failed check (`web-v4/explorer/check.mjs`, `network_reset`), and the demo blog keeps a payment it could not get confirmed with the network it was made on, so that a new account with the number of an old one never settles a payment of the chain that is gone (`web-v4/merchant/app.js`).

**Not run in a browser.** `network-reset.test.mjs` runs the real page on a stand-in site whose network is replaced; `shop-network.test.mjs` the blog's page, and `web-v4/explorer/network-reset.test.mjs` the explorer's check.

## A busy network

A service in front of the network (the test network's proxy, the gateway) may refuse a request for load: status 429 (too many requests from this caller) or 503 (busy), with `Retry-After` in whole seconds. The wallet tells such an answer by its status alone, before it reads anything of it, and never shows its body (`busy.mjs`; the SDK, `client-v4/time-sdk.js`, `BUSY`). An older or other service that answers a plain-text 429 or 503, with or without `Retry-After`, is treated the same.

- **Reads wait and ask again by themselves.** The wait is the `Retry-After`, held to 1 to 30 seconds, or, without one, 2, 4, 8 and 16 seconds and then 30; each wait is made up to a quarter longer, never shorter, so that many browsers do not ask again at the same moment. A read is sent 4 times at most within 20 seconds. While one request of a kind (a route) waits, no other of that kind goes out, and the same read asked twice is sent once. The reading every five seconds skips its turn while one runs. An answer after a wait ends the wait for every request of that kind.
- **The header says it, quietly.** "The network is busy. Trying again…" (before the first answer: "Connecting… The network is busy. Trying again…"), from a busy answer until the wait it asked for is over and five more seconds, or until an answer comes. A line that says signing is off is never covered by it.
- **Signing stays on.** A busy answer is no finding about the network: what was verified stays verified, and the rule for how long is the one it always was (every action reads a verified state again before it signs; the light client's checkpoint is good for seven days). A read that truly fails (a proof that does not verify, a block of another chain) still turns signing off with "Verification paused".
- **A signed action is sent again as it is.** The same bytes, never a new signature and never a second review: for 30 seconds, at most 10 sends. The signed action is kept in this browser before it is sent (the pending record). If the network stays busy, the notice says "The network is busy, so this signed action was not sent yet. It is kept in this browser, and nothing was signed again. Send the same action in a moment: under Activity, “Retry the exact signed action”." An action stopped by a busy answer before anything was signed says "The network is busy, so this could not be done now. Nothing was signed or sent. Try again in a moment."
- **An action that protects the account** (a freeze, a cancellation, new recovery words, finishing a replacement) is sent again for two minutes. If the network stays busy, an urgent notice comes into view with its button in focus: "The network is busy, and “Freeze account” could not be sent yet. It is signed and kept in this browser. Press “Try again” to send the same action now." "Try again" sends the kept bytes. On an account without the Protection card, "Cancel recovery" and "Cancel pending action" are sent again for two minutes too; they keep a place of their own, so a busy end says "The network is busy, so this signed cancellation was not sent yet. It is kept in this browser, and nothing was signed again. Press the same button again in a moment: it sends the same action." (its review is shown again; the bytes are the kept ones).
- **The start** keeps "Connecting…" with the busy line and the quiet notice "The network is busy. The wallet keeps asking and is ready as soon as it answers. Nothing needs doing.", asks again after each wait without end, and becomes ready when the network answers, without a reload.
- **Opening an account.** A busy answer while an account opens is waited for. If the first reading stays busy, the account stays open and the notice says "The network is busy. Your account opens as soon as it answers: this browser keeps asking. Nothing needs doing."; the first reading that is answered opens it. If the network stays busy before the account could be read at all, the wallet locks again and says "The network is busy, so your account could not be opened now. Nothing was changed. Open it again in a moment." (a sign-up: "The network is busy, so creating your account could not go on now. Everything is kept in this browser: open your account again in a moment, and it goes on from what the network shows."). Other tabs are not locked.
- **The services the wallet asks itself.** The trust file, the test faucet's question and the registration service's question wait by the same rule. A request for test time and a registration are sent once: a busy answer to them may come after the service acted. The faucet's and the registration service's own refusals in their own form (`{"error": {"code": …}}`: a limit of a day, "not giving now") are read as before, because a browser does not let a page read the `Retry-After` of another origin. The words: "The test faucet is busy. Nothing was asked. Try again in a minute.", "The test faucet is busy, and this browser did not ask it again. If it sent test time anyway, your balance shows it in a moment. Otherwise try again in a minute.", "The registration service is busy. Nothing more was sent. Open your account again in a moment: the wallet goes on from what the network shows." The alert service's requests are asked again (a subscription is its endpoint, a challenge is new each time); one that stays busy says "The alert service is busy. Nothing was changed. Try again in a minute."
- **A file of the page** (`app.js`, a module it imports, the stylesheet) answered 429 or 503 is a file that did not load: the browser runs no script that could wait for it. The page shows what `boot-check.js` shows for any file that did not load: "The wallet could not load. Check your connection and reload this page. Nothing was changed." with "Reload". That button is the right way out there: nothing was opened or signed, and a reload asks for the files again. One file is loaded later, at the first check: the light client (`/sdk/light-client.mjs`, by the SDK); a 429 on it then fails that check ("Cannot check the network · signing is off"), and a reload is the way out there too.
- Each busy answer is counted on the page (`data-busy-answers` on `<html>`, a number), for the browser check (`campaign/lib/browser-check.mjs` records what each run met).

**Run in a browser on the preview server only.** `busy-page.test.mjs` runs the real page over a stand-in gateway that refuses requests (start, opening, the five-second read, a payment, a freeze, the faucet, a proof that does not verify); `signup.test.mjs` a protected sign-up through busy answers. `tests/preview-server.mjs` has the switch `/__busy`, and `tests/preview-drive.mjs --only busy` drives Chrome through those screens at any width, light or dark. Not run on a test network.

## The market: seconds against bitcoin

On a network whose proven state has the `market` setting (`spec/market.md`; read with `TimeSDK.marketSetting` from every verified state, never from a gateway), More has a row "Market" and Home a line "Market". On any other network neither is there, nothing of the market is drawn, no list is asked for and the address codec is not loaded. Bitcoin mainnet is closed in this version: on a network whose Bitcoin network is mainnet the panel says so and offers nothing; on the others a strip says "The bitcoin here is test bitcoin (…). It has no value. Never send real bitcoin to an address shown here."

The wallet holds no Bitcoin key and sends no bitcoin. A buyer pays from their own Bitcoin wallet to an address the wallet shows; a seller lists addresses of their own Bitcoin wallet.

**The rule.** Nothing a person pays on is taken from a server's word.

- *Lists* (the book, the trades, the chart, "what the account has a part in") come from the trading API under the wallet's own address (`market-api.mjs`: `/v1/market…`, `/v1/accounts/N/market`; `deploy/api/README.md`, "The market"). Each answer is held to a form: decimal strings, a few fixed words, true and false. No sentence of the service reaches the page. They are shown as lists, with the service's own flags when they are not clean ("its list does not agree with the chain", "its list of trades does not add up", "the list is behind").
- *Everything shown to pay and everything signed on* is read through the wallet's own verifier (`market-ui.mjs`, `proven`: `verifiedState` with `include_market` and the rows named): the order before an offer is shown and again when its review opens (with its owner's account and security row, to judge whether the owner stands: `market.mjs`, `ownerStands`); the fill at every look at the pay screen; the scripts of the address list; an order after it was made.
- *Where the proven row is not what the list said* (price, terms, an order that is gone or closed, a deposit) the page stops and says so ("The price changed: the chain says …, the list said …. Nothing was signed."), and offers the next order. A fill a list names in which the account has no part by its proven row is left out.
- *What is still to pay* is the proven fill's `sats`, read, never computed. The address is the proven fill's script through the codec (`/web-v4/shared/bitcoin-address.mjs`), for the Bitcoin network the proven state names.
- *A take does not sign the deposit, the confirmations or the time to pay* (`spec/market.md`, 10.19). The review takes each from the order row proven at that moment.

**The screens** (`market-ui.mjs`; one panel, `panel-market`, its views drawn by script with `createElement` and `textContent`).

- *My Bitcoin addresses.* Pasted, one or many; each checked by the codec (form, network, checksum), shown whole in groups of four, kept in this browser. Its state is read from proven script rows: Free, Listed (in which order), In use (by which purchase), Used. Said there: the wallet holds no Bitcoin key; nothing is published until an address is put into an order, and then it is public for good; never an address of an exchange account unless its deposits never change; an address serves one purchase, ever; the list is kept in this browser, not locked. A stored entry whose script is not its address's is left out, and counted.
- *Buy seconds.* "I want seconds" or "I have bitcoin". The plan is made from the list (lowest price, a free address, the order's smallest purchase and the rest it must leave: `market.mjs`, `planPurchase`), the order is read proven, and the offer shows its price, the bitcoin to pay in BTC and satoshis, the deposit and when it is lost, the time to pay in Bitcoin blocks and about how long, the confirmations, the fee. A seller who does not stand, or an order without a free address, is passed over, and the page says so. The review asks for a tick ("I will pay the exact amount in time, from a Bitcoin wallet I control. If I do not, I lose the deposit."). The fill it made is found in the account's proven row.
- *The pay screen.* Waiting for payment, part counted, or ended; the amount (large) and the address, a `bitcoin:` link, "Copy the address", "Copy the amount", the last Bitcoin block to pay in and how many are left, when it can end unpaid, and the sentences of `PAY_WORDS`: pay exactly this amount; paying from an exchange is risky (late, short, a batch too large to prove); a payment that confirms after the purchase has ended is with the seller for nothing; a service sends the proof, and what to do after an hour (`deploy/market-prover/README.md`, "Proving one payment by hand"); giving up costs the deposit; all of it is public. A purchase someone else prepared says so, with the seller's price against the other offers of the list; a price more than a fifth above the lowest other offer is a warning on any purchase. A halted header chain and a time to pay that is over are said in red. While the screen is open it is read again every twenty seconds. A purchase is found again after a reload or on another device from the account's proven row, and one prepared by someone else from the list (then read proven).
- *Sell seconds.* Seconds, a price (the chain's last recorded price is shown as a record, not a quote), the free addresses to tick, and the terms, which start from the network's rules: its fewest confirmations, six blocks to pay, a deposit of 3%, and a smallest purchase that shares the order over the addresses ticked. The addresses are read proven as free before the review, which shows each whole. After the order is in a block it is read back, proven, and its addresses are shown to compare.
- *My purchases and orders.* Purchases to pay; each order with what is left, its addresses and their state, and its open purchases; "Add addresses" (kind 58, which counts like a new order), "Cancel this order", "Hand over without proof" (a settle, with "This gives the buyer the seconds without any proof that bitcoin arrived…" and a tick) and, when the chain lets a purchase end, "End it: not paid in time".
- *For traders* (`market-trade-ui.mjs`). The book as price levels with the depth as a bar and its number; recorded trades; a candle chart drawn as SVG by `market-trade.mjs` (`candleGeometry`), with the numbers in a table beside it; forms to take a sell order (also for another account), to sell into a buy order with one free address of a kind the buyer pays to, and to post a buy order with its bond; and the stop orders. The chain's own candles are compared with proven rows and the page says whether they agree. Every past price is called "recorded" (`spec/market.md`, section 9).

**Refusals, before anything is signed** (`market.mjs`, `limitWords`, `sizeRefusal`, `termsRefusal`, `changedWords`): not enough seconds for what is locked and the fees; the daily limit ("Daily limit reached. … counts N seconds against your daily limit, like a payment"), with what each action counts by `spec/market.md`, section 7 (a settle counts the seconds handed over, a release the seconds given up and the sender's deposit, new addresses the order's seconds); a frozen account; an order gone, closed or changed; a size the order does not take; an address that is no longer free; a halted header chain. Whether the header chain is fresh cannot be proven yet: the service's word that it is not is a warning in the review, never a promise either way, and the chain decides. A refusal of the chain reaches the wallet as a gateway's text: the wallet says its own sentence for it (`refusalWords`: the text only picks the sentence) and shows the gateway's words after it, marked as such.

**Signing.** As everywhere: a review, then `sendSigned`. On a protected account the payment key signs, opened with the payment password for that one action (`security-ui.mjs`, `marketAction`, which judges the daily limit again on a fresh proven row). For that, the payment key's list grew by the market's eight actions (`security-flows.mjs`, `MARKET_KINDS`: an order, a cancel, a take, a sale, an expiry, a release, a settle, new addresses). A proof of payment is not on it: the wallet builds none.

**Stop orders are this wallet's, not the chain's.** The chain has none and will have none (`spec/market.md`, section 11). A stop order is a rule kept in this browser, looked at only while the wallet is open and unlocked here, at most every thirty seconds.

- It always has a limit price: one without is refused ("A stop order needs a limit price: this wallet sends no order at any price.").
- Its trigger is the best price of the book among orders of at least a size the person sets (each order that counts is read through the verifier; the least size must be more than the network's smallest trade buys), or several recorded trades in a row (3 to 20) together with the chain's own last recorded price. Never the last recorded price alone.
- It never signs. The wallet's signing asks the person for every action, and on a protected account the payment key is open for one action only; no stop order changes that, and there is no setting to act without asking. When a stop order fires it is marked, the notice says so, and "Review and sign the order" opens the usual review of an order at the limit price, which also says what the trigger shows now.
- A stop order that sells keeps one free address aside; the sell forms do not offer it. Without a free address it is refused.
- Which orders exist comes from the list: a service that hides orders can make a stop order fire early, or not at all. The limit price is what protects. The form says so.

**What it keeps** (in the clear, like the wallet's other settings without a secret): `timecurrency.market-addresses.v1:<network>:<name>` (the addresses, with their scripts), `timecurrency.market-fills.v1:<network>:<name>` (for each fill this browser made, what was to pay at first, so that "part counted" can be said), `timecurrency.market-stops.v1:<network>:<name>` (the stop orders). Someone who can read this browser's storage sees a person's Bitcoin addresses before they are published, and whatever can write it can put another address into the list: the review shows every address whole before an order is signed, and the order is read back afterwards.

**Its limits.**

- A list can leave things out. An order that is not on the list is not offered; a purchase prepared for the account that the list does not name is not found (the fill's id from whoever prepared it would find it; the page has no field for that yet).
- "Paid" is the list's word: the chain proves that a fill is gone, not how it ended. The page says so, and shows the balance it verified.
- The pay screen cannot see Bitcoin. It shows what the chain has counted.
- The test network's proxy passes only `/v1/touches` to the API today (`testnet/plan.mjs`): the market's routes must be passed under the wallet's address too, or the lists do not load ("The market's list could not be reached…").
- A deposit, a time or a date is said with the network's own numbers (a Bitcoin block is taken to last ten minutes on a production network); blocks come when miners find them.

**Run so far.** Unit tests of the rules (`market.test.mjs`, on the engine's vectors and, through the installed verifier, on the engine's proven rows; `market-trade.test.mjs`), the screens on a stand-in page over a stand-in chain (`market-ui.test.mjs`, `market-flows.test.mjs`, `market-stops.test.mjs`; the chain is `tests/mock-market-chain.mjs`, a reading of `spec/market.md` and not the engine), and headless Chrome 158 on the preview at 320, 390, 768 and 1280 px, light and dark (`tests/preview-drive.mjs --only market`). Nothing of it has met a network, a real Bitcoin wallet, the trading API, the prover, a phone or a screen reader.

## What this browser keeps

Everything is in the browser's `localStorage`, under the wallet's address, by network (its id) and by account (its name or its number). No password, no recovery word and no private key is ever in it in the clear.

**Locked while the wallet is locked** (since 24 November 2026, `locked-store.mjs`): what says what the account did or whom it paid.

- `timecurrency.activity.v2:<network>:<name>`: the newest 40 actions this browser signed or checked (hash, block, label such as "Send 5 s to @bob", time, and the account's nonce when the action took one), and from which nonce the list is complete.
- `timecurrency.names.v2:<network>:<name>`: the names the account paid from this browser (see "A name that looks like one you have paid").
- `timecurrency.orders.v2:<network>:<name>`: the marks of the orders of websites it paid (the order's reference, the payment's hash, the website, whether its receipt was checked), so that the wallet says plainly, before signing, that this browser has paid an order already; the newest 200.
- `timecurrency.store-key.v1:<network>:<name>`: the data key of these three, wrapped once for each login key of this browser that may open them, by that key's public key. Nothing in it opens anything without a login key.

How: each of the three is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a random data key of the account, with a fresh IV at each write and the record's place (its kind, the network, the name) and the data key's id as additional data, so that a record moved to another place or account does not open. The data key is wrapped under each login key's storage key: HKDF-SHA-256 of that key's 32-byte seed, in a domain of its own (`TCDEVNET-WALLET-PRIVATE-STORE-V1`) and for one account of one network (`vault.mjs`, `storageKeyFrom`). That key exists only while the login key's secret is in memory: when the vault is opened (`openVault`) or the key is made (`newDevice`, `sealedKey`). The data key is imported so that it cannot be exported, and both are dropped when the wallet locks or the page hides. A vault keeps its format: vaults made before open as before.

- *A new login key made in this browser while the account is open* (a protected sign-up, "Protect my account", a new login password, a new payment password, a new login key after this browser's was removed, a login asked of another device) gets a wrap of its own as soon as it is made: the history survives the change. Wraps of keys this browser no longer holds go at the next unlock.
- *A login key without a wrap* (keys made by a recovery on this browser, a backup file of another key, a store someone damaged) cannot read what was kept: a new data key starts empty records and the old ones go (unless another login key this browser holds, one kept aside for a change of keys, can still read them: they then stay for it, and this opening keeps its history in the page's memory only). The activity then says that it does not know where it starts, and the Alerts panel counts nothing as signed elsewhere until a marker from after its first reading (see "Alerts").
- *Records in the clear of an earlier version* (`timecurrency.activity.v1:`, `timecurrency.names.v1:`, `timecurrency.order.v1:<network>:<account number>:<reference>`) are merged in and written encrypted at the first unlock (the marks of orders at the first verified reading, when the account's number is known), and the plain copy is removed only after the encrypted one was written: a page closed half way loses nothing, and the next unlock does it again.
- *Writing* is queued at once, in order, with the keys of the session that asked, so that two receipts that come together, a lock or a page that hides lose nothing; each write reads the stored record and merges (the three only grow), under a Web Lock per account, so that tabs of one account do not write over each other, and a tab hears another's writes through the `storage` event. A signed action is cleared from its pending place only after its receipt was written (`signing.mjs`, `security-ui.mjs`): a page closed in between keeps the pending action, and "Check pending receipt" records it at the next opening.
- *The mark of a website's order is a courtesy, not the protection* (`locked-store.mjs`, `orderMarkers`). It lets the wallet say plainly, before signing, that this browser has paid an order already. What pays an order once at most is the chain: since protocol version 4 the engine takes a website payment only if its reference carries the sender's next nonce and an expiry within a day (`v5/src/engine.rs`, "merchant reference reserved another sender nonce", "expired or exceeds one day"), so a second payment of the same order needs a nonce the first one uses up, or that only one of the two can take. And the SDK takes no quote on a network before that rule (`TimeSDK.checkMerchantQuote`, called before anything is signed; pinned by `client-v4/orders.test.mjs` and `client-v4/sdk.test.mjs`). So the mark is written encrypted where the store can write it, and where it cannot (an opening that keeps its history in the page's memory only, or a tab whose data key another tab replaced) it lives in that page's memory: nothing of the order or the website is written in the clear, and no payment is refused for it. A mark that the store refuses to write (a full storage) still stops the payment, as before.
- *A session that does not see the store* (the page's memory only, or stale) counts no action as signed elsewhere: it cannot see what other tabs of this browser sent. Its Alerts panel says nothing about actions until an opening that sees the store, and the names paid it knows are those of this page only, as in a new browser (see "A name that looks like one you have paid").

**In the clear, and why:**

- `timecurrency.vault.v1:<network>:<name>` (the login key, or the key of an account with one key, under the login password), `timecurrency.spend-vault.v1:<network>:<name>` (the payment key, under the payment password): they are what unlocking opens. Each is locked to the device with its password (version 3), or under a password the wallet made (marked so), by the rule; records from before the rule, under passwords people chose, keep opening and Keys names them (see "No key under a password you chose alone"). While a protected sign-up is kept, the login place holds its registration key under the new login key kept aside (version 4).
- `timecurrency.device-key.v1` (the id of the device's key for this browser and site, and its random user handle; no account in it): kept so that every account of this browser uses the one key, and a key made again replaces the earlier one on most devices. `timecurrency.vault-lock.v1:<network>:<name>:login|spend` (the two new records while the lock is turned on or off, for the moment of the swap): read at the start, before any unlock, to finish a swap that a closed page left half way.
- `timecurrency.vault-next.v1:<network>:<name>:signin|spend|action` (new keys kept aside until the network has them, and the action that puts them there), `timecurrency.login-request.v1:<network>:<name>` (a login asked of another device), `timecurrency.security-recovery.v1:<network>:<name>` (a recovery on this browser: public keys and the account's number), `timecurrency.signup-consent.v1:<network>:<name>` (a kept sign-up): they are read before any unlock, to settle a change of keys after a reload, to go on with a recovery that has no unlocked key, or to let a sign-up go.
- `timecurrency.vault-next.v1:<network>:<name>:move|movesignin|movespend|moveheldsignin|moveheldspend|movebound` on the new device of a move (the request, its two sealed records, those of a request withdrawn while a move on it could still land, and the last moment a move on any of them could land), and `…:moved` on the old device (the move it sent, until the network decides): read before any unlock, so that a request is shown again after a reload and either device finishes after its page was closed (see "Move my account to another device"). Public keys, signatures and sealed records only.
- `timecurrency.pending.v1:<network>:<account number or slot>`: a signed action waiting for its receipt (its bytes, hash and label). It says what the account is doing and to whom, from the signature until the receipt, a few seconds, or until the next opening when the page closed meanwhile. It stays in the clear for now: it is written before the bytes leave and read by every tab inside the signing lock, at once, and moving it means changing the signing path itself. A follow-up.
- `timecurrency.market-addresses.v1:<network>:<name>`, `timecurrency.market-fills.v1:<network>:<name>`, `timecurrency.market-stops.v1:<network>:<name>` (the market: the receiving addresses of the person's own Bitcoin wallet, what was to pay at first for each fill this browser made, and the stop orders the wallet keeps). No secret, but the addresses are a person's own before an order publishes them: see "The market: seconds against bitcoin", "What it keeps".
- `timecurrency.alerts-seen.v1:` and `timecurrency.alerts-shown.v1:<network>:<account number>` (the marker of the account as last confirmed, and the alerts confirmed: counters, keys and rules of proven state), `timecurrency.alerts-push.v1:` (the push service's address of this browser), `timecurrency.signal.v1:<network>:<account number>` (a validator's upgrade signal as proven state showed it and as signed here: public on the chain, and compared by every tab at every reading), `timecurrency.v4.epochs:<network>:<account number>` (the auction epochs of a laboratory network), `timecurrency.recovery-kind.v1:`, `timecurrency.pause.v1:`, `timecurrency.backup-saved.v1:`, `timecurrency.backup-chosen.v1:` (this key came from a backup file of a password a person chose: Home suggests a new file, and says why), `timecurrency.recovery-claim.v1:`, `timecurrency.upgrade-entry.v1:<network>`, `timecurrency.device-check.v1`, and the light client's checkpoint: settings and what proven state shows, not whom the account paid.

**What it does not hide.** The account's name and public keys are in the names of these places and in the vault, and the chain is public: someone who reads the storage learns which account this browser holds, and can look its actions up on the chain. Locking the list keeps it from a casual reader of a locked browser, not from someone who looks the name up. Hiding the name itself is a change of its own (the places are named in one function, `locked-store.mjs`, `placeOf`).

## Known limits

- Deriving a recovery key's public key from its seed exports the key once as JWK inside WebCrypto, so the private scalar briefly exists as a JavaScript string (`d`) that cannot be zeroed; the wallet drops the reference at once. WebCrypto offers no way to get an Ed25519 public key from a private key without an export.
- The daily limit is a bucket that refills over 24 hours: the full limit can leave at once and, as it refills, almost as much again within the same 24 hours. The screens say so.
- A light-client checkpoint that this browser stored is taken as the place to go on from when it is higher than the pinned one and consistent in itself (`client-v4/light-client.mjs`). Whatever can write this browser's storage and also answer as the network's node can move the wallet onto a chain of its own: the storage is a root of trust (web audit of November 2026, W-10; left open).
- The trial of a device key ("Check this device", `device-key.mjs`): its salt names neither the network nor the account. The lock that holds keys ("Lock my keys to this device") has salts of its own that name the purpose, the network, the account and the vault's key (K-8, settled there). A passkey that the person's Apple or Google account syncs carries the secret to every device of that account: the lock takes it and says so before it is turned on, rather than refusing it (a page cannot reliably tell a synced key from one that is not before using it, and refusing would leave most phones without the lock). Any script of the wallet's origin can ask for the secret with one prompt.
- A vault is Argon2id of 64 MiB and three passes: one graphics card tries about 1,650 passwords a second. So a password chosen here must be at least 15 characters, and is refused when it repeats a part of up to eight characters with at most three others around it, has the same word of four or more letters twice, is a keyboard run of eight or more, is letters or digits in order, or is one of the commonest words (or the account's name) with only digits and signs around it. A payment password at most three edits from the login password, or sharing a beginning of 80% of the shorter one, is refused too (`vault.mjs`, `checkPassword`; `security-flows.mjs`, `checkPasswords`; web audit K-6). A vault that exists opens as before, whatever its password. The rule still takes passwords that a guessing program tries early (`manchesterunited`, `qwertyuiop123456`, whose keyboard run has more than three other characters beside it, `ilovemydogbuddy` with `ilovemycatfluffy` as the payment password): someone with a copy of this browser's storage or of a backup file guesses a password of 40 bits in about ten years on one graphics card, or in hours on thousands. Since 24 November 2026 a chosen password seals a key only together with the device's secret, and elsewhere the wallet makes the passwords (see "No key under a password you chose alone"). What is left open: keys sealed before that under chosen passwords, in this browser and in files and copies made before, until the person lets the wallet make the passwords or locks the keys (which does not undo old copies), or replaces the keys (`AUDIT-2026-11.md`, O-3).
- An account with one key (a network without `protected_registration`, the laboratory's) gets a password the wallet makes, never the device lock: vault version 3 holds login and payment keys only, and widening it was left out.
- "Move my account to another device": the code proves that the request the old device holds is the one on the new device's screen, not who made it; a person who types a request and a code someone sent them gives that person the account, and only the sentence above the field stands against it. The engine takes a move over a freeze or a waiting replacement, which only the wallet refuses. A signed move lands until its approvals expire, whatever happens to the account's login keys meanwhile. Until the new device puts its keys in place they live only in its browser's storage, which a private window or a browser short of room may delete (see "Move my account to another device", "Limits").
- What the wallet made is shown, not kept: a person who presses "I have them" without writing the payment password down, or whose browser did not save the login password, has lost it; the recovery words are the way back, as for a forgotten chosen password. (Closed any other way, the dialog keeps nothing, and the step starts again.) A made password is a JavaScript string while it is shown and cannot be wiped from the page's memory; the page drops it when the dialog closes.
- Chrome 158 offers to keep the made login password from the dialog (seen on 24 November: its log said "Decision: ASK the user [made-form: new password made-login, username made-name]", and it offered nothing for the payment password or the recovery words; see "No key under a password you chose alone"); the password managers of other browsers have not been seen. The dialog's form is sent by "I have them" (`method="dialog"`): the browser fires its submit event but does not navigate, and its password fields are read-only, outside the tab order and one pixel wide; Chromium offers to save only once it counts a form as sent and as succeeded, and ranks fields a person could not have typed in last: Chrome 158 offered all the same, and other browsers may offer nothing. Where the device fails after a person typed chosen passwords at a sign-up, the form of "Create an account" was already sent with them: the browser may then offer to save the login password typed, which opens nothing (the wallet made the passwords instead). Nothing seals with it; a person who lets it be saved is told, when it is filled in, that the login password is one the wallet made. A way that is known to work in Chromium is the Credential Management API (`navigator.credentials.store` with a `PasswordCredential` of the name and the made login password), which asks the person at once; Safari and Firefox have no such call, and offer to save only a visible field a person typed in or that a form sent before a navigation.
- Going back to a version of the page from before 24 November 2026 (`main` before this change) leaves every key sealed since unopenable there: its `vault.mjs` refuses a record that says `"password": "made"` (versions 1, 2 and 3) and every record of version 4 ("Unsupported vault version, intent or KDF"); a record of version 3 without the mark still opens. An older wallet cannot restore a backup file made since, which always holds a record of a made password. The records stay in the browser, and a page of this version opens them again (`BACKUP_FORMAT.md`).
- The manual tools `tests/preview-drive.mjs` and `tests/security-ui-smoke.mjs` start with keys of before the rule (their fixtures) but still type chosen passwords in the flows that now make them: they were not brought to the dialog of made passwords, and are not among the repository's checks.
- An error the wallet did not write is told by its kind, in fixed words, and a refusal of the verifier by its code and fixed words, or by the verifier's own sentence; the verifier's full text is kept in `error.detail` for logs (`actions.mjs`, `failureNotice`; `client-v4/proof-verifier.mjs`; web audit W-2). A gateway's refusal is still shown after the sentence that says whose words follow.
- On a network whose trust data does not say that its nodes report upgrades, a gateway can keep a plan from the wallet, and the wallet then says that it could not check.
  - The wallet learns of a scheduled change of the network's rules from proven state. Whether the network's nodes answer that question comes from the trust data installed with the wallet (`nodes_report_upgrades: true` in `/trust-manifest.json`; a production network's says so, see `tools/genesis-v5.mjs`). It is never taken from a gateway.
  - *A network that says so:* every verified read carries the question from the first one. Nothing is probed and nothing is remembered. A gateway that holds or refuses the question fails the read itself, and the wallet shows "Verification paused".
  - *A network that does not say so* (an older laboratory network): the wallet asks in a read of its own, every minute, until a node has answered. While that read fails, for whatever reason, one line stands above the account. It is not a warning: "This app could not check whether an upgrade of the network is scheduled: the answer could not be verified. This network does not state that its nodes report upgrades, so they may not be able to. This app keeps asking." The line stays until the question is answered, and it is the same whatever the gateway said: the wallet reads no error text to choose what it shows.
  - What remains is this: on such a network a gateway can hold the question for as long as it likes, and the owner sees that line and no plan.
- On such a network, once a node has answered, the question is on every read, and that is remembered.
  - The wallet keeps that fact per network in the browser (`localStorage`, key `timecurrency.upgrade-entry.v1:<network id>`). A gateway can then no longer hide a plan by holding or refusing the one question: the whole read fails and the wallet shows "Verification paused".
  - The same happens if the network's nodes really stop answering the question, for example when a gateway is moved behind an older node.
  - The way out is on the page: open "Offline trust and checkpoint refresh" at the bottom and press "Forget the answer about upgrades". The button is shown only while such an answer is remembered. The wallet then asks in a read of its own again. The vault, the key and the checkpoint stay as they are.
  - Forgetting gives a gateway back the room described in the limit above. Do it only when the network's nodes were really replaced.
