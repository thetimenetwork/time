# V4 browser wallet

See [the V4 rehearsal guide](../README.md) and [the SDK trust and integration contract](../../client-v4/README.md).

This surface uses real Bitcoin regtest receipts, dynamic PoS, fresh selected JMT proofs, fourteen-day stake exposure and explicit seven-day checkpoint refresh. It remains a private test network.

## The screens

The page is made for people who are not technical, on a phone or a laptop. A strip at the top of every screen says that this is the Time test network, that its money is test money with no value, and that the network may be reset. The unit is the second.

- **Start.** "Create an account" (a name, a password, the password again) or "Open my account" (name, password). One sentence under the password says what it is: it unlocks the key this browser keeps, it is never sent anywhere, nobody can reset it. Under "Other ways in": add this browser to an account (the request another browser approves), restore from a backup file, recover with the recovery sheet, recover with guardians. On a network that takes accounts protected from their first block, "Create an account" asks for a login password, a payment password and a daily limit, and shows the recovery papers before the account is made (see "Signing up protected"). A browser that keeps accounts of a test network that was reset, and none of this one, is told so here, with one button (see "A test network that was reset").
- **Five places.** Home, Payments, Activity, Keys and More, always labelled: at the bottom of the screen on a phone or a tablet, in a column on the left of a screen 1024 px wide or more.
- **Home.** The `@name` with its initial and "Copy", the balance labelled "Balance", in seconds, and three round actions: Send, Receive, Get test time. Under them one line "Staked" (what the account has lent to validators, which is not in the balance) that opens Staking. The line "Checked by this browser against the network at block N" is the wallet's own verification of the account proof (before: "Account proof verified"). The card "Protection": for a protected account its freeze, its daily limit and its pause, and under it what is waiting on the account; for any other account "Not protected yet" with one button (see "Protection" below). The newest three entries of this browser's activity. Until a backup file was downloaded (or this key came from one), an account with one key sees a line that suggests it.
- **Payments: Send.** Send and Receive are its two sides. Name, amount (large), the fee; under the name, the names this account has paid from this browser, which a tap puts in the field (the same names the review compares a new name with, `names.mjs`). The review says From, To, Amount, Fee, Total (in bold), Network and When ("In the next block, within seconds. Once it is in a block, it cannot be undone."), and its button says what it does ("Send 1.5 seconds"); then "Sent. @bob has the 1.5 seconds now and needs to do nothing. This browser checked the network's receipt." in the notice and under the form. A protected account is asked for its payment password, as before.
- **Payments: Receive.** The name to give, and the account number.
- **Activity.** What this browser signed or checked, newest first, with the pending action and its buttons, and a search that hides the lines without the words typed (it reads the page only).
- **Keys** (`actions.mjs`, `keysView`, drawn by `app.js`, `renderKeys`). Each thing that opens the account, as a card: what it does, where it is, when it is needed, what to do if it is lost, marked in words "Every day", "When you pay" or "Keep safe".
  - An account with one key: the name and the password every day, the backup file to keep safe, and the form that downloads it.
  - "Protect my account": first what the person gets (login password, payment password, recovery sheet and passphrase), then the existing steps of `security-ui.mjs` in plainer words.
  - A protected account: its state and its limits, five cards (login password, payment password, recovery sheet, recovery passphrase, this browser), "Print this page" (the cards only, black on white; no secret is on them), the backup file (which holds the login key only), and under "Change something" a new login password, new recovery papers and a new payment password. The freeze, the end of a freeze, the daily limit and what is waiting were here; they are on Home now, and Keys says so.
- **More.** The account's initial and name, then Staking (with the Bitcoin regtest auction card only on a network that has that auction), Devices, Guardian recovery, Validator signal (only for the owner of a validator), Use in your app, Validator operator. "Offline trust and checkpoint refresh" stays at the foot of every screen, closed, because it must be reachable while the wallet is locked.
- **A website's window** (`/approve`). The website's address, large; what it asks ("Sign in only", or "Pay exactly 0.1 seconds to account 7, once"); Approve and Decline. The rest of the wallet is hidden there. The review and every check of the request are as before.

**How it looks** (the same for every page of `web-v4`; the tokens are the first `:root` block of `styles.css`, copied as they are into each page's stylesheet). A white canvas, near-black ink (#222), pale gray surfaces (#F1F1F1), secondary text #666, #DDD only for separators and #767676 where a line marks a control; the dark palette has the same roles and was checked on its own (text at least 4.5:1, the edges of controls at least 3:1). No colour says anything alone: green, red and amber come with a word or a sign. Sections are flat and grouped by space and headings; only the Protection card, what waits on the account and a website's request sit on a surface or in a frame. Numbers are tabular and bold where they matter, with their unit; names, hashes and keys are in a monospace font. Buttons are pills 52 px tall, fields 56 px, rows 64 px, every target at least 44 px. Signs are line drawings used as masks over the text colour (`data:` images, allowed by the page's `img-src`); the drawings of people are inline SVG in the same colours, only on Receive and an empty Activity, never over a number, a name or a review. Movement is brief (180 ms, the review's sheet 260 ms) and stops when the system asks for less motion. Nothing is loaded from elsewhere: system fonts, no library, and the page's Content-Security-Policy (`style-src 'self'`, no inline script or style) is unchanged.

**Looking at the screens without a network.** `tests/preview-server.mjs` serves these files on 127.0.0.1 with the gateway's Content-Security-Policy, in front of a mock chain and a mock faucet with a sample website; `tests/preview-drive.mjs` drives a headless Chrome with a new profile through every screen at 390 and 1280 px, or the widths given with `--widths` (on 2026-11-22 also 320 and 768 px, and 390 px with `--theme dark`) (screenshots and a log) and walks the paths of the browser check that the mock can carry. The mock's state is taken as proven by a stand-in verifier and it applies transfers only: it shows the pages and their wiring, never that a check of the wallet works.

## Get test time

When the wallet's service names a registration service (`registrationService` in `/api/network`: the faucet of a test network), Home offers "Get test time". Without one the button is not there. One press (`app.js`, `getTestTime`; `actions.mjs`, `askFaucet`):

1. reads the faucet's facts (`GET <origin>/v1/faucet`). A faucet that answers for another network, says it is not giving, or does not answer is asked nothing;
2. asks for one grant for this account: `POST <origin>/v1/faucet/grants` with `{"account": "<number>"}`, by its number (a name of digits only would be read as a number by the faucet). Nothing else is sent: no cookie, no referrer, no name;
3. waits for the receipt of the hash the faucet answered, verifies it like any receipt, and checks that it is a transfer to this account from the faucet's account (`faucetReceiptPays`). Only then does it say "You received 0.1 seconds of test time. This browser checked that it arrived." and record it in Activity. Otherwise it says that the faucet says it sent test time and that this browser has not seen it arrive yet.

Each answer of the faucet is waited for fifteen seconds and read to 64 KiB at most, as for a registration. A refusal whose code the faucet's README lists (`deploy/faucet/README.md`, "Refusals") is told in the wallet's own sentence, which says that it is what the faucet says (`FAUCET_SAYS`); the faucet's own words are then not shown. Any other refusal is shown as a gateway's words are: after "The test faucet refused this request. Its answer follows; this wallet did not check it. Do not copy or enter anything from it:", plain, cut to 160 characters, and last (`faucetWords`). The faucet must list the wallet's origin in its `cors_origins`, as it must for registrations; its `Retry-After` header is not readable from the wallet's origin and is not used.

What a faucet can make the wallet show: one of its fixed sentences, or its own words marked as such. It cannot make the wallet say that time arrived: that is said only after the wallet's own check of the receipt.

## Creating an account: who sponsors the registration

A new account needs a sponsor: an existing account that pays the registration charge and signs for the new key. The wallet finds one in the answer of its own service (`/api/network`):

- `sponsorAccount`: the service sponsors by itself. Only the laboratory's launcher does that (`tools/start-v4-lab.mjs`), with a key of the laboratory.
- `registrationService`: an origin the wallet may ask, such as the faucet of a test network. An operator sets it in the gateway (`deploy/gateway/README.md`, "Where a new account is registered"). The wallet asks it which account sponsors and for which network, signs its consent for that account, and sends the new public key, the commitment of the name and the consent. The name itself is revealed later, by the wallet, on the chain.
- Neither: the wallet says that its service names no registration sponsor, before it makes a key or stores a vault.

In every case the key is made in this browser and the password never leaves it. The sponsor gets no power over the account.

- **The wallet takes a sponsor's word for nothing about the chain.** It waits for the receipt of the sponsor's action, checks that the receipt names its key and its commitment, and reads the commitment in proven state before it reveals the name. When the service answers that this very registration waits on the chain already, there is no receipt to wait for, and the wallet reads proven state for up to half a minute.
- **What a registration service is told.** The address the person comes from, the new public key and the commitment. Not the name. Once the wallet has revealed the name on the chain under that key, the service can put that address and that name together.
- **What it can make the wallet show.** A refusal, in its own words. The notice shows them after a sentence of the wallet that says whose words follow and that it did not check them, with only plain characters, with anything that looks like a number or a hash broken up, cut to 160 characters, and last (`actions.mjs`, `registrationWords`). That is the rule for a gateway's words.
- **What it can hold up.** The wallet waits fifteen seconds for each answer and reads 64 KiB of it at most. A service that is down, slow or refusing stops the sign-up there: the key and its encrypted vault stay in this browser, and unlocking again goes on from what the chain shows.
- **A day's wait in one case.** The faucet sponsors one registration for a key in 24 hours. If it took the request and its action never reached a block, this browser's key for that name gets no second one from it that day.
- The consent, and the registration that waits for its name, are good for five minutes, or twenty seconds less than the network's longest if that is shorter.

A wallet that was installed for one network refuses a registration service that answers for another.

## Signing up protected

On a network whose genesis has `protected_registration` (read from every verified state, never from a gateway: `TimeSDK.protectedRegistration`), "Create an account" makes an account that is protected from its first block (`signup.mjs`, `signup-ui.mjs`, and `client.registerProtected` of the SDK, through the sponsor of "Creating an account" above). On any other network "Create an account" is as it was, and the script draws nothing of this.

**What the person does.**

1. On the start screen: the name; "Login password" and again, under it, marked "Every day": "Opens this wallet and signs you in to websites. It cannot pay. Nobody can reset it."; "Payment password" and again, marked "When you pay": "Pays up to your daily limit. It must differ from your login password. Do not let the browser save it."; "Daily limit, in seconds", filled with 600: "Suggested: 10 minutes of seconds a day (600). What your payment password alone can pay in a day, fees included. More waits, or needs a recovery paper." (the number is `DEFAULT_DAILY_LIMIT_SECONDS` of `security-flows.mjs`, as on Keys, and a number typed is kept). The waiting period and the freeze length are those "Protect my account" suggests, 48 hours or the network's minimum if that is longer, and are not asked. The button says "Next: my recovery papers".
2. The wallet checks the name with the network and that the registration service answers for this network and sponsors; only then does it make the keys, lock them with the passwords and make the two papers. "Your recovery sheet" and "Your recovery passphrase", each marked "Keep safe", with the sentences of "Protect my account", "Print the sheet", and "Only these two papers will work for your account. If you start over, you get new ones." Then "I wrote both down".
3. "Check your papers": two groups of the sheet and the passphrase. A wrong one shows the papers again. "Create my account" keeps what is needed in this browser, creates the account, and opens it: "Your account is ready, protected from its first block. This browser checked it with the network. Next time, open it with your name and your login password." The card on Home says "Your account is protected." and "You can pay 600 of 600 seconds now without waiting, fees included."

"Start over", locking the wallet or switching to "Open my account" before step 3 forget the keys and the papers: "Nothing was created. The recovery papers that were shown will never work: destroy them if you wrote them down." The papers are never skipped: nothing reaches the chain before they were typed back.

**Left alone.** While the papers are on the page no account exists, so the lock of an idle wallet has nothing to lock. The same rule hides them (`signup-ui.mjs`, `PAPERS_ACTIVITY`; the time is `IDLE_LOCK_MS` of `guards.mjs`, which the lock uses too): after five minutes without use, at step 2 or step 3, the keys and the papers are forgotten as by "Start over", the form is back, and the notice says "Your recovery papers were hidden after 5 minutes without use, and nothing was created. The papers that were shown will never work: destroy them if you wrote them down. Start again to get new ones." Use is what a person does who is writing the papers down or typing them back on the page: a tap or a click, a key, text that comes without a key (pasting, dictation), and scrolling by a wheel, a trackpad, a finger, the scroll bar or the keyboard; each starts the five minutes again. A pointer that only moves over the page, a field that takes the focus and the page being shown again do not count: they happen when nobody is there too, and the lock does not count them. From "Create my account" on, nothing is hidden: the papers are being checked and kept, and if they come back (a paper typed back wrong) the five minutes start again.

**The account.** A login key, a payment key and the two recovery keys in their roles, two proofs for big changes, and the daily limit and the waits above: what "Protect my account" makes of an account, from its first block. The registration key that signs the registration becomes the account's identity key and holds no role. The newcomer pays nothing.

**What this browser keeps, and when.** Nothing before the papers are checked. Then, before anything is sent, all of it or none of it (`signup-ui.mjs`, `keep`): the registration key, locked with the login password and the salt of the name, in the place of the account's login vault; the login key under the login password and the payment key under the payment password, kept aside in the places "Protect my account" uses (`security-flows.mjs`, `stageVaults`); and a note of the public half of the sign-up, signed by the registration key: the name, the registration key, the four keys with their acceptances of their roles, and the policy (`signup.mjs`). With the registration key and its salt that is everything `registerProtected` needs to be called again with the same arguments. Before each consent the registration key gives a sponsor leaves the browser, the expiry of that consent is kept too, signed by the registration key (`localStorage`, key `timecurrency.signup-consent.v1:<network id>:<name>`; see "Letting a kept sign-up go"). Once the chain holds the account with these keys, they take the places of the live keys, the registration key is overwritten (`settleStaged`) and the record of the consent removed. No password, no paper and no key is ever in the storage in the clear.

**Where it can stop, and what the person sees on return.**

- *After the passwords, before the papers were checked* (the page closed or reloaded, the wallet locked, "Start over"): nothing was kept and nothing was sent. The start screen is as before; the papers that were shown never work.
- *After the papers were checked, before the registration service paid, or after it paid and before the registration was sent:* the start screen fills in the name and says "Creating @name was not finished. Open it with your login password: this browser goes on from what the network shows." The login password opens the registration key, and the wallet calls `registerProtected` again with what was kept. A commitment the chain holds is not paid for again, and the service is not asked while the chain holds the commitment or the account: the wallet then passes `registerProtected` no sponsor. Should the commitment run out between the wallet's read and the SDK's, the SDK says so before it signs anything (`SPONSOR_REQUIRED`), and the wallet asks the service then. The registration is the same bytes as before.
- *After the registration, before the keys were put in place:* when the page opens it sees the account hold the kept keys and puts them in place before anything is typed: "The new keys of @name are active on this browser. Unlock with the login password chosen for them." If it could not read the chain then, the login password opens the registration key, the wallet finds the account on the chain, puts the keys in place and opens the account with the new login key.
- *Any other failure on the way* (a service that does not answer, a refusal the wallet has no sentence for): "Your sign-up is kept in this browser, locked with your passwords. Open your account with its name and your login password to go on. ", then the reason. The wallet is locked.

**Refusals, in the wallet's words.**

- The name was taken meanwhile (SDK code `NAME_TAKEN`): "Someone else took the name @name before your account was made. Nothing of yours is on the network. The recovery papers you wrote down for @name will never work: destroy them. Create your account with another name." The form for a new account is shown again.
- The network refuses the name as too close to one that exists (`distinct_names`; told as in "A new name the network refuses" below): "That name is too close to @x, which already exists. Choose another. The recovery papers you wrote down for @name will never work: destroy them."
- The service named a payment that is not for this account (`SPONSOR_MISMATCH`): "The registration service named a payment that is not for your account, so this browser did not use it, and nothing was registered. Try again later: open your account with its name and your login password, and this browser goes on. Your recovery papers stay valid."
- The service paid for this key in the last 24 hours (its code `account_limit`), which happens when the commitment it paid for ran out, five minutes after, before the account was made: "The registration service says that it paid for this sign-up in the last 24 hours, and that this payment ran out before your account was made. It pays again 24 hours after the first time: then open your account with your login password, and this browser goes on. Your recovery papers stay valid."
- Another commitment of this key on the chain (`COMMITMENT_MISMATCH`): "The network holds a registration of this sign-up's key with other details than this browser keeps, so nothing was signed. That should not happen: this browser's data may have been changed. Wait an hour, then open your account with your login password to try again."
- The account of this sign-up holds other keys or another policy (`REGISTRATION_MISMATCH`): "The account @name was made by this sign-up, but its keys or its limits are no longer the ones this browser made. If you changed them on another device, use that one. If you did not, someone has your recovery papers: recover the account with them at once."
- The network does not take such a registration (`PROTECTED_REGISTRATION_UNAVAILABLE`; a genesis cannot lose the setting, so this means that the page is of another network): "This network does not take accounts that are protected from the start, so nothing was sent. Reload this page: the wallet then makes your account the way this network takes it."
- What was kept was changed: "The sign-up kept in this browser was changed after it was made, so this browser does not use it, and sends nothing for it. Create your account with another name." The note is used only when the registration key's signature over it holds, and only with the kept keys it names: whatever can write this browser's storage could otherwise put a payment key of its own into a sign-up that is gone on with. It cannot open the registration key, which only the login password opens.

**Its limits.**

- A sign-up that is kept holds its name in this browser ("An encrypted vault already exists for this account here. Unlock it instead.") until it is finished, or let go (see "Letting a kept sign-up go").
- The papers are hidden after five minutes without use (see "Left alone" above): a person who writes them down slowly without touching the device for five minutes starts again, with new papers.
- The account's first recovery passphrase names the registration key in its salt (see "Recovery passphrase" below): a wallet that does not know that cannot use it.

**On a network, in Chrome only.** The sign-up, each stop and each refusal ran on the stand-in page of `signup.test.mjs`, over a stand-in chain and registration service that apply the rules of `spec/settings.md`, section 4.1, with the SDK's own functions; that the engine takes the registration's bytes is shown in `client-v4/protected-signup.test.mjs`. On 23 November the browser check signed up two accounts this way ("wallet-create", "wallet-second") in Chrome 158 on laboratory networks with `protected_registration`, the faucet as the registration service (campaigns `tc-v5-final8` and `tc-v5-final9`): the card on Home showed the first protected at once, with the daily limit of 600 seconds. `tc-v5-final8` found that after the sign-up the start screen had lost its form, so that "Lock" left the wallet with no way to open it until the page was reloaded; fixed in `ff8fe46` (`signup-ui.mjs`; `signup.test.mjs` now checks the form after the account is made and after a lock), and the lock passed in `tc-v5-final9`. The stops and the refusals above have not run on a network.

## Letting a kept sign-up go

A sign-up that is kept (see "Signing up protected") is finished with the login password chosen for it. A person who forgot that password can let it go from the start screen: under "Open my account", when the name typed is that of a sign-up this browser keeps, one line says "Forgot the login password you chose for this sign-up?" with the button "Let this sign-up go" (`signup-ui.mjs`, `letGo`; the rule is `signup.mjs`, `letGoState`).

**When it is offered.** Only when nothing of the sign-up can ever be on the network, so that letting it go cannot lose an account, or keys that an account could still come to have. A state of the network that this browser verifies at that moment, read by the name and by the registration key, must show:

- no account of the sign-up: none whose identity key is the registration key, and none of its name that holds one of its keys;
- no commitment of the registration key that the chain can still use: the registration needs one that has not run out;
- no other account of its name. Such a sign-up can never finish, and letting it go would free nothing; and it is the one case in which an account of that name exists, which whatever can write this browser's storage could pass off as a sign-up of its own (below);
- and the chain's time past the expiry of every consent the registration key gave a sponsor, since a sponsor may still use one to make a commitment until then. Only the registration key signs a consent, and only the login password opens it here: whoever forgot that password gives no new one.

The expiry of the last consent is the record this browser keeps of it (above), when its signature by the registration key holds and it names this network, this name and this key. Without such a record (a sign-up kept before there was one, or a record that was removed or does not hold) the page waits by itself: every consent the SDK signs is dated at most five minutes ahead of the chain (`TimeSDK.SPONSOR_CONSENT_MAX_AHEAD_MICROS`), so ten minutes of the chain's time after its first reading that showed nothing, all that were given before have run out. That first reading is kept in the page's memory only: a page opened again waits again.

The network, and the record of the consent with it, are read once before the review and once again after it is confirmed (another tab that goes on with the sign-up meanwhile keeps a newer record); both must allow it.

**What the person is told.**

- Offered: a review, "Let this sign-up go", with two lines: "@name is free again, for anyone. This browser forgets the keys it made for this sign-up; it cannot be undone.", and "The recovery sheet and passphrase you wrote down for @name will never work: destroy them." Its button is "Let it go". Then: "This browser let the sign-up of @name go. Destroy the recovery papers you wrote down for it." Cancelled: "Nothing was changed. The sign-up is still kept in this browser."
- An account of the sign-up on the network: "The network holds the account @name that this sign-up made, so this browser keeps what opens it. Open it with the login password you chose for it; if you forgot that, recover the account with your recovery sheet and your recovery passphrase, under "Other ways in"."
- Another account of the name: "The name @name belongs to another account on the network, so this sign-up can never finish, and this browser keeps it as it is. If that account is yours, open it with its login password, or recover it with your recovery sheet and your recovery passphrase; if not, create your account with another name."
- A commitment: "A registration of @name from this sign-up waits on the network until …, so this browser keeps the sign-up. If you remember your login password, open your account with it before then; if not, come back after that time to let it go."
- A consent that may still be used: "Nothing of this sign-up is on the network now, but a registration of it that was asked for could still reach it until …. Come back after that time to let it go." Without a record: "… This browser does not know when a registration of it was last asked for, so it waits ten minutes to be sure that none can reach the network any more: keep this page open, and press "Let this sign-up go" again after …."

**What is removed.** The five places of the sign-up, and nothing else: the registration key in the login place, the login and payment keys kept aside, the note, and the record of the consent. Each is removed only while it still holds what was checked, under the lock of the name's vault.

**What whatever can write this browser's storage can and cannot do with it.** Nothing is removed on the word of the storage alone: the button, the network read twice and the person's confirmation come first. A note that is not the one the registration key signed, or keys aside that are not the note's, is refused ("The sign-up kept in this browser was changed after it was made, so this browser cannot tell which keys are this sign-up's, and does not let it go. Create your account with another name."). A payment key kept in the live place of the name is an account's, and the sign-up of that name is not let go ("This browser keeps a payment key of an account @name, so it does not let a sign-up of that name go. Open that account with its login password."). A record of a consent that does not hold is not used. What remains:

- Whatever kept an older record of this very sign-up, signed by its key, can put it back in place of the newer one. Letting go is then offered after the older consent's expiry, while a newer consent, given less than five minutes before, may still be used. For that to make an account, a sponsor would have to use that consent, and the registration, which only the registration key signs, would have to reach the chain from a copy kept of an earlier attempt. The account would hold nothing, and its keys would be gone from this browser; the person's recovery papers recover it, if they were not destroyed.
- Whatever can write the storage can delete a kept sign-up, as it could before; and it can put a sign-up of its own key in the place of an account's login key, which deletes that key by itself. Letting go is then refused: the network holds an account of that name, and a payment key of the name may be kept here too.
- A sign-up whose name someone else took on the network (the refusal "Someone else took the name …") cannot be let go: its name is not free in any case. Its entries stay in this browser.
- The state that shows nothing is a state of the network this browser verified; it is as recent as such a state is, and no more.

**Not run on a network or in a browser.** It ran on the stand-in page of `signup.test.mjs`, over its stand-in chain and registration service: let go once the consent ran out and signed up again under the same name, refused with a commitment, with the account, and with every change of the storage above; and the record kept with the expiry the SDK gave.

## Protection: freeze, daily limit, pause

Home has one card, "Protection" (`index.html`, `protect-card`; what it says is `protection.mjs`, `protectionView`; `security-ui.mjs` draws it and signs). What it shows is read from the account's security row in proven state at every reading of the account, never from what this browser remembers. A switch that is pressed goes back at once to what the chain says, and opens a small step that asks for what the chain needs for it; the switch moves when a verified state shows the change. One step is open at a time, and a step that closes takes what was typed into it along.

- **Not protected yet.** An account with one key: "One key can move everything, and a forgotten password cannot be replaced.", and one button, "Protect my account", which opens the form on Keys with the focus on its first field. The form suggests a daily limit of 600 seconds, written "10 minutes of seconds a day (600)"; the number is in one place (`security-flows.mjs`, `DEFAULT_DAILY_LIMIT_SECONDS`), and a limit the person typed is not written over. Nothing about the account is held back until it is protected. On a network that takes protected sign-ups a new account of this wallet is protected from its first block and never shows this; an account made before, or by another wallet, can.
- **Freeze.** A switch. On: the payment password or one recovery paper, one review, at once.
  - A freeze lasts the account's freeze length (48 hours unless another was chosen) from the block that holds it, and ends by itself. Nothing keeps it on. It can be placed again only in its second half, and then lasts the whole length from that moment: the card names the time from which that is possible, and offers "Freeze for another 48 hours" from then.
  - While frozen, the payment password alone pays nothing and starts nothing, websites that use the login library take no sign-in, and what was waiting was dropped when the freeze began. Seconds sent to the account arrive. The payment password together with one recovery paper can still pay, and ends the freeze early: that is "End the freeze", which the switch opens when it is pressed while on.
  - An account that is frozen because a change of all its keys waits is shown as that, and its switch cannot be used: that change is finished or cancelled under "Waiting on your account".
- **Daily limit.** A bar and one sentence: what can be paid now without waiting, out of the limit. The numbers are the chain's allowance, which refills evenly over 24 hours (`/sdk/account-security.mjs`, `allowance`), and the limit in force (a raise whose wait is over counts, although the stored row shows it only after the account's next action; `protection.mjs`, `effectiveLimit`). "Change" opens the change of the limit: a lower limit applies at once; a higher one waits the account's waiting period and can be cancelled, or applies at once when a recovery paper is typed as well. Which of the three the typed number will be stands under the field while it is typed, and again in the review.
- **Pause payments.** A switch; see the next paragraphs.
- **Waiting.** The card counts the payments and changes that wait. The list itself, each item with when it takes effect and its "Cancel", is the card "Waiting on your account" directly under it; it was on Keys. The count that stood on the Keys button stands on the Home button.
- The card ends with one sentence: switching protection on is immediate; switching it off waits, or needs a recovery paper, because nobody can reverse a theft here.

**What "Pause payments" is on the chain.** The engine has no pause. The wallet lowers the daily limit to exactly the fee of one action (kind 77, a lowering, at once; `security-flows.mjs`, `buildPause`), and an account whose limit is not above the fee is what the card calls paused.

- *Why not zero.* The engine takes a limit of zero (`v5/src/security.rs` puts no lower bound on `daily_limit_micros`). But it charges every action the payment key signs with a nonce to the allowance, fee included: a payment, both steps of a payment that waits, a change of the limit, a change of login keys. With a limit of zero the payment key alone could then not even start a payment that waits or ask for a higher limit, and the one way back would be a recovery paper. With a limit of one fee no payment of any amount fits (a payment costs its amount and the fee), and the payment key can still do one thing a day that costs a fee.
- *What still can happen while paused.* The payment password can start a payment that waits the waiting period, and send it after that if nobody cancelled it; ask for the limit back, which waits as long; freeze; cancel what waits; replace a login key. Each of the first, the second and the last costs a fee, and the paused limit covers one fee a day between them. A pause does not stop a payment that waits: whoever does not look at "Waiting on your account" for a whole waiting period is not protected by it. A freeze drops what waits; a pause does not.
- *Switching it on* costs a fee that is charged to the allowance, like every change of the limit. With less than one fee left of the day's limit the chain refuses it; the wallet says so before anything is signed, and names the freeze, which is charged to the balance. A raise of the limit that was waiting is dropped by the pause, and the review says so. A frozen account cannot be paused.
- *Switching it off by waiting* is a raise of the limit (kind 77): it waits the waiting period, is listed under "Waiting on your account" and can be cancelled there by the payment key or a recovery paper. It costs a fee charged to the allowance, so it is not possible while the day's one fee is used up: the step then says when it will be.
- *Switching it off at once* takes the payment password and one recovery paper. The engine has one operation that sets a limit at once: a replacement of the keys and the policy approved by the payment key and a recovery key (kind 73). The wallet signs one that keeps every key in its role and changes the limit only (`buildLimitNow`). Being a replacement, it also cancels everything that waits on the account and moves its authority epoch on, so websites end their sessions; the review says both. It would end a freeze as well, so it is not offered for a frozen account. The same operation raises an ordinary limit at once from "Change".
- *After a pause the allowance is not full.* By either way the allowance keeps what was left under the paused limit, at most one fee, and refills over 24 hours under the limit that is back. Right after a pause ends almost nothing can be paid at once. The steps, the reviews and the card say so.
- *The limit before the pause* is remembered in this browser so that ending the pause can offer it: `localStorage`, key `timecurrency.pause.v1:<network id>:<account name>`, `{"limit": "<microseconds>", "at": <time>}`; nothing secret. It is written before the pause is sent, removed when the pause was ended at once, and dropped when a reading shows the account not paused and the record is older than ten minutes. It is a suggestion in a field that can be changed, shown again in the review. A browser that holds no record asks for the number.
- *A limit below the fee*, zero included, is not something this wallet sets. An account that has one (from another wallet) is shown as paused, with what that means: the payment password alone starts nothing, and only the way with a recovery paper is offered.

**How the card can mislead, and what it does not show.**

- "Paused" is not "nothing can leave". A payment can be made to wait and sent after the waiting period, by whoever has the payment password. The card says so in one sentence; it does not watch the list for the person.
- "Frozen" does not hold against the payment password together with a recovery paper, and it ends by itself.
- The card shows the state of the last verified reading, which the open wallet repeats every five seconds. While the wallet says "Verification paused" the card shows the last state it could verify, without a mark of its own.
- The bar is what the payment password can pay at once. What can leave in 24 hours is up to about twice the limit, which Keys says and the card does not.
- The times of a freeze and of a waiting change are the chain's times written in this device's time zone and format. On a network whose waits survive a stop they are the earliest times, said "not before" (see "The clock of the waits").
- The remembered limit is not locked as a key is: whatever can write this browser's storage can change the number that ending a pause suggests. The number is shown before anything is signed.
- "Not protected yet" says that a forgotten password cannot be replaced. An account with guardians, or with a key in another browser, has those ways in besides; the card does not list them (Keys does).
- A switch that is greyed says nothing by itself; the sentence under it says why.

In both, the chain is `tests/mock-security-chain.mjs`: a reading of the engine's rules, written for these tests, and not the engine. On 23 November the steps of `campaign/lib/browser-check.mjs` ran on a laboratory network, in headless Chrome 158 on Linux (campaign `tc-v5-final9`, 38 of 38): "protection-limit-lower" (a lower limit from "Change", in force at once), "protection-card" (the card of an account protected from its first block, read from proven state, its switches real switches with labels, and nothing that needs sideways scrolling at a phone's width), "protection-pause" (the verified limit was one fee, and a payment made to wait waited on the chain), "protection-pause-off" with the recovery sheet (the verified limit was back at once and what waited was cancelled), and "security-freeze" from the switch. A node took a pause signed by this wallet, and a replacement that changes the limit only. "protection-not-yet" did not run: on a network with `protected_registration` the check's accounts are protected from their first block. ## The clock of the waits

On a network whose genesis has `waits_survive_stops` the waits and the freezes of a protected account do not run while the chain is stopped (`spec/settings.md`, section 3). Every moment its security row stores is then a moment on the clock of the waits: the chain's time since genesis less the total of its stops, which every proven state carries (`stopped_micros`). Read on the chain's time, after a stop of more than five minutes a freeze would look over, a payment that waits ready, a raise of the limit applied and the allowance refilled, each too early by the stops.

- **Compared with that clock** (`TimeSDK.securityNow`; `security-flows.mjs`, `waitsOf`): the Protection card (the freeze and when it can be placed again, what can be paid now, a raise that waits, the pause and its end), Keys (the limit in force, the payment card), Send and its choice above the limit, staking, the operation of a validator and the moment its limit has come back, a validator's signal, a website's sign-in, what waits on the account and the moment it can be sent, the end of a freeze and a raise of the limit, a recovery on a new browser and its end, and a browser whose login key was removed.
- **Shown as a date** with the stops added (`TimeSDK.securityDeadline`; `momentOf`). While a later stop can still move it the date is the earliest, and the page says so: "Your account is frozen. It ends not before …", "freeze again not before …", "it applies not before …", "Can be sent not before …", "It can finish not before …", "The pause ends not before …", in a review "48 hours: it can be sent not before …" and "Not before …". The screen "Validator signal" adds the stops of the state its status was read from, which the SDK's status of a signal carries (`waits`): "This account is frozen; the freeze ends not before … UTC."
- **Left on the chain's time:** what a key signs. The expiry of a freeze, of the approvals of an operation with two keys and of a login request for another browser, and the check that retires such a signed action once its expiry has passed (`security-ui.mjs`, `retired`).
- **On a network without the setting** the two clocks are one, every date is exact and no "not before" is said: nothing changed.

**Not run across a stop on a network.** On 23 November the page ran in Chrome on laboratory networks with the setting (campaigns `tc-v5-final8` and `tc-v5-final9`), on which the chain did not stop, so that the two clocks were one there. `waits-clock.test.mjs` runs the real page on a stand-in chain that stood still for six hours; ten of its eleven tests fail on the page before this clock, and the eleventh is the network without the setting. The check of staking and of a validator's operation against the daily limit (`security-ui.mjs`, `limitOf`, which `stakeLimit` and `stakeAction` use) is held by the last but one test of `validator-protected.test.mjs`. One place has no test that fails without it: the lookup of an item that waits when it is acted on (`actOnEvent`), whose result is the same on either clock. No chain with the setting has stopped under this page.

## A name that looks like one you have paid

An account name is 2 to 15 characters of `a-z`, `0-9` and `_`, so no capital letter, accent or space can imitate a name. A name can still read like another one: `paypa1` for `paypal`, `alice_` for `alice`, `barn` for `bam` in some fonts, `b1nance` for `binance`. Before a payment is signed, its review compares the name it goes to with the names this account has paid from this browser and with the account's own name (`names.mjs`; `app.js`, `review`, `payeeLines` and `paidNamesNow`).

- **A name this account has paid:** the review is as it was.
- **A name it has not paid, which looks like one it has:** a warning that shows both names in the page's monospace font, where `1` and `l`, `0` and `o` differ: "You have not paid @paypa1 before. It looks like @paypal, which you have paid. Check every letter." The review has a box, "I checked the name, letter by letter.", and the button that signs stays disabled until it is ticked, also after the review's first 0.7 seconds. A review confirmed without the tick counts as cancelled.
- **A name it has not paid, which looks like the account's own name:** the same warning and the same box, with the words "It looks like @alice, your own name." A request to send seconds "back to yourself" may name such an account. When a name looks like the own name and like a paid one, the stronger likeness (by the order of the rule below) is the one told, and the own name when both are equally strong.
- **Any other name it has not paid:** one plain line, "First payment to @name."

**When two names look alike.** Two different names, by the first of these that holds:

1. Their skeletons are equal. The skeleton of a name is the name with every `_` dropped, `0` read as `o`, `1` as `l` and `w` as `vv`, and then `rn` read as `m` and `vv` as `w`.
2. Their looser keys are equal: the skeleton with `i` read as `l`, and the digits that are written for letters read as those letters (`3` e, `4` a, `5` s, `7` t, `8` b, `2` z, `9` and `6` g).
3. Both skeletons have four characters or more and differ by one edit: one character added, removed or changed, or two neighbours swapped.

**Which payments.** Send, for an account with one key and for a protected account (within the limit, "Pay after the waiting period" and "Pay now with a recovery paper"). A payment a website asks for: the name is that of the account the website names by its number, read from proven state. Not staking: a validator is chosen from a list by its account number, and no name is typed or shown. Not the sending of a payment that has waited (Home, "Waiting on your account"): it goes to the account number that was signed when the payment was made to wait, and the check is in the review of that earlier step.

**The names typed for guardians.** Guardians can replace every key of an account, so a name typed for one that reads like another may be someone else's account (More, Guardian recovery; `recovery.mjs`; `names.mjs`, `guardianChecks`). Each typed name is compared with the account's own name, with the names it has paid from this browser, and with the guardians typed before it in the same list. For each likeness the review of the policy shows one warning with both names, in the same font: "@d4na looks like @dana, your own name. Check every letter.", "@karol looks like @carol, which you have paid. Check every letter.", "@b0b looks like @bob, another guardian in this list. Check every letter." The review then has the box "I checked the guardian names, letter by letter.", and nothing is signed without the tick. A typed name that is itself one the account has paid is known, and is not compared with the other paid names. The guardians of a policy the account already has are not compared with: a new policy replaces the whole list.

**What it compares with.** The names this account has paid from this browser on this network, the account's own name, and nothing else. The list is kept in the browser beside the activity (`localStorage`, key `timecurrency.names.v1:<network id>:<account name>`): names only, nothing secret, and nothing is sent anywhere. A name enters it when this browser has verified the receipt of a payment to that name that was signed here; a payment that waits counts once the action that makes it wait has its receipt. The account a website was paid at is added when that payment is made; if its receipt is only checked later, from Activity, it is not. The payments that Activity kept from before this list existed fill it too (Activity keeps the newest 40 actions, and names no website's account). 500 names are kept; after that the one paid longest ago goes.

**What it cannot do.**

- It knows nothing about a name this account has never paid from this browser: a name you only heard of, a name you paid from another browser or another wallet, or before this browser's data was cleared. In a new browser every payment is a "First payment", and no look-alike is found.
- It does not know who has paid you. The wallet keeps no record of payments that came in, so a name is not compared with the names of senders.
- If your first payment went to the look-alike, the look-alike is the name this browser knows, and the warning then stands on the right name.
- It does not find every look-alike: not two changed letters, not one changed letter in a short name (`bob`, `rob`), not a name with a word added (`alice_shop` for `alice`), not a digit written for a letter together with a changed letter, and no pair of letters that look alike in some font and are not in the list above.
- The list is not locked as the key is. Whatever can change what this browser stores for the wallet, such as another program on the device or a browser extension, can put a name on it.
- It is a warning, not a block. With the box ticked the payment is signed, and from then on that name is one you have paid: it is not warned about again.
- "First payment" means the first that this list knows of.
- A list that cannot be read is an empty one, and one that cannot be stored is kept for as long as the page is open. No payment fails for it.

**A new name the network refuses.** A network may refuse to register a name that looks like a name it already has. Its refusal reaches the wallet through the gateway, and holds the words "too close to an existing name", or "too close to the existing name", followed by that name. The wallet takes the name only as where to look. When, by the rule above, it looks like the name you chose, and proven state holds an account of that name, the wallet says "That name is too close to @x, which already exists. Choose another." and shows the form for a new account again (`names.mjs`, `tooCloseName`; `app.js`, `nameRefusal`). Otherwise the refusal is shown as every gateway's words are. The encrypted vault that was made for the refused name stays in this browser; it opens no account.

**Not run in a browser.** The line, the warning, the box and the refused name ran on the stand-in page of the tests (`names.test.mjs`, `name-warning.test.mjs`; the own name and the guardians in `protection-card.test.mjs`), never in a browser and never against a network that refuses such names. A person must check in a browser: that the two names of the warning are drawn in a font in which `1` and `l`, `0` and `o` differ, each on one line, also on a phone; that the box stands beside its sentence and the button stays grey until it is ticked; that the review of a first payment and of a repeated one read well; and the same in the window a website opens.

## Recovery passphrase: made by the wallet, 72 bits, a 256 MiB calculation

A secured account has two recovery keys that are never stored: the printed recovery sheet and the recovery passphrase.

The wallet makes the passphrase; nobody chooses it. Its key's public half is on the chain, where anyone can test guesses offline, so a chosen phrase is too weak. It is 72 random bits written as four pronounceable words of six letters (for example `kerine netoji nesimo vebine`): each word is three syllables, each syllable one of 16 consonants (b d f g h j k l m n p r s t v z) followed by one of 4 vowels (a e i o), 6 bits per syllable. It is shown once, written down apart from the sheet, and typed back before it is used. Reading ignores case, spacing and dashes; a word that the wallet could not have made is named, and any other typo shows as "not one of this account's recovery keys". No word list is used: no permissively licensed list (such as BIP-39 English) was on this machine (the cargo registry has none), and nothing was downloaded.

The key is recomputed every time the passphrase is used (securing the account, freezing, paying above the limit, replacing a key, recovering a lost device) with Argon2id at 256 MiB of memory, 4 passes and 1 lane, from the passphrase and a salt naming the network and the account (`security-flows.mjs`, `PASSPHRASE_KDF`). The first papers of an account signed up protected are made before the account has a number, so their salt names the network and the account's identity key, the registration key, which the chain shows as `public_key` (`registrationPassphraseSalt`). On a network that takes such sign-ups, a passphrase that does not give a recovery key of the account by its number is tried by its identity key too: a mistyped passphrase there costs two calculations. New papers made later name the number, as always.

On a phone or browser with little memory the calculation can fail. The wallet then says so and stops. Finish the step on a computer, in a current desktop browser. There is no lighter version: other settings would give a different key, which the account does not know. The recovery sheet needs no such calculation, but the steps that need two recovery keys (recovering a lost device) need the passphrase too.

## Using a second browser

A secured account adds a browser as a login with its payment key. The new browser shows a request (its new login key, that key's acceptance of the role, and the account's authority epoch and an expiry six hours ahead, signed by the same key) and a fingerprint of the key. The device with the payment password checks both signatures, the epoch and the expiry, shows the same fingerprint, and adds the key. A request is refused once the account's keys have changed or after six hours, so a request saved in a chat cannot bring back a key the owner removed. The request waits in its own slot on the new browser: it blocks nothing, can be withdrawn, and is dropped, never promoted, once that browser holds a live key of the account.

## Validator operator

More, Validator operator (`pos-ui.mjs`) registers the account's validator from the registration text that the join script prints (`testnet/join.sh`), adds to its bond, takes the bond out and retires it. A bond taken out is listed there with "Withdraw" once its 14 days are over, as it is under Staking. An account with one key signs each of these with this browser's key, and its screen and its reviews are as they were.

**A protected account** signs each with its payment key, unlocked for that one action with the payment password typed on the screen ("Payment password", shown only for a protected account; `security-ui.mjs`, `stakeAction`). What the engine allows it, read on 2026-11-23 (`v5/src/engine.rs`, `apply`, the branch of kinds 5 to 7 and 17 to 24; `v5/src/security.rs`, `Security::spend`):

- Only a key that may spend signs these actions: the payment key. The login key and the recovery keys cannot ("spending device required").
- What an action takes out of the balance, fee included, is charged to the daily limit: the bond and the fee of a registration, the amount and the fee of an addition to the bond, the fee of taking the bond out and of retiring. The bond of a registration is the network's least bond (`pos/src/lib.rs`, `Profile::min_self_bond`): one second on a laboratory network such as the test network, a day of seconds (86,400) on a production one. A withdrawal brings the seconds back, and the engine charges it only what it brings back less than the fee.
- Each that is charged anything is refused while the account is frozen, and above what is left of the day's limit. None of it waits, and no recovery paper pays it: what waits is a payment (kind 75), and the operation the payment key signs with a recovery paper pays transfers only (`Operation::Pay`). The limit itself can be raised on Home: that waits the waiting period, or applies at once with a recovery paper, and either way what can be used fills up under the new limit over 24 hours.

**What the person is told** (`protection.mjs`, `validatorLimit`; read on the clock of the waits):

- Before the review, when it cannot be signed now, and nothing is unlocked or signed: "Your account is frozen, so registering a validator is refused until the freeze ends. See Home." (and "adding to a validator's bond", "taking a validator's bond out", "retiring a validator"); "The amount and the fee, 150.001 seconds, are more than the 100 seconds left of your daily limit today, and the network lets none of it wait or be paid with a recovery paper: try again after …, when enough of it has come back."; and, above the limit itself, "The bond and the fee, 86400.001 seconds, are more than your daily limit of 600 seconds, and the network lets none of it wait or be paid with a recovery paper: raise the limit on Home to at least 86401 seconds (it waits 48 hours, or applies at once with a recovery paper), then try again once it has filled up, within a day."
- In the review, two lines more: "Daily limit", "The bond and the fee, 1.001 seconds, count against your daily limit: 600 seconds is left today, 598.999 seconds after this." (or "The amount and the fee, …", "The fee, …, counts against …"; for a withdrawal "The seconds come back to your balance: nothing of your daily limit is used, unless less than the fee comes back."), and "Signed by", "Your payment key, with your payment password".
- Unbonding and withdrawing under Staking get the same two lines; their refusal while frozen is said as before.

**What the wallet asks beyond the engine.** A withdrawal is checked as Staking always checked it: its fee must fit what is left, and the account must not be frozen. The engine would take a withdrawal of a frozen account that brings back more than the fee; the wallet does not sign it until the freeze ends.

**What does not apply here.** No name is typed or shown: the registration text names the account by its number and the validator by its key, so the review compares no name with the names this account has paid (`names.mjs`). A registration text is taken only for this account and this network, as before.

**On a production network** a registration needs a daily limit above a day of seconds, and that much left of it: a protected account with the suggested 600 seconds is told to raise its limit first. On the test network the bond is one second.

**On a network, in Chrome only.** `validator-protected.test.mjs` runs the real page on a stand-in chain that applies the engine's rules as read above (its head says which lines), for a protected account and for an account with one key. On 23 November the browser check ("wallet-validator-protected") registered a validator from a protected account in Chrome 158, on laboratory networks with protected sign-up (campaigns `tc-v5-final8` and `tc-v5-final9`): the review said what the bond and the fee take of the daily limit, the bond was taken from the balance and from the limit and taken out again with the payment password, and a text made for another account was refused in words. The refusals while frozen and above what is left of the limit ran on the stand-in chain only.

## Validator signal

The owner account of a validator supports a change of the network's rules, or withdraws its support, from the screen "Validator signal" under More (`upgrade-signal-ui.mjs`, `upgrade-signal.mjs`; the signature is made in `signing.mjs`). The rules are in `../../docs/design/upgrade-mechanism.md`; the action is kind 80, built by the SDK (`../../client-v4/README.md`, "A validator's upgrade signal").

**When the screen is there.** Its row under More is shown only while proven state holds a validator candidate of the unlocked account that is not archived. Nothing a gateway says brings it up.

**What it shows.** What the screen says about the network, the plans and your validator is read with proofs the wallet verified itself. The screen shows nothing a gateway answered, neither as a sentence of its own nor as a quotation:

- the version in force, the scheduled plan and its start in UTC, and whether a validator safety hold is on;
- your validator's votes, its signal, when it was sent, and when you may change it again (one change every 10 minutes);
- whether your account is frozen or in a recovery: its signal then does not count, and it can neither signal nor withdraw;
- every plan that validators of the current validator sets support, with the hash in eight groups, its supporters, and its share of the votes against four fifths (to be scheduled) and three quarters (to stay scheduled). The share is the lowest over the validator set in force and the announced ones, as the network counts it, and is rounded down;
- every validator with its votes and its signal. A plan is named there by its whole hash, in groups: two hashes that begin and end alike are two plans.

**What one proof cannot hold.** Whether another validator's signal counts depends on its owner account (frozen, or in a recovery) and, during a safety hold, on which validators are jailed. A proof holds eight accounts and sixteen validators. The wallet reads the rest in further proofs, a few blocks after the first, and says from which blocks it read. Where a read failed, the count is one the network may not share, in either direction. A signal that does not count may have been counted: the share shown is then too high. During a safety hold a jailed validator that does not support the plan may still be in the total: the share shown is then too low. The screen then states no threshold as reached or missed. It says what proven state itself says (scheduled or not), the rule, and that it could not check the count. Every sentence that gives that share carries the same caveat: the list of plans, the line under the form, the review and the result. With a hundred validators the status is more than a dozen requests, so it is not read at every refresh of the wallet. It is read when the screen appears, when it is opened, on "Read again from proven state", and whenever the account's action number has moved since the last reading. Before anything is signed it is read again.

**Supporting a plan.** You type or paste the version, the specification hash and the start time from the release announcement of the software your validator runs.

- The wallet fills in none of the three, ever: not from the scheduled plan, not from what other validators signalled, not from the gateway, not from an address or from storage. The fields are emptied once a signal has been signed and when the wallet locks.
- The start time is the number the announcement gives: microseconds of network time. The wallet shows it as a date in UTC before you sign. Compare both with the announcement.
- The wallet compares what you typed with proven state and says which it is: the scheduled plan, a plan that validators support (and with how much of the votes), or a plan nobody has signalled. If a proven plan agrees with yours in version and start time and differs in the hash, the review shows a warning with that plan's hash beside yours. A plan with another start time or another version is another plan and brings no warning: whoever signals could otherwise put a warning into every owner's review.
- You tick that you checked your validator's node: it must run a release that implements this version with this hash, or it stops at the start. The node's own status lists what its program implements, each version with its hash (`upgrade.implemented`). On the validator's host it is the answer to the query `/status` of the node's local RPC (`http://127.0.0.1:26657/abci_query?path="/status"`, whose `response.value` is that status in base64); `node deploy/operator.mjs probe SETTINGS` makes the same query and prints the versions without their hashes. This sentence is from reading the kit's code; the query was not run against a live node here.

**Why you type the hash, and there is no button "support this plan".** The hash decides which rules the network will run. A signal in proven state proves that some validator sent it, not that its plan is the right one: any owner of a validator can signal any plan, and a button would put that plan one click away for every other owner. Comparing 64 characters by eye fails in the middle of the string. So the source is the release announcement, which the gateway does not control, and the comparison is the wallet's, over all 64 characters. Proven hashes are shown in groups of eight and the form takes a hash only as one run of 64 characters, so a hash copied from the proven lists of this page is refused, with the reason. An owner who removes the spaces by hand can still paste it: the form stops a careless copy, not a deliberate one. A gateway cannot put a hash on this screen: the line under the form takes only refusals the wallet states itself, and where the gateway refused a request the screen says one fixed sentence in place of the gateway's answer.

**Before you sign,** the review states: the version; the hash; the start in UTC and as the number you typed; that every program that does not implement the version with exactly these rules stops at the start; that your own node must run a release that implements it; your votes; what the plan has now; any warning; when you can change the signal again; which key signs; the fee. The review of this screen has a box to tick ("I compared the version, all eight groups of the hash and the start date with the release announcement"). The button that signs is disabled until it is ticked, and a review confirmed without the tick counts as cancelled. The withdrawal and the discard have a box of their own. While one step of the screen runs, a second one does not start: a key held down on the form opens one review.

**Which key signs.** A key of the account that may spend. For an account with one key that is this browser's key. For a secured account it is the payment key, unlocked with the payment password for this one action; the login key and the recovery keys cannot signal. The fee counts against the daily limit.

**Afterwards** the wallet waits for a receipt that it verifies itself, then reads proven state of that block and shows your validator's entry and the plan's share from it. If proven state does not show what was signed, it says so.

**Withdrawing** is a button of its own. Its review names the plan and says what the withdrawal does to a scheduled plan.

**A signal you did not send.** Only a signed action of the account creates or changes its signal. The wallet remembers, in this browser, what proven state last showed for the account and which signals were signed or discarded here (`localStorage`, key `timecurrency.signal.v1:<network id>:<account id>`; nothing secret). At every reading it compares.

- The first reading of an account in a browser is taken as it is: there is nothing to compare it with.
- A change the wallet signed itself, or still holds as pending, says nothing.
- Any other change is told at once in the notice, and a warning stands above the status for as long as proven state shows that entry. It names the plan with its whole hash and the date, and says what to do. If you sent it from another device or another wallet, nothing is wrong. If you did not, someone else holds a key that may spend: on a secured account freeze it on Home (the switch "Freeze" under Protection), then make a new payment password under Keys ("Change something", "New payment password"); on an account with plain keys remove every key you do not know under More, Devices. Then withdraw the signal, or signal again, once the network takes a change (one every 10 minutes; a frozen account can do neither).
- An entry that is gone was dropped by the network (its start came closer than the notice, its version is no longer the next one, or the validator was retired): said once, not as a warning.
- The wallet only sees this while it is open. The alert watch (`../../deploy/watch/README.md`) tells the owner of a watched account by its own channel.

**Refusals.** Before anything is signed the wallet applies the network's rules to proven state and says what to do:

- too early to change again: when you may;
- a start that is not far enough ahead (21 days, and 30 seconds for the node) or too far (120 days): the earliest or latest date as of now, and that the start is never chosen by one owner;
- a version that is not the next one: which one is;
- a frozen account, or one in a recovery: until when, and where to lift it;
- a plan your validator already supports, or a withdrawal with nothing to withdraw;
- less than the fee in the account, or, for a secured account, less than the fee left of the daily limit.

**A signed signal without a receipt.** A refusal from the gateway can mean that the node did not take the signal, or only that the gateway did not serve its receipt. The wallet does not go by the gateway's words. It reads proven state for a few blocks:

- if the account's action number has moved and proven state shows the signal, it says that the signal was included and that it holds no receipt for it;
- if the action number has moved and proven state shows something else, it says that another action of the account is in a block in the signal's place, and, if proven state shows a signal you discarded earlier, that it was that one;
- if nothing moved, it applies the rules to proven state again and names the rule that refuses the signal now, or says that the node did not take it (next paragraph);
- if no status could be read with a proof after signing, it says only that: it does not know whether the signal reached the network, and it claims nothing about proven state or about the node.

In each case the signed signal stays pending until Activity has checked or cleared it.

**A node that does not take the signal.** A node admits a signal of support only if its own program implements that version with that hash. Proven state does not show which program a node runs, so the wallet learns this only when the node refuses. It then says, in its own words, to check the hash against the announcement and to use a gateway in front of a node that runs the release. What the gateway answered is not shown.

- The signed signal stays pending, like every signed action. It stays valid until another action of the account is in a block. Signing another action does not end it: the gateway has the bytes of both, and whoever has them can have either one included.
- Send the same bytes again from Activity ("Retry the exact signed action") once the gateway's node runs the release.
- To end it for certain, use "Cancel pending action" in Activity, where this browser's key may manage the account: it puts another action of the account in a block. A secured account's login key cannot; there the signal ends only with the next action of the account that is in a block.
- Or discard it in the screen, to sign something else. The review of the discard says that discarding does not make the bytes invalid, and until when they stay valid. The wallet remembers what you discarded. If proven state later shows the discarded signal, the screen says that it was included after all, and to withdraw it if you do not want it.
- In release 1 no program implements a version 5, so every node refuses every signal of support this way. A withdrawal is not subject to that policy.

**Not verified.** These screens were run on a fake page with a mock chain (`upgrade-signal.test.mjs`, the reviewer's `review-signal.test.mjs`), and the real `app.js` was run on a stand-in page with a stand-in for the compiled verifier (`app.test.mjs`; which key may sign what is tested in `signing.test.mjs`), never in a browser, and no signal has been sent from them to a live network. The bytes of the action are compared with signals the engine accepted (`../../client-v4/upgrade-signal.test.mjs`). A person must check in a browser:

1. The screen appears for the owner account of a validator and for no other account, and switching to and from it works.
2. The checkbox stands beside its label, can be ticked, and is empty again after every attempt (`styles.css` has one new rule for it).
3. The review dialog with its twelve or more long lines can be read and scrolled on a phone, and the hash in groups wraps without losing a group.
4. Pasting a 64-character hash into the field keeps all of it; a phone keyboard does not capitalize or correct it.
5. The list of plans and the list of validators can be read with five validators and with a hundred.
6. On a secured account the payment password field is shown, is not filled by the browser, and is empty after the attempt.
7. The first reading when the screen appears, its failure text, and "Read again from proven state" while the wallet is busy.
8. A signal that the node refused: the text, the pending card, the discard, and "Retry the exact signed action" in Activity.
9. Locking and unlocking: the screen, the form and the result are empty again.
10. The review of a signal: when it opens, the focus is on the box and not on "Sign the signal"; "Sign the signal" is greyed until the box is ticked; pressing Enter with the box unticked does nothing; the box is empty again in the next review. Then hold Enter down in the form with all fields filled: one review opens and nothing is signed. The same for "Withdraw support" and "Discard" pressed with a held Enter. A review of another screen (a payment) has no box and works as before.
11. A signal sent with another key of the account while the wallet is open: within a few seconds the notice shows the warning and it stands above the status. Close and open the wallet: the warning is still there; after your own withdrawal it is gone.
12. A signal discarded and then included by whoever had its bytes: the screen says so after the next reading.
13. A gateway that refuses a request on another screen: the notice begins "The gateway refused this request. Its answer follows; this wallet did not check it." and a long number or hash in it is in groups of four.
14. On a network whose nodes run a rehearsal build with a version 5: a signal of support from the screen is included, the plan is scheduled at four fifths, and a withdrawal is included.

## A test network that was reset

A test network can be replaced by a new chain under the same addresses (`testnet/README.md`, "Resetting a network"). The wallet keeps all it stores under the network's id: the vaults of its accounts, their activity, the names they paid, the checkpoint of its light client. After a reset that stays in the browser under the old id, and opens nothing (`reset.mjs`).

- **The start screen** of a browser that keeps accounts of another network and none of the one the site serves now says, above the form: "The test network was reset. Your old account @alice and its test seconds no longer exist. Create a new account. This browser still keeps the old keys; they open nothing now." One button, "Remove the old account from this browser", opens a review ("Remove the old test network from this browser": the accounts, how many entries go, that everything of the network that runs now stays, that it cannot be undone) and then removes every entry of the wallet kept under another network's id, and nothing else: no entry of the network the site serves, and nothing that is not the wallet's. "Removed. This browser keeps nothing of the test network before its reset."
- **"Open my account"** with a name of the old network says "The test network was reset: @alice and its test seconds no longer exist. Create a new account.", and with any other name "The test network was reset: the accounts this browser kept are of the old one, and no longer exist. Create a new account." Not "No encrypted vault …, Choose New device", which stays for a browser that keeps no account of an old network.
- **A page that is open** during the reset fails every verification. After a failure (the reading every five seconds, a button, opening an account) it reads the site's trust file again, at most once every half minute; when that names another network, the notice says "The test network was reset. Reload this page." in place of the failure, and nothing can be signed. A failure while the trust file names the same network is said as before ("Verification paused. …").
- What this does not know: a reset is read from the trust file of the site, which the site serves with the page itself. A site that serves another network's trust file serves another page too; nothing here adds to what it could do. The old keys are removed only when the person presses the button and confirms.

The explorer says the same in place of a failed check (`web-v4/explorer/check.mjs`, `network_reset`), and the demo blog keeps a payment it could not get confirmed with the network it was made on, so that a new account with the number of an old one never settles a payment of the chain that is gone (`web-v4/merchant/app.js`).

**Not run in a browser.** `network-reset.test.mjs` runs the real page on a stand-in site whose network is replaced; `shop-network.test.mjs` the blog's page, and `web-v4/explorer/network-reset.test.mjs` the explorer's check.

## Known limits

- Deriving a recovery key's public key from its seed exports the key once as JWK inside WebCrypto, so the private scalar briefly exists as a JavaScript string (`d`) that cannot be zeroed; the wallet drops the reference at once. WebCrypto offers no way to get an Ed25519 public key from a private key without an export.
- The daily limit is a bucket that refills over 24 hours: the full limit can leave at once and, as it refills, almost as much again within the same 24 hours. The screens say so.
- On a network whose trust data does not say that its nodes report upgrades, a gateway can keep a plan from the wallet, and the wallet then says that it could not check.
  - The wallet learns of a scheduled change of the network's rules from proven state. Whether the network's nodes answer that question comes from the trust data installed with the wallet (`nodes_report_upgrades: true` in `/trust-manifest.json`; a production network's says so, see `tools/genesis-v5.mjs`). It is never taken from a gateway.
  - *A network that says so:* every verified read carries the question from the first one. Nothing is probed and nothing is remembered. A gateway that holds or refuses the question fails the read itself, and the wallet shows "Verification paused".
  - *A network that does not say so* (an older laboratory network): the wallet asks in a read of its own, every minute, until a node has answered. While that read fails, for whatever reason, one line stands above the account. It is not a warning: "This app could not check whether an upgrade of the network is scheduled: the answer could not be verified. This network does not state that its nodes report upgrades, so they may not be able to. This app keeps asking." The line stays until the question is answered, and it is the same whatever the gateway said: the wallet reads no error text to choose what it shows.
  - What remains is this: on such a network a gateway can hold the question for as long as it likes, and the owner sees that line and no plan.
- On such a network, once a node has answered, the question is on every read, and that is remembered.
  - The wallet keeps that fact per network in the browser (`localStorage`, key `timecurrency.upgrade-entry.v1:<network id>`). A gateway can then no longer hide a plan by holding or refusing the one question: the whole read fails and the wallet shows "Verification paused".
  - The same happens if the network's nodes really stop answering the question, for example when a gateway is moved behind an older node.
  - The way out is on the page: open "Offline trust and checkpoint refresh" at the bottom and press "Forget the answer about upgrades". The button is shown only while such an answer is remembered. The wallet then asks in a read of its own again. The vault, the key and the checkpoint stay as they are.
  - Forgetting gives a gateway back the room described in the limit above. Do it only when the network's nodes were really replaced.
