Staking

Put some seconds to work.

Staked now: —

On this network all new seconds, up to 86,400 a day, go to the validators who sign blocks, by voting power, and through them to the people who stake with them. Stake with a validator below and your part of its reward is added to your stake as it arrives; the validator keeps a commission of at most 20% of it. While the network is starting up, most of what its starting validators earn is staked for them, so few seconds circulate yet: your first seconds come from someone who already has some.Your first seconds come from someone who already has some: on a test network, from the faucet.

Staked seconds cannot be sent. Rewards change the value of your share of the validator's pool. Taking seconds back out takes 14 days, during which they can still be cut if the validator misbehaves.

For developers

The wallet checks short proofs for the accounts and validators it looks at. It does not recount every account of the network. This is a test network: real use needs HTTPS, reviewed code and secure hosting.

Validator operator

A validator is a computer you run that signs blocks. Its key stays on that machine. Here your account registers it, adds to its bond or takes the bond out. The explorer's Validators page says how to set one up.

Your account is protected, so its payment key registers the validator, adds to its bond, takes the bond out, retires it and withdraws, each with this password, for that one action. The bond and each fee count against your daily limit, and cannot wait or be paid with a recovery paper.

Offline trust and checkpoint refresh

A node of this network once answered this wallet's question about scheduled upgrades, so the wallet now asks it with every read. If every read fails since the network's node was replaced by an older one, the wallet can forget that answer and ask on its own again. Your key and your checkpoint stay.

After seven days offline, signing stays off until you bring in a current checkpoint of the network that you checked yourself. A checkpoint file alone cannot give trust: confirm the whole block hash through a channel of the network's operator that you trust.
